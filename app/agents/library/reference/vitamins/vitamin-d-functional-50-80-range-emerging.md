Some functional, integrative and longevity practices aim for a 25-hydroxyvitamin D (vitamin D level, 25-OH-D) of roughly 50 to 80 ng/mL. This target is Emerging and is not supported by current guidelines.

- The Endocrine Society's 2024 guideline suggests against routine 25(OH)D testing and against using a target level to guide supplementation in healthy adults, because trials have not shown that any specific level delivers outcome benefits.
- The National Academies' cut-offs, summarized by the NIH Office of Dietary Supplements, treat 20 ng/mL or higher as adequate for most people and link levels above 50 ng/mL to potential adverse effects, particularly above 60 ng/mL.

So a 50 to 80 ng/mL goal sits above the NASEM adequacy level and overlaps the range NASEM flags for possible harm. A result in the 20s or 30s ng/mL is adequate by NASEM standards even if a functional range labels it suboptimal. A result above 50 ng/mL, especially with ongoing high-amount supplementation, merits clinician review. Decisions about supplementing, and how much, belong with the member's clinician.
