The NIH Office of Dietary Supplements vitamin D fact sheet identifies groups more likely to have inadequate vitamin D status (low vitamin D, vitamin D deficiency, low 25-OH-D):

- Breastfed infants, because human milk alone usually does not meet infant vitamin D needs.
- Older adults.
- People with limited sun exposure.
- People with dark skin, because more skin pigment reduces vitamin D made from sunlight.
- People with conditions that limit fat absorption, since vitamin D is fat-soluble and needs dietary fat to be absorbed.
- People with obesity and those who have had gastric bypass surgery.

The ODS notes that getting enough vitamin D from unfortified foods alone is difficult, so fortified foods and some sun exposure matter for most people. Being in a risk group does not by itself mean testing is needed: the Endocrine Society's 2024 guideline suggests against routine 25(OH)D screening even in adults with dark complexion or obesity.
