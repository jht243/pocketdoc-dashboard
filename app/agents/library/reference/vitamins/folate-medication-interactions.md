The NIH Office of Dietary Supplements folate fact sheet lists medications that interact with folate (folic acid, vitamin B9):

- Methotrexate (Rheumatrex, Trexall) is a folate antagonist used for cancer and autoimmune conditions. People taking it for cancer should consult their oncologist before using folate supplements, which could interfere with its anticancer effect. In contrast, for low-amount methotrexate in rheumatoid arthritis or psoriasis, folate might reduce gastrointestinal side effects.
- Antiepileptic (antiseizure) medications such as phenytoin, carbamazepine and valproate can lower serum folate, and folate supplements might in turn lower blood levels of these medications.
- Sulfasalazine (Azulfidine), used mainly for ulcerative colitis, inhibits intestinal folate absorption and can cause folate deficiency.

The ODS also notes that alcohol interferes with folate absorption and liver uptake and increases its excretion. People taking these medications regularly are advised to discuss folate intake with their clinician, who decides whether supplementation is appropriate and in what amount; the NIH sets a tolerable upper intake level for folic acid.
