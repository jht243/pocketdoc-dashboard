title: Groups the Endocrine Society says may benefit from vitamin D
source: Endocrine Society 2024 Clinical Practice Guideline, Vitamin D for the Prevention of Disease

The Endocrine Society's 2024 guideline Vitamin D for the Prevention of Disease suggests empiric vitamin D, meaning without first testing a blood level, for four groups:

- Children and adolescents ages 1 to 18, to prevent nutritional rickets and possibly lower respiratory tract infections.
- Adults 75 and older, because of a potential to lower mortality.
- Pregnancy, because of a potential to lower risk of preeclampsia, preterm birth, small-for-gestational-age birth and fetal and neonatal death.
- Adults with high-risk prediabetes, in addition to lifestyle change, to reduce progression to type 2 diabetes. High-risk prediabetes in the trials meant meeting two or three ADA prediabetes criteria or having impaired glucose tolerance.

For healthy adults under 50 and those 50 to 74, the guideline suggests against vitamin D beyond the intake already recommended by the National Academies (IOM) dietary reference intakes. Empiric vitamin D can come from fortified foods, multivitamins or a supplement, and the guideline prefers daily over intermittent high-amount regimens in adults 50 and older. All of these are conditional (grade 2) suggestions. The amount for any individual is set by their clinician; the NIH sets a tolerable upper intake level.
