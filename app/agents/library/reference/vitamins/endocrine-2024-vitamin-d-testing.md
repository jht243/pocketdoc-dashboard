The Endocrine Society's 2024 guideline Vitamin D for the Prevention of Disease, which replaced its 2011 vitamin D deficiency guideline, suggests against routine 25-hydroxyvitamin D testing (25-OH-D, 25(OH)D, vitamin D blood test, vitamin D level) in generally healthy people. The suggestion covers:

- Healthy adults under 50, adults 50 to 74, and adults 75 and older.
- Pregnancy.
- Adults with dark complexion and adults with obesity, two groups often assumed to need testing.

The panel's reason is that clinical trials have not established 25(OH)D levels that deliver outcome-specific benefits, so a level cannot reliably guide whether or how much to supplement. It also suggests against routine follow-up testing to adjust supplementation. These are conditional recommendations (grade 2) based on low-certainty evidence.

The guideline applies only to people without an established reason for testing. Testing remains appropriate when a clinician has an established indication, such as low blood calcium, and the guideline does not cover people who already have an established indication for vitamin D treatment or testing. The 2024 guideline does not define 25(OH)D thresholds for sufficiency, insufficiency or deficiency.
