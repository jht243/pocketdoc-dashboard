The NIH Office of Dietary Supplements vitamin B12 fact sheet names two common medication classes that can lower B12 (cobalamin, vitamin B12 level):

- Metformin, the first-line medication for prediabetes and type 2 diabetes, might reduce B12 absorption and significantly lower serum B12 concentrations. The ADA Standards of Care have recommended periodic B12 monitoring in people on long-term metformin since 2017, particularly when peripheral neuropathy or anemia is present.
- Gastric acid inhibitors, including proton pump inhibitors (PPIs such as omeprazole and lansoprazole) and H2 blockers (such as cimetidine), reduce stomach acid. Acid is needed to release B12 from food protein, so long-term use can interfere with absorption and lead to deficiency.

The ODS advises that people taking these medications regularly discuss their B12 status with their clinician. These facts are reasons to check B12, not reasons to change the medication; any change to metformin or an acid reducer is a decision for the prescriber.
