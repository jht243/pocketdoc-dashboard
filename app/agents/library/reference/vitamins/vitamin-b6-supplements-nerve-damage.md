Vitamin B6 (pyridoxine, pyridoxal 5-phosphate, P5P, PLP) is water-soluble, but long-term high intake from supplements can damage nerves. The NIH Office of Dietary Supplements fact sheet states that high B6 intake from food has not been reported to cause harm, while chronic high-amount pyridoxine supplementation can cause severe, progressive sensory neuropathy with loss of coordination (ataxia). Symptoms usually stop if supplements are stopped as soon as nerve symptoms appear. The NIH sets a tolerable upper intake level for B6 that counts food and supplements together.

Australia's Therapeutic Goods Administration (TGA) found that neuropathy can also occur at lower intakes than previously thought, and when people take several products that each contain B6:

- Symptoms are tingling, burning or numbness, usually in the hands or feet; delayed diagnosis and continued exposure can let it progress.
- No minimum amount, duration of use or patient risk factor has been identified; risk varies between people.
- Of 32 well-documented Australian reports, 69% had elevated blood B6 levels, and in many cases people did not know they were taking B6 because the product was sold as a magnesium supplement.
- The TGA advises clinicians to consider B6 toxicity in anyone with peripheral neuropathy and to review all sources, especially multivitamins, B-complex, magnesium and zinc products taken together.

Tingling or numbness while taking supplements is a reason to list every product for a clinician, who may check a plasma B6 (PLP) level.
