The NIH Office of Dietary Supplements vitamin B12 fact sheet identifies groups more likely to have low B12 (cobalamin deficiency, low vitamin B12):

- Older adults. Depending on the definition, about 3% to 43% of community-dwelling older adults have B12 deficiency, often linked to atrophic gastritis, which reduces stomach acid and intrinsic factor. Atrophic gastritis affects about 2% of the general population but 8% to 9% of adults 65 and older. Helicobacter pylori infection is another contributor.
- People with pernicious anemia, an autoimmune disease that destroys the stomach cells making intrinsic factor, the protein needed to absorb B12.
- People with stomach or small-intestine disorders such as celiac disease, and those who have had gastrointestinal surgery, such as weight-loss surgery or removal of part of the stomach.
- People following vegetarian or vegan diets, since vitamin B12 comes mainly from animal foods, and infants breastfed by mothers who follow vegan diets.
- People taking metformin or long-term acid-suppressing medications.

The ODS notes symptoms can take several years to appear and include fatigue, neurological changes, megaloblastic anemia, a sore or inflamed tongue, palpitations, and low red and white blood cell counts.
