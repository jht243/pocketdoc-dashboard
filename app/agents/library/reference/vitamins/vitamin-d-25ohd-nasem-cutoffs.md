Serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D (25-OH-D, 25(OH)D, vitamin D level) is the standard blood marker of vitamin D status. The NIH Office of Dietary Supplements summarizes the cut-offs set by the Food and Nutrition Board of the National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine (NASEM, formerly IOM):

- Below 12 ng/mL (30 nmol/L): associated with vitamin D deficiency, which can cause rickets in children and osteomalacia in adults.
- 12 to under 20 ng/mL (30 to under 50 nmol/L): generally considered inadequate for bone and overall health in healthy people.
- 20 ng/mL (50 nmol/L) or higher: generally considered adequate for most people.
- Above 50 ng/mL (125 nmol/L): linked to potential adverse effects, particularly above 60 ng/mL (150 nmol/L).

To convert, 1 ng/mL equals 2.5 nmol/L. The ODS notes that optimal 25(OH)D levels for bone and general health have not been established and likely vary by life stage, race and ethnicity, and the outcome measured. It also notes the Endocrine Society's 2024 guideline does not define sufficiency or deficiency cut-offs and does not recommend routine testing in healthy people.
