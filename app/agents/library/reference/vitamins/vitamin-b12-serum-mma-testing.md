The NIH Office of Dietary Supplements vitamin B12 fact sheet describes how B12 status (cobalamin, vitamin B12 level, B12 deficiency) is assessed:

- Serum or plasma B12 is the usual first test. Cut-offs vary by lab and method, but most labs define subnormal values as below 200 or 250 pg/mL.
- Methylmalonic acid (MMA) is the most sensitive marker of B12 status. An MMA above 0.271 µmol/L suggests B12 deficiency. Experts suggest checking MMA when serum B12 falls in the gray zone of 150 to 399 pg/mL, to confirm or rule out deficiency.
- Homocysteine rises quickly as B12 declines, and a level above 15 µmol/L suggests deficiency, but it is not specific because low folate and reduced kidney function also raise it.

MMA has its own caveats: it rises with kidney insufficiency and tends to run higher in older adults. B12 deficiency can cause megaloblastic anemia (large red cells, high MCV) and neurological changes, and symptoms can take years to appear. A low or borderline B12 result is a reason for clinician review rather than a diagnosis on its own.
