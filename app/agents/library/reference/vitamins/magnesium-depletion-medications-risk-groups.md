The NIH Office of Dietary Supplements magnesium fact sheet identifies medications and conditions that can lower magnesium (low magnesium, hypomagnesemia):

- Proton pump inhibitors (PPIs such as esomeprazole and lansoprazole) can cause hypomagnesemia when taken for prolonged periods, typically more than a year. The FDA advises clinicians to consider measuring serum magnesium before starting long-term PPI treatment and periodically afterward.
- Loop diuretics (furosemide, bumetanide) and thiazide diuretics (hydrochlorothiazide) increase urinary magnesium loss; potassium-sparing diuretics such as spironolactone reduce it.
- Type 2 diabetes and insulin resistance, through increased urinary losses driven by high glucose in the kidney.
- Gastrointestinal disease with chronic diarrhea or fat malabsorption, such as Crohn's disease and celiac disease, and small-bowel resection or bypass.
- Alcohol dependence and older age.

Magnesium also binds certain drugs: magnesium-containing products reduce absorption of oral bisphosphonates such as alendronate and of tetracycline and quinolone antibiotics, so timing is separated by the prescriber or pharmacist.
