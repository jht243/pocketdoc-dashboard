---
title: Beta-carotene supplements raised lung cancer risk in smokers
source: NIH ODS Vitamin A and Carotenoids Fact Sheet summarizing the ATBC and CARET trials; USPSTF 2022 Vitamin, Mineral, and Multivitamin Supplementation recommendation
evidence: Strong
approved_by: pending
as_of: 2022-06
url: https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/vitamin-supplementation-to-prevent-cvd-and-cancer-preventive-medication
---

Two large randomized trials found that beta-carotene supplements (provitamin A, antioxidant supplements) increased lung cancer in people at high risk, as summarized by the NIH Office of Dietary Supplements:

- ATBC (Alpha-Tocopherol, Beta-Carotene Cancer Prevention Study): 29,133 male smokers ages 50 to 69 took beta-carotene, vitamin E, both, or placebo for 5 to 8 years. Beta-carotene raised lung cancer risk by 18% and overall death by 8%, mainly from lung cancer and heart disease.
- CARET (Carotene and Retinol Efficacy Trial): 18,314 current and former smokers and asbestos-exposed workers took beta-carotene plus retinyl palmitate or placebo. The trial was stopped early after the supplements increased lung cancer by 28%, lung cancer death by 46% and all-cause death by 17%.

The National Academies' Food and Nutrition Board advises against beta-carotene supplements for the general population except as a vitamin A source to prevent deficiency. The US Preventive Services Task Force (USPSTF) in June 2022 recommended against beta-carotene supplements, and against vitamin E supplements, for preventing cardiovascular disease or cancer in community-dwelling, nonpregnant adults (grade D).

Beta-carotene from fruits and vegetables is not the concern; the harm signal comes from concentrated supplements, especially in current and former smokers. Smokers who take a multivitamin or eye-health formula may want a clinician to review whether it contains beta-carotene.
