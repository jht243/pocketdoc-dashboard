The Physical Activity Guidelines for Americans, 2nd edition (HHS, 2018) say older adults should follow the same aerobic and muscle-strengthening guidelines as other adults where they are able, and as part of their weekly activity should do multicomponent physical activity that includes balance training alongside aerobic and muscle-strengthening work. Examples include tai chi, yoga, dancing, and exercises that challenge standing balance.

The World Health Organization 2020 guidelines are more specific for adults aged 65 and older: varied multicomponent activity that emphasizes functional balance and strength training at moderate or greater intensity on 3 or more days a week, to improve function and prevent falls (strong recommendation).

For older adults with diabetes, the ADA Standards of Care in Diabetes, 2026 (recommendation 5.38) advise flexibility and balance training 2 to 3 times a week.

Both HHS and WHO stress that older adults who cannot meet the full guidelines because of chronic conditions should be as active as their abilities allow, and that the activity level should match their fitness.

Members ask about this as fall prevention, balance exercises, staying steady, exercise for seniors, mobility, or tai chi.
