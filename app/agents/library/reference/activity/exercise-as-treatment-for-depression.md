A network meta-analysis by Noetel and colleagues (The BMJ, 2024) pooled 218 randomized trials with 14,170 participants who met clinical cut-offs for major depression. Compared with active control conditions such as usual care or a placebo pill, several kinds of exercise produced moderate reductions in depression symptoms, and the authors judged walking or jogging, yoga and strength training the most effective:
- Walking or jogging.
- Yoga.
- Strength training.
- Mixed aerobic exercise.
- Tai chi or qigong.

Benefits grew with the intensity prescribed, so more vigorous sessions tended to help more. Yoga and strength training were the best tolerated, with fewer people dropping out. The authors concluded that exercise could be considered alongside psychotherapy and antidepressants as a core treatment for depression.

The caveat is study quality: only one trial met Cochrane criteria for low risk of bias, and confidence in the results was rated low for walking or jogging and very low for the other types. That is why this passage is graded Moderate.

The HHS Physical Activity Guidelines, 2nd edition (2018) similarly state that regular activity lowers the risk of developing depression, improves symptoms in people with depression, and reduces anxiety symptoms over weeks to months.

Exercise complements, rather than replaces, care from a clinician. Thoughts of self-harm need urgent help: call or text 988, the Suicide and Crisis Lifeline, in the US. Members ask about exercise for mood, depression, anxiety, stress, or yoga and mental health.
