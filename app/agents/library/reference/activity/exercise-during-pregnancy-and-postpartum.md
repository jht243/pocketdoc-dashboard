ACOG Committee Opinion No. 804 (2020) states that, without obstetric or medical complications or contraindications, physical activity in pregnancy is safe and desirable, and women should be encouraged to continue or start safe activities. Women who were active before pregnancy, including those used to vigorous exercise, can generally keep going during pregnancy and after birth. Observational studies link exercise in pregnancy with less gestational diabetes, fewer cesarean and assisted deliveries, and faster postpartum recovery, and activity may help prevent postpartum depression.

The HHS Physical Activity Guidelines, 2nd edition (2018) and ACOG both set a goal of at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic activity a week during pregnancy and the postpartum period, spread through the week. Moderate means able to talk but not sing. Women new to exercise can start with a few minutes a day and build up.

ACOG's patient guidance lists activities to avoid, including contact sports, activities with a high risk of falling, scuba diving, skydiving, hot yoga or hot Pilates, and exercise above about 6,000 feet for those not used to altitude. Warning signs to stop and call the ob-gyn include vaginal bleeding, dizziness or feeling faint, shortness of breath before starting, chest pain, headache, muscle weakness, calf pain or swelling, regular painful contractions, and fluid leaking from the vagina.

Pregnant women should check with their obstetric clinician about their own plan. Members ask about working out while pregnant, prenatal exercise, lifting weights in pregnancy, or getting back to exercise after the baby.
