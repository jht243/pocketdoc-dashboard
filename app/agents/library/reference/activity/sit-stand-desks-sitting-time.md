title: Sit-stand desks cut sitting time but health benefits are unproven
source: Shrestha et al. 2018 Cochrane Review, Workplace interventions for reducing sitting at work
evidence: Emerging
approved_by: pending

A Cochrane review by Shrestha and colleagues (2018) looked at 34 studies with 3,397 office workers in high-income countries to see what reduces sitting at work.

- Sit-stand desks, alone or with information and counseling, cut sitting at work by about 100 minutes per workday over the first three months compared with ordinary desks, though the evidence quality was low.
- Over 3 to 12 months the reduction was smaller, about 57 minutes a day, in two studies.
- Total daily sitting and long unbroken sitting bouts also fell.
- Fixed standing desks and sit-stand desks performed similarly. Treadmill and cycling desks had unclear or inconsistent effects.
- No studies tested standing or walking meetings.

The review measured sitting time, not long-term health outcomes, so it cannot say whether standing desks lower the risk of heart disease, diabetes or death. That is why this passage is graded Emerging.

Current guidance supports the general idea. The WHO 2020 guidelines recommend limiting sedentary time and replacing it with activity of any intensity, and the ADA Standards of Care in Diabetes, 2026 advise interrupting prolonged sitting at least every 30 minutes.

Members ask about standing desks, sit-stand desks, sitting all day at work, desk jobs, under-desk treadmills, or standing versus walking breaks.
