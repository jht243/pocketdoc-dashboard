A meta-analysis led by Paluch and colleagues (The Lancet Public Health, 2022) pooled data from 15 cohorts including 47,471 adults followed for a median of about 7 years. Taking more steps per day was associated with a progressively lower risk of death from any cause, up to a point that depended on age.

- Compared with the lowest quarter of step counts (median about 3,550 steps a day), the higher quarters had roughly 40 to 53% lower mortality risk.
- For adults aged 60 and older, the risk curve leveled off at about 6,000 to 8,000 steps a day.
- For adults younger than 60, it leveled off at about 8,000 to 10,000 steps a day.
- After accounting for total steps, walking pace was less clearly linked to mortality.

The authors note that the popular 10,000-steps target had little prior evidence behind it. These are observational associations, not proof that adding steps causes the benefit, so this passage is graded Moderate. US physical activity guidelines are written in minutes of activity, not steps.

Members ask about this as steps per day, step goal, 10,000 steps, walking more, or step count on a phone or watch. Wearable step counts are estimates and vary between devices.
