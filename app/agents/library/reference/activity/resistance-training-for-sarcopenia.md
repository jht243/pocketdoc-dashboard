Sarcopenia is the age-related loss of skeletal muscle mass, strength and function. It raises the risk of falls, frailty and loss of independence.

The International Conference on Frailty and Sarcopenia Research (ICFSR) task force published clinical practice guidelines for sarcopenia in 2018, led by Dent and colleagues and graded with the GRADE method. For treatment, the panel strongly recommended resistance-based physical activity, meaning progressive strength training against weights, bands or body weight. It made only a conditional (weaker) recommendation for protein supplementation or a protein-rich diet, and it made no recommendation for vitamin D or anabolic hormones because the evidence was insufficient.

This fits the broader guidelines: the HHS Physical Activity Guidelines and WHO 2020 recommend muscle strengthening on 2 or more days a week for all adults, and the ADA Standards of Care in Diabetes, 2026 note that muscle-strengthening exercise may help preserve lean mass during weight loss with obesity medicines or metabolic surgery.

Members ask about this as muscle loss, losing strength with age, sarcopenia, frailty, weak legs, grip strength, or keeping muscle while losing weight. Assessing sarcopenia itself is a clinician's job.
