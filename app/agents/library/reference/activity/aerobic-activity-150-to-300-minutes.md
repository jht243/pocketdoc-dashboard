The Physical Activity Guidelines for Americans, 2nd edition (HHS, 2018) recommend that adults get 150 to 300 minutes a week of moderate-intensity aerobic activity, or 75 to 150 minutes a week of vigorous-intensity aerobic activity, or an equivalent combination, preferably spread through the week. Going beyond the equivalent of 300 minutes of moderate activity a week brings additional health benefits. The 2nd edition is still the current US edition; a 3rd edition is expected around 2028.

The World Health Organization 2020 guidelines give the same ranges for adults aged 18 to 64 (strong recommendation) and add that going beyond them may bring extra benefit.

Moderate intensity means breathing harder but still able to talk, such as brisk walking, easy cycling or fast dancing. Vigorous intensity means only a few words at a time, such as running, swimming laps or hard cycling. Roughly, one minute of vigorous activity counts about the same as two minutes of moderate.

The 2018 guidelines dropped the old rule that activity had to come in bouts of at least 10 minutes; short bouts count toward the total.

Members ask about this as cardio, exercise minutes, how much exercise, zone 2, active minutes, walking, running or weekly activity goals on a wearable.
