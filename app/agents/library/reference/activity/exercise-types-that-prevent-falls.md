At least one in three community-dwelling adults over 65 falls each year. A Cochrane review by Sherrington and colleagues (2019) analyzed 108 randomized trials with 23,407 participants aged 60 and older, average age 76, living in the community.

Overall, exercise programs reduced the rate of falls by 23% and the number of people who fell by 15%, both high-certainty evidence. Benefits were similar whether or not participants were chosen for higher fall risk. Exercise may also reduce fall-related fractures and falls needing medical attention, though that evidence is low certainty.

The type of exercise mattered:
- Balance and functional exercises, which train balance, walking and everyday movements, reduced the fall rate by 24% (high certainty).
- Programs combining several types, most often balance and functional work plus resistance training, probably reduced the fall rate by 34% (moderate certainty).
- Tai chi may reduce the fall rate by 19% and reduced the number of people falling by 20%.
- For programs that were mainly resistance training, dance or walking, the effect on falls was uncertain.

Adverse events were mostly minor muscle and joint complaints. This fits the HHS and WHO guidance that older adults include balance training in multicomponent activity. Members ask about fall prevention, balance exercises, tai chi, steadiness, or exercise for seniors. A recent fall or new unsteadiness belongs with a clinician.
