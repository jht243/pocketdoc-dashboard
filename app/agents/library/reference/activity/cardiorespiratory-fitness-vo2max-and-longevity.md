Cardiorespiratory fitness is how well the heart, lungs and muscles supply and use oxygen during sustained exercise. VO2 max is its classic lab measure; treadmill tests estimate it in METs, and many wearables offer their own VO2 max estimate.

Mandsager and colleagues (JAMA Network Open, 2018) followed 122,007 adults who had a treadmill exercise test at Cleveland Clinic between 1991 and 2014, for a median of 8.4 years. Fitness was ranked against age- and sex-matched norms from low (below the 25th percentile) to elite (97.7th percentile and above).

Higher fitness was associated with lower all-cause mortality at every step, with no upper limit of benefit observed. Elite performers had about 80% lower adjusted risk of death than low performers. The mortality risk linked to low fitness was comparable to or greater than that linked to coronary artery disease, smoking or diabetes. The authors call fitness a modifiable indicator of long-term risk.

This is an observational study of people referred for testing, with fitness measured once, so it shows association rather than cause and is graded Moderate. Wearable VO2 max numbers are estimates, best followed as a trend.
