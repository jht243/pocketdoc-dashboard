The Bone Health and Osteoporosis Foundation 2022 Clinician's Guide (LeBoff and colleagues) advises counseling or referring patients for balance training and muscle-strengthening exercise to help prevent fractures, describes weight-bearing exercise such as walking and stair climbing as part of bone health, and recommends a multicomponent program combining resistance, balance and weight-bearing work for people with osteoporosis. It notes that structured exercise has produced modest increases in bone density in research settings, while studies with fractures as the main outcome are few.

The HHS Physical Activity Guidelines, 2nd edition (2018) state that the usual decline in bone density with aging can be slowed by aerobic, muscle-strengthening and bone-strengthening activity of moderate or vigorous intensity.

The LIFTMOR randomized trial (Watson and colleagues, Journal of Bone and Mineral Research, 2018) tested supervised high-intensity resistance and impact training twice a week for 8 months in 101 postmenopausal women with low bone mass (osteopenia or osteoporosis). Compared with a home low-intensity program, it improved lumbar spine and femoral neck bone density, height and physical function, with one minor adverse event. Training was closely supervised and participants were screened, so results may not carry over to unsupervised heavy lifting.

Evidence for bone density is moderate and fracture evidence is limited, so this passage is graded Moderate. People with osteoporosis or past fractures should get exercise guidance from a clinician or physical therapist. Members ask about osteoporosis, osteopenia, DEXA scores, lifting weights for bones, or jumping and impact exercise.
