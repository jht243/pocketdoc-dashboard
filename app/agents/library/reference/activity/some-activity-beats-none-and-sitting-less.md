The World Health Organization 2020 guidelines state that some physical activity is better than none, and that people not yet meeting the recommendations should start small and gradually increase frequency, intensity and duration. The HHS Physical Activity Guidelines, 2nd edition (2018) make the same point with the message "move more and sit less", and note that any amount of activity has some health benefit.

On sitting, WHO recommends that adults limit sedentary time and replace it with physical activity of any intensity, including light activity (strong recommendation). Adults with a lot of sitting time are encouraged to go beyond the standard activity targets to offset it. WHO found the evidence was not strong enough to set a specific maximum sitting time. HHS links more sedentary behavior with higher risk of heart disease, high blood pressure and death from all causes.

The ADA Standards of Care in Diabetes, 2026 (recommendation 5.34) advise that prolonged sitting be interrupted at least every 30 minutes for blood sugar and other benefits.

Members ask about this as a desk job, sitting all day, sedentary, getting started with exercise, being too busy to work out, standing desks, or inactivity alerts on a watch.
