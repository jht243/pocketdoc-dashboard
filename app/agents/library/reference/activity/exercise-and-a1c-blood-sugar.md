---
title: Exercise lowers A1C and improves blood sugar control
source: ADA Standards of Care in Diabetes 2026, Section 5 Physical Activity
evidence: Strong
approved_by: pending
as_of: 2026-01
url: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12690188/
---

The ADA Standards of Care in Diabetes, 2026 recommend that most adults with type 1 or type 2 diabetes get 150 minutes or more of moderate- to vigorous-intensity aerobic activity a week, spread over at least 3 days, with no more than 2 days in a row without activity; 75 minutes a week of vigorous or interval training may be enough for fitter people (recommendation 5.36). They add 2 to 3 resistance sessions a week on nonconsecutive days (5.37) and interrupting prolonged sitting at least every 30 minutes (5.34).

The Standards cite a meta-analysis, mostly of aerobic training studies, in which structured exercise programs of at least 8 weeks lowered A1C (hemoglobin A1c) by 0.66 percentage points in type 2 diabetes, even without significant change in BMI. Combining aerobic and resistance exercise may give larger glucose benefits than either alone. Higher relative muscle mass is associated with better insulin sensitivity. For type 1 diabetes, the A1C evidence is less clear.

The Standards also note that exercise can raise insulin sensitivity for hours afterward, so people using insulin or insulin secretagogues can have low blood sugar after exercise, while very intense exercise can briefly raise glucose. Those medication questions belong with the prescriber.
