The Physical Activity Guidelines for Americans, 2nd edition (HHS, 2018) recommend that adults do muscle-strengthening activities of moderate or greater intensity that work all major muscle groups on 2 or more days a week, in addition to aerobic activity. The World Health Organization 2020 guidelines make the same recommendation (strong).

Muscle-strengthening activity includes lifting weights, resistance bands, body-weight exercises such as push-ups, squats and lunges, heavy gardening, and some forms of yoga. All major muscle groups means legs, hips, back, abdomen, chest, shoulders and arms.

For people with diabetes, the ADA Standards of Care in Diabetes, 2026 (recommendation 5.37) advise 2 to 3 sessions of resistance exercise a week on nonconsecutive days. The Standards note that resistance training can improve blood sugar, strength, muscle size, bone density and cardiometabolic health, and that higher relative muscle mass is associated with better insulin sensitivity. They also note that meeting activity guidelines, especially muscle strengthening, may help maintain lean mass for people losing weight with obesity medicines or metabolic surgery.

Members ask about this as strength training, weightlifting, resistance training, lifting, building muscle, toning, or keeping muscle on GLP-1 medicines.
