The 2003 AHA/CDC scientific statement on inflammatory markers (Pearson et al., Circulation) lists characteristics associated with higher or lower hs-CRP (high-sensitivity C-reactive protein), noting the list is likely incomplete.

Associated with higher hs-CRP:

- Elevated blood pressure and higher body mass index
- Cigarette smoking
- Metabolic syndrome or diabetes
- Low HDL and high triglycerides
- Estrogen plus progestogen hormone use
- Chronic infections such as gum disease (gingivitis) or bronchitis
- Chronic inflammatory conditions such as rheumatoid arthritis

Associated with lower hs-CRP:

- Moderate alcohol intake
- Higher physical activity and endurance exercise
- Weight loss
- Certain lipid medications, including statins, fibrates and niacin

The statement highlights body weight and metabolic syndrome as the most consistent links, and weight loss as associated with lower hs-CRP. These are associations, so they explain why a value may be high but do not prove that changing one factor will lower it by a set amount. The evidence label is Moderate because these are observational links summarized by the statement. Keywords: why is my CRP high, lower inflammation, CRP and weight, CRP and smoking.
