MTHFR (methylenetetrahydrofolate reductase) is an enzyme in folate and homocysteine metabolism. Common variants, especially C677T and A1298C, are found in a large share of the population. People who carry two copies of C677T can have somewhat higher homocysteine, particularly when folate intake is low.

The American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics (ACMG) 2013 practice guideline concluded that MTHFR polymorphism testing has minimal clinical utility. It advises that MTHFR genotyping should not be ordered as part of a routine evaluation for blood clots (thrombophilia) or recurrent pregnancy loss, and should not be ordered for at-risk relatives. The guideline cites meta-analyses that found no association between MTHFR status and venous blood clots, and none between raised homocysteine and coronary heart disease. It also notes that MTHFR status does not change standard folic acid advice for people who could become pregnant.

Where homocysteine itself is measured and found high, the usual next step is checking B12, folate and kidney function rather than genotype. Keywords: MTHFR mutation, MTHFR gene, C677T, methylation gene, MTHFR test.
