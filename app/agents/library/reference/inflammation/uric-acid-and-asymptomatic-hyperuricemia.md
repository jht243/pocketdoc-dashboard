Uric acid (serum urate, SU) is a waste product made when the body breaks down purines, which come from the body's own cells and from foods and drinks. The kidneys clear most of it. MedlinePlus notes that levels can rise with gout, kidney disease or kidney stones, some cancers and their treatment, heavy alcohol use and preeclampsia, and with some medicines such as diuretics, aspirin and niacin. Many people with a high level never have health problems from it.

The 2020 American College of Rheumatology (ACR) gout guideline defines asymptomatic hyperuricemia as a serum urate of about 6.8 mg/dL or higher in someone who has never had a gout flare or tophi (urate deposits under the skin). For these people the ACR conditionally recommends against starting urate-lowering medication. Its reasons: in trials only a small share developed gout either way, and in observational data only about 20% of people with urate above 9 mg/dL developed gout within 5 years, so the panel judged that benefits would not outweigh costs and risks for most.

For people with gout, the ACR conditionally recommends limiting alcohol, purine-rich foods and high-fructose corn syrup, and weight loss for those with overweight, and conditionally recommends against adding vitamin C supplements. Evidence is labelled Moderate because these are conditional recommendations. Keywords: high uric acid, uric acid level, hyperuricemia, urate, purines, gout risk.
