The white blood cell count (WBC, white count, leukocytes) and the lymphocyte percent come from a complete blood count with differential. Both appear in the Levine 2018 PhenoAge biological age formula: a higher WBC pushes the estimate older and a higher lymphocyte percent pushes it younger.

The 2003 AHA/CDC scientific statement on inflammatory markers lists WBC count among markers considered as cardiovascular risk predictors and notes that older prospective studies linked higher WBC counts to more cardiovascular disease, with the link persisting after adjusting for other risk factors. The statement did not recommend WBC as a stand-alone risk test; hs-CRP was the marker it found most practical.

A WBC can rise for many short-term and long-term reasons, including infection, smoking, stress, steroid medicines and chronic inflammation, and it can fall with some infections and medications. Normal ranges are set by each lab. Because WBC and lymphocyte percent are not direct treatment targets, Thumbprint explains them in the biological age view rather than offering them as goals, and significant abnormalities go to a clinician. Keywords: white blood cells high, WBC, lymphocytes, white count, CBC inflammation.
