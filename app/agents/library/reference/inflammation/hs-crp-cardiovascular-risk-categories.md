High-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hs-CRP, high sensitivity CRP, cardiac CRP) is a blood marker of low-grade inflammation. The 2003 AHA/CDC scientific statement on inflammatory markers (Pearson et al., Circulation) set the relative cardiovascular risk categories still used on lab reports:

- Low risk: under 1.0 mg/L
- Average risk: 1.0 to 3.0 mg/L
- High risk: above 3.0 mg/L

These cut-points correspond roughly to thirds of the adult population. The statement recommends hs-CRP be reported in mg/L, measured twice, fasting or not, ideally about 2 weeks apart, with the average used, and only when a person is metabolically stable and free of infection or acute illness. A single result is less reliable because CRP varies more from day to day than cholesterol does.

The statement considered hs-CRP most useful as an added tool for people at intermediate risk, where it can help decide how intensively to manage other risk factors. It is a risk marker, not a diagnosis, and a high result does not identify where inflammation is coming from.
