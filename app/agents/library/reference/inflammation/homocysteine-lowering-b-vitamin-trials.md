Higher homocysteine is associated with heart disease and stroke in observational studies, which led to trials testing whether lowering it with B vitamins (folic acid, vitamin B6 and vitamin B12) prevents cardiovascular events.

The 2017 Cochrane systematic review (Marti-Carvajal et al.) pooled 15 randomized trials with 71,422 participants. Compared with placebo, B-vitamin regimens that lowered homocysteine:

- did not reduce heart attacks (myocardial infarction), with a risk ratio of about 1.02;
- did not reduce death from any cause, with a risk ratio of about 1.01;
- showed a small reduction in stroke, with a risk ratio of about 0.90.

The authors rated the evidence for all three outcomes as high quality and judged further placebo-controlled trials unlikely to change the conclusion. Large trials such as HOPE-2 and NORVIT also found no cardiovascular benefit. This means homocysteine is better treated as a marker than as a proven treatment target for heart protection. Treating a confirmed B12 or folate deficiency is a separate question, decided by a clinician on its own merits.
