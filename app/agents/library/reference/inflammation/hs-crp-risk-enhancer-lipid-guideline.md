In the 2026 ACC/AHA multisociety dyslipidemia guideline, hs-CRP (high-sensitivity C-reactive protein) is used selectively to refine cardiovascular risk when the standard PREVENT estimate leaves a decision uncertain, typically at borderline 10-year risk of 3% to under 5%.

Clinical summaries of the guideline list an hs-CRP of 2 mg/L or higher, found on more than one measurement and after excluding other causes of inflammation, as a risk-enhancing factor. Risk enhancers favor starting or intensifying cholesterol-lowering therapy alongside lifestyle changes. The 2 mg/L enhancer threshold is lower than the over 3 mg/L high-risk category from the 2003 AHA/CDC statement; the two serve different purposes and should not be mixed.

Other enhancers in the same list include premature family history of heart disease, Lp(a) of 125 nmol/L or higher, chronic inflammatory diseases such as rheumatoid arthritis, psoriasis or lupus, pregnancy complications, early menopause, and cardiovascular-kidney-metabolic syndrome. A single elevated value during an illness does not count. Exact wording should be checked against the full guideline.
