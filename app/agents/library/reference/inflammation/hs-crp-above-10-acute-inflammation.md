The 2003 AHA/CDC scientific statement on inflammatory markers (Pearson et al., Circulation) notes that more than 95% of adults have hs-CRP values below 10 mg/L. A result above 10 mg/L usually reflects an acute process such as an infection, injury, flare of an inflammatory condition, or recent surgery, rather than baseline cardiovascular risk.

The statement advises that an hs-CRP above 10 mg/L should not be used for cardiovascular risk classification. Instead, clinicians look for an obvious source of infection or inflammation, set that result aside, and repeat the test after about 2 weeks once the acute problem has settled. People with active infection, systemic inflammation or trauma should not be tested for cardiovascular risk until those conditions have resolved.

A persistently high hs-CRP on repeat testing without an obvious cause is something for a clinician to evaluate, since it can reflect chronic inflammatory, infectious or other conditions. Thumbprint separately does not calculate the PhenoAge biological age score when hs-CRP is above 10 mg/L; that is a Thumbprint app rule built on the same reasoning.
