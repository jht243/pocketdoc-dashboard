Colchicine is a long-used gout medicine that also dampens inflammation. In June 2023 the FDA approved Lodoco, a low-dose colchicine tablet, to reduce the risk of heart attack, stroke, coronary revascularization and cardiovascular death in adults with established atherosclerotic disease or multiple cardiovascular risk factors, and it was announced as the first anti-inflammatory drug approved for cardiovascular disease.

The approval rested mainly on LoDoCo2 (Nidorf et al., NEJM 2020): in 5,522 people with chronic coronary disease followed for a median of about 2.4 years, colchicine lowered the combined rate of cardiovascular death, heart attack, ischemic stroke or ischemia-driven coronary procedures by about 31% (hazard ratio 0.69). Non-cardiovascular deaths were numerically higher with colchicine.

A later trial was neutral. In CLEAR SYNERGY (Jolly et al., NEJM 2025), 7,062 people who started colchicine soon after a heart attack had the same event rate as placebo over about 3 years (hazard ratio 0.99), despite lower CRP. Diarrhea was more common.

The FDA label lists contraindications including use with strong CYP3A4 or P-glycoprotein inhibitors, kidney failure, severe liver impairment and existing blood disorders, and warns of muscle toxicity, with higher risk alongside statins or fibrates. Evidence is Moderate because trial results are mixed. Keywords: colchicine heart, Lodoco, anti-inflammatory for heart disease, inflammation and heart attack.
