Gout is a painful arthritis caused by urate crystals forming in and around joints when uric acid stays high. The 2020 American College of Rheumatology (ACR) gout guideline, built with GRADE methods, made 42 recommendations, 16 of them strong.

Strong recommendations include:

- Urate-lowering therapy (ULT) for everyone with tophi (urate deposits), joint damage from gout on imaging, or frequent gout flares.
- A treat-to-target approach: the ULT dose is adjusted using repeated serum urate (SU) blood tests, aiming to reach and keep SU below 6 mg/dL.
- Allopurinol as the preferred first-line ULT, including for people with moderate to severe chronic kidney disease.
- Anti-inflammatory preventive treatment for at least 3 to 6 months when ULT is started, because flares are common early on.
- Colchicine, NSAIDs or glucocorticoids for treating a gout flare.

After a first flare, the ACR conditionally recommends against starting ULT unless the person also has chronic kidney disease stage 3 or worse, a serum urate above 9 mg/dL, or kidney stones. The guideline does not set a lower target than 6 mg/dL for more severe disease, noting a lack of trial data. Medication choices and amounts are decided by the treating clinician.
