An abdominal aortic aneurysm (AAA) is a bulge in the main artery in the belly that can rupture. The US Preventive Services Task Force (USPSTF) 2019 recommendation:

- Men 65 to 75 who have ever smoked: one-time screening with abdominal ultrasound (grade B).
- Men 65 to 75 who have never smoked: clinicians may selectively offer screening based on health history and preference (grade C).
- Women 65 to 75 who have ever smoked or have a family history of AAA: evidence is insufficient to decide (I statement).
- Women who have never smoked and have no family history of AAA: routine screening is not recommended (grade D).

Ever smoked is commonly defined as having smoked 100 or more cigarettes in a lifetime. Abdominal duplex ultrasound is the standard screening test; the Task Force does not recommend CT or physical exam for screening. The categories follow sex assigned at birth because risk estimates are driven by biologic sex. Keywords: aortic aneurysm screening, AAA ultrasound, belly aorta scan, aneurysm smokers.
