The US Preventive Services Task Force (USPSTF) 2020 recommendation (grade B) is to screen for hepatitis C virus (HCV) infection in adults aged 18 to 79, including pregnant people, who have no symptoms and no known liver disease.

For most adults this is a one-time test. People with ongoing risk, such as those who inject drugs, should be tested periodically, although the best interval is not established. Clinicians may also consider screening people younger than 18 or older than 79 who are at high risk.

Screening starts with an anti-HCV antibody blood test. A positive antibody result is followed by a PCR test for HCV RNA, which shows whether the infection is current; a positive antibody alone can reflect a past infection that has cleared. The 2020 update replaced the earlier approach that focused on people born from 1945 to 1965 and on those with specific risk factors.
