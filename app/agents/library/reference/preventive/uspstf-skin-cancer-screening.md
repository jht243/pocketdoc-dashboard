The US Preventive Services Task Force (USPSTF) April 2023 statement found the evidence insufficient to weigh the benefits and harms of a visual skin examination by a clinician to screen for skin cancer, including melanoma, in adolescents and adults without symptoms (I statement). An I statement is neither a recommendation for nor against screening; it means the research does not yet settle the question, and clinicians use judgment.

The statement does not apply to:

- people with a personal or family history of skin cancer
- people with symptoms, such as a new, changing or irregular mole or skin growth
- people already under surveillance for high risk, such as those with a familial syndrome

The Task Force also found insufficient evidence on counseling adults to do skin self-examinations. A new or changing spot on the skin is a reason to see a clinician regardless of screening policy. Evidence is labelled Moderate because the statement is an I statement rather than a graded recommendation. Keywords: skin check, mole check, dermatologist screening, melanoma screening, full body skin exam.
