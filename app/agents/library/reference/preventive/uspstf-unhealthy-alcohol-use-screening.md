The US Preventive Services Task Force (USPSTF) 2018 recommendation (grade B) is to screen adults 18 and older, including pregnant people, for unhealthy alcohol use in primary care and to offer brief behavioral counseling to those who drink at risky or hazardous levels. For adolescents 12 to 17 the evidence was insufficient (I statement).

Recommended brief screening tools:

- AUDIT-C, a 3-question version of the Alcohol Use Disorders Identification Test
- The Single Alcohol Screening Question (SASQ) from NIAAA: how many times in the past year a person had 5 or more drinks in a day (men) or 4 or more (women and adults over 65)

The statement describes risky drinking using NIAAA limits: more than 4 drinks a day or 14 a week for men aged 21 to 64, and more than 3 a day or 7 a week for women and for men 65 and older. Unhealthy alcohol use covers everything from risky drinking to alcohol use disorder. A positive brief screen is often followed by the full 10-question AUDIT. The USPSTF lists this topic as an update in progress. Keywords: alcohol screening, how much drinking is too much, AUDIT-C, binge drinking, heavy drinking.
