The US Preventive Services Task Force (USPSTF) 2021 recommendation (grade B) is to screen for prediabetes and type 2 diabetes in adults aged 35 to 70 who have overweight or obesity and no symptoms. Overweight means a BMI of 25 or higher and obesity a BMI of 30 or higher; for Asian American adults, a BMI cut-point of 23 or higher may be used.

Screening tests are fasting plasma glucose, hemoglobin A1c, or an oral glucose tolerance test. A diabetes result should be confirmed with repeat testing. The best rescreening interval is uncertain; the Task Force suggests every 3 years may be reasonable after normal results.

Clinicians should consider screening at a younger age, or at a lower weight, for people with a family history of diabetes, a history of gestational diabetes or polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS), and for American Indian or Alaska Native, Asian American, Black, Hispanic or Latino, and Native Hawaiian or Pacific Islander adults. The glucose and A1c cut-offs that define prediabetes and diabetes come from the ADA Standards of Care, not from the USPSTF.
