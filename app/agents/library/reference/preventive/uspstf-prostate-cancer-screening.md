The US Preventive Services Task Force (USPSTF) 2018 recommendation on prostate cancer screening with the PSA (prostate-specific antigen) blood test:

- Men 55 to 69: the decision to be screened should be an individual one, made after discussing the potential benefits and harms with a clinician (grade C).
- Men 70 and older: the Task Force recommends against PSA-based screening (grade D).

The Task Force judged the benefits and harms closely balanced for men 55 to 69. Possible benefit is a small reduction in the chance of dying from prostate cancer. Possible harms include false-positive results, biopsies and their complications, and overdiagnosis and overtreatment, with treatment side effects such as erectile dysfunction and urinary incontinence. Factors that often enter the discussion include family history and Black race, both associated with higher prostate cancer risk.

Evidence is labelled Moderate here because the guideline itself grades the 55 to 69 recommendation C, an individual decision. The USPSTF lists this topic as an update in progress, so the 2018 statement stands until replaced. Keywords: PSA test, prostate screening, should I get a PSA, prostate cancer blood test.
