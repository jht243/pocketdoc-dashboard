The US Preventive Services Task Force (USPSTF) April 2022 recommendation on daily low-dose aspirin to prevent a first heart attack or stroke (primary prevention) in adults without known cardiovascular disease:

- Ages 40 to 59 with a 10-year cardiovascular risk of 10% or higher: starting aspirin is an individual decision made with a clinician (grade C). The net benefit is small, and people not at increased bleeding risk who are willing to take it daily are more likely to benefit.
- Age 60 and older: the Task Force recommends against starting aspirin for primary prevention (grade D), because bleeding risk offsets the benefit.

Aspirin raises the risk of stomach and intestinal bleeding and bleeding in the brain, and that harm grows with age. Other bleeding risk factors named in the statement include male sex, diabetes, a history of stomach ulcers or GI bleeding, liver disease, smoking, high blood pressure, and use of NSAIDs, steroids or blood thinners. The statement does not apply to people with known cardiovascular disease, and it found the evidence inadequate that aspirin lowers colorectal cancer risk. Risk was estimated with the older Pooled Cohort Equations, not PREVENT. Evidence is labelled Moderate because the 40 to 59 recommendation is grade C.
