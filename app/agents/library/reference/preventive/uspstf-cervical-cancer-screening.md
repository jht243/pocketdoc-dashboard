The US Preventive Services Task Force (USPSTF) final recommendation on cervical cancer screening (2018, all grade A for screening) for people with a cervix:

- Ages 21 to 29: cervical cytology (Pap test) alone every 3 years. HPV testing is not recommended in this age group because infections are common and usually clear.
- Ages 30 to 65: any of three options: Pap test alone every 3 years, high-risk HPV (hrHPV) test alone every 5 years, or co-testing with Pap plus hrHPV every 5 years.
- Under 21: do not screen (grade D).
- Over 65 with adequate prior negative screening and not otherwise at high risk: stop screening (grade D).
- After hysterectomy with removal of the cervix and no history of high-grade precancer or cervical cancer: do not screen (grade D).

The recommendation does not apply to people with a history of high-grade precancer or cervical cancer, in utero DES exposure, or a weakened immune system such as HIV. The USPSTF lists an update in progress; a December 2024 draft added patient-collected HPV testing for ages 30 to 65, but the 2018 statement remains the final version until replaced.
