Harmful changes in the BRCA1 and BRCA2 genes raise the risk of breast, ovarian, fallopian tube and peritoneal cancer. The US Preventive Services Task Force (USPSTF) 2019 recommendation for women:

- Women with a personal or family history of breast, ovarian, tubal or peritoneal cancer, or an ancestry linked to BRCA1/2 variants such as Ashkenazi Jewish ancestry: clinicians should assess risk with a brief familial risk tool, and if positive, offer genetic counseling and then genetic testing if indicated (grade B).
- Women without such history or ancestry: routine risk assessment, counseling or BRCA testing is not recommended (grade D).

Family history features the tools look for include breast cancer before age 50, cancer in both breasts, breast and ovarian cancer in the same person, breast cancer in a male relative, several relatives with breast cancer, and relatives with two BRCA-related cancers. Named tools include the Ontario Family History Assessment Tool, Manchester Scoring System, Pedigree Assessment Tool and 7-Question Family History Screening Tool. General breast cancer risk calculators such as the Gail model are not meant for BRCA risk. The USPSTF lists this topic as an update in progress. Keywords: BRCA test, breast cancer gene, hereditary cancer, genetic testing for breast cancer, Ashkenazi.
