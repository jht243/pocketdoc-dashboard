The US Preventive Services Task Force (USPSTF) April 2024 recommendation on breast cancer screening:

- Women and others assigned female at birth aged 40 to 74: biennial (every 2 years) screening mammography (grade B). This moved the routine starting age from 50 down to 40.
- Age 75 and older: evidence is insufficient to weigh benefits and harms (I statement).
- Dense breasts: evidence is insufficient to assess supplemental screening with breast ultrasound or MRI after a negative mammogram (I statement); this is neither for nor against.

The Task Force found that every-2-year screening had a more favorable balance of benefits and harms than yearly screening. The recommendation applies to people at average risk and to those with factors such as a family history or dense breasts. It does not apply to people with a high-risk genetic variant such as BRCA1 or BRCA2, prior chest radiation at a young age, or a personal history of breast cancer or high-risk breast lesions, who follow separate plans. Keywords: mammogram age, how often mammogram, breast cancer screening, dense breast tissue.
