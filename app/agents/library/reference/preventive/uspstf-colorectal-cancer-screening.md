The US Preventive Services Task Force (USPSTF) 2021 recommendation on colorectal cancer (colon cancer, bowel cancer) screening for average-risk adults without symptoms:

- Ages 45 to 49: screen (grade B). This lowered the starting age from 50.
- Ages 50 to 75: screen (grade A).
- Ages 76 to 85: clinicians selectively offer screening based on overall health, prior screening and preference (grade C).

Accepted test options and intervals in the recommendation:

- Stool test, high-sensitivity guaiac (gFOBT) or FIT: every year
- Stool DNA-FIT (for example multitarget stool DNA tests): every 1 to 3 years
- CT colonography: every 5 years
- Flexible sigmoidoscopy: every 5 years, or every 10 years with a yearly FIT
- Colonoscopy: every 10 years

A positive stool test or CT colonography needs a follow-up colonoscopy to complete screening. The recommendation does not cover people with a personal history of polyps or colorectal cancer, inflammatory bowel disease, or strong family or genetic risk; they follow separate surveillance schedules.
