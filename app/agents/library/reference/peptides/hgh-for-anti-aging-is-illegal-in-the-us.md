Human growth hormone (HGH, hGH, somatropin; brand names include Genotropin, Humatrope, Norditropin and Omnitrope) is an FDA-approved prescription drug, but only for specific medical conditions.

What the law and guidelines say:

- Federal law, 21 U.S.C. 333(e), makes it a crime to knowingly distribute growth hormone, or possess it with intent to distribute, for any human use other than treating a disease or recognized medical condition for which FDA has approved it, on a physician's order. Penalties reach up to 5 years in prison, or 10 years if a minor is involved. The DEA investigates these offenses.
- The 2019 American Association of Clinical Endocrinologists (AACE) growth hormone guideline states that off-label distribution or marketing of growth hormone to treat aging or age-related conditions, or to enhance athletic performance, is illegal in the US, and that it should never be prescribed for sports or anti-aging (Grade A). It notes no studies have assessed long-term efficacy or safety for anti-aging, and a meta-analysis in healthy older adults found small body composition changes with significant rates of side effects.
- The DEA's 2026 fact sheet lists accepted adult uses as growth hormone deficiency and AIDS-related wasting, and describes anti-aging clinics and internet pharmacies as common illicit sources.

Growth hormone is also banned in sport. Agents explain status only; testing for and treating growth hormone deficiency belongs to an endocrinologist.
