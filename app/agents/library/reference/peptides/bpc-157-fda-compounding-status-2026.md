BPC-157 (body protection compound 157, a synthetic peptide) is not an FDA-approved drug. Its compounding status has changed several times:

- September 2023: FDA placed BPC-157 in Category 2, citing possible immunogenicity (immune reactions) for some routes, complexities with peptide-related impurities and ingredient characterization, and little or no human safety information for the proposed routes.
- April 15, 2026: FDA announced it would remove 12 peptides, including BPC-157, from Category 2 after seven days because the nominators withdrew their nominations. FDA's page now lists BPC-157 among withdrawn nominations. Legal commentators noted removal does not place it on the 503A bulks list or in Category 1.
- July 23-24, 2026: FDA's Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee voted 8-6, with one abstention, to recommend adding BPC-157 to the 503A bulks list, over FDA staff's recommendation against it. The committee also backed KPV, TB-500, MOTS-c, Semax and Epitalon, and rejected emideltide (DSIP).

Advisory votes are not binding. As of October 2026, no final FDA action adding BPC-157 to the bulks list was found; formal rulemaking was expected to extend into 2027. Status should be described as under review. Agents educate on status only; prescribing decisions belong to a licensed clinician.
