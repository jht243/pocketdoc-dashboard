Compounded GLP-1 medicines, such as compounded semaglutide or tirzepatide sold through telehealth and med-spas, are a common peptide question.

FDA's stated concerns:

- Compounded drugs are not FDA-approved. FDA does not review them for safety, effectiveness or quality before marketing, and says they may be appropriate only when a patient's medical need cannot be met by an approved drug.
- FDA has received adverse event reports, some requiring hospitalization, linked to dosing errors with compounded injectable semaglutide. Patients measured the wrong amount, and some health care professionals miscalculated.
- Semaglutide sodium and semaglutide acetate are different active ingredients from the approved product. FDA says it is not aware of any lawful basis for compounding with these salt forms.
- Counterfeit Ozempic has been found, with wrong, too much, too little or no active ingredient.
- Products labeled "research use only" or "not for human consumption" have unknown quality.

FDA warning letters have also cited sellers for implying compounded products are the same as brand-name approved drugs. Questions about switching between compounded and approved products, or about amounts, belong with the prescribing clinician and pharmacist.
