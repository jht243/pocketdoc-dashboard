BPC-157 is promoted online for tendon, ligament and muscle healing, gut healing, joint pain and injury recovery. Most of that support comes from animal and laboratory studies.

What official reviews say:

- In its Category 2 evaluation, FDA stated it had identified no, or only limited, safety-related information for the proposed routes of administration, and therefore lacked enough information to know whether BPC-157 would cause harm in humans. It also flagged possible immune reactions and impurity concerns.
- At the July 2026 FDA advisory committee review, FDA staff recommended against listing BPC-157, and a committee member who voted no cited the absence of randomized controlled trials. The committee's narrow vote in favor concerned whether pharmacies may compound it, not proof that it works.

Any claimed benefit of BPC-157 in people should therefore be described as Emerging: plausible from animal research, but not established by human clinical trials. The same applies to most other unapproved "healing" or "longevity" peptides such as TB-500, MOTS-c and Epitalon.

Agents can describe the evidence and regulatory status. Whether trying any peptide is appropriate, and in what form, is a decision for a licensed clinician.
