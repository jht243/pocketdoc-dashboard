Many peptides are sold online as "research use only", "for research purposes" or "not for human consumption", often alongside syringes and bacteriostatic water.

FDA's position:

- FDA has warned companies selling unapproved semaglutide, tirzepatide, retatrutide and similar compounds labeled for research or not for human consumption while marketing them to consumers with dosing instructions. FDA urges consumers not to buy these products because their quality is unknown and they may be harmful.
- In a February 2025 warning letter to an online peptide seller, FDA set aside "research use only" and "not for human consumption" disclaimers because the site's own marketing showed the products were meant for people. It cited the products as unapproved new drugs and misbranded, including sale of prescription drugs without a prescription.

Quality concerns with these products include unknown identity and strength (too much, too little or none of the stated ingredient), impurities, and no assurance of sterility for injectable products. FDA also warns that counterfeit versions of approved drugs exist.

A "research use only" label is not a legal shield and is not a quality guarantee. Agents can explain these risks; any decision about using a peptide belongs with a licensed clinician.
