For athletes subject to drug testing, the World Anti-Doping Agency (WADA) Prohibited List governs Olympic and many professional and collegiate sports. The 2026 list took effect January 1, 2026, and its rules apply at all times, in and out of competition.

- Section S0, Non-Approved Substances, bans any drug not covered elsewhere on the list that has no current government approval for human therapeutic use. The 2026 list names BPC-157 as an example.
- Section S2, Peptide Hormones, Growth Factors, Related Substances and Mimetics, names growth hormone-releasing hormone analogues (examples include CJC-1295, sermorelin and tesamorelin), growth hormone secretagogues (examples include ipamorelin and ibutamoren, MK-677), growth hormone-releasing peptides (GHRP-2, GHRP-6 and others), and thymosin beta-4 and its derivatives, such as TB-500.

Being FDA-approved does not make a substance allowed in sport: tesamorelin is an approved drug but is still listed under S2. Athletes with a genuine medical need can apply for a Therapeutic Use Exemption.

Athletes are strictly liable for what is in their bodies, so mislabeled or contaminated "research" products can cause a positive test. The list is updated every January; athletes should check the current list or their sport's anti-doping body. This is education on rules; medical use of any peptide is a decision for a licensed clinician.
