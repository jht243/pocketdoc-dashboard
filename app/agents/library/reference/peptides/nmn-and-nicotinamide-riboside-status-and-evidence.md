NMN (nicotinamide mononucleotide) and NR (nicotinamide riboside, sold as Niagen and Tru Niagen) are forms of vitamin B3 marketed as NAD+ boosters for aging, energy and longevity. Both are sold as dietary supplements, which FDA does not review for effectiveness before sale.

Regulatory status of NMN:
- FDA had earlier taken the position that NMN was excluded from the dietary supplement definition because it had been authorized for investigation as a drug.
- In letters dated September 29, 2025, responding to a citizen petition, FDA reversed that position and determined NMN may be lawfully marketed as a dietary ingredient.
- In February 2026, Niagen Bioscience, which sells NR, sued FDA in federal court challenging that reversal. As of its mid-2026 filing, the case was pending, so NMN's status could still change.

What the human evidence shows:
- A 2023 Science Advances review of 25 human NR trials found that oral NR reliably raises NAD+ in whole blood but had shown few clinically relevant effects; it found no indication NR raises muscle NAD+. Many trials were small, short or lacked a placebo group. NR appeared safe in the doses studied.
- Benefits for lifespan, healthy aging, metabolism or muscle in people have not been established, so any such claim is Emerging.

Agents describe status and evidence only; whether to use a supplement is a decision for the member and their clinician.
