Many peptides people ask about (BPC-157, TB-500, ipamorelin, CJC-1295 and others) are not FDA-approved drugs, so the only lawful US route to a prescription version is usually a compounding pharmacy. Whether a pharmacy may compound with a raw ingredient (a bulk drug substance) depends on FDA rules under section 503A of the Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act.

Per FDA, a 503A compounder may use a bulk substance only if it (1) has a USP or NF monograph, (2) is a component of an FDA-approved drug, or (3) appears on FDA's 503A bulks list. It must also come from an FDA-registered supplier with a valid certificate of analysis.

While FDA evaluates nominated substances, it sorts them into interim categories:

- Category 1: nominated with enough information and may be eligible for the list. FDA does not intend to take action against compounding with these if policy conditions are met.
- Category 2: FDA has identified significant safety risks. Not covered by the Category 1 policy.
- Category 3: nominated with insufficient information. Not covered by the Category 1 policy.

Leaving Category 2 does not by itself make a substance compoundable. Agents can explain regulatory status; whether any peptide is appropriate, and prescribing it, rests with a licensed clinician.
