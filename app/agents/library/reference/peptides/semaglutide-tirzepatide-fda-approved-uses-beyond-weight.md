Semaglutide (Wegovy) and tirzepatide (Zepbound) are FDA-approved peptide medicines. Beyond chronic weight management, their current labels include these uses, each in combination with a reduced-calorie diet and more physical activity:

Wegovy, semaglutide (label revised March 2026):
- To reduce the risk of major adverse cardiovascular events (cardiovascular death, nonfatal heart attack or nonfatal stroke) in adults with established cardiovascular disease and either obesity or overweight. FDA approved this use in March 2024, based on the SELECT trial.
- To treat noncirrhotic MASH (metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis, formerly NASH, fatty liver inflammation) with moderate to advanced liver fibrosis, stages F2 to F3, in adults. Added in August 2025 under accelerated approval, which means continued approval may depend on a confirmatory trial.
- Wegovy tablets, an oral form added in December 2025, carry the weight and cardiovascular indications.

Zepbound, tirzepatide (label revised February 2026):
- To treat moderate to severe obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) in adults with obesity, approved in December 2024.

Both labels advise against combining the drug with other semaglutide- or tirzepatide-containing products or any other GLP-1 receptor agonist.

Compounded versions are not FDA-approved and were not tested for these uses. Side effects and label warnings are covered in separate passages. Whether one of these medicines is appropriate is a prescriber decision.
