The 2026 ACC/AHA multisociety dyslipidemia guideline is the first US cholesterol guideline to cover high triglycerides (hypertriglyceridemia, TG, blood fats) as a formal topic. Key levels it uses:

- Triglycerides of 150 mg/dL or higher are associated with increased ASCVD risk. In this range the guideline keeps LDL-C and non-HDL-C as the main targets; secondary causes and lifestyle (diet, activity, alcohol, weight) are addressed first, and statins stay central when indicated.
- Fasting triglycerides of 500 mg/dL or higher: after LDL-C is at goal, guideline summaries describe clinicians considering added triglyceride-lowering therapy.
- Triglycerides of 1,000 mg/dL or higher: the guideline emphasizes triglyceride-lowering treatment to prevent pancreatitis, plus referral to a registered dietitian.

For most people with triglycerides below 500 mg/dL, the main concern is heart and blood vessel risk rather than pancreatitis. Common secondary causes of high triglycerides include uncontrolled diabetes, alcohol, weight gain, kidney or thyroid problems, and some medications; these are checked before triglycerides are treated on their own. Keywords: triglycerides high, TG, blood fats, fatty blood, pancreatitis risk.
