Specialty consensus statements add more detail on Lp(a), also written Lpa or lipoprotein little a, than the ACC/AHA guideline does. These are expert consensus documents, so their bands carry Moderate rather than guideline-level evidence.

The National Lipid Association 2024 focused update on Lp(a) (Journal of Clinical Lipidology) recommends measuring Lp(a) at least once in every adult and describes three bands:

- Under 75 nmol/L (under 30 mg/dL): low risk
- 75 to under 125 nmol/L (30 to under 50 mg/dL): intermediate
- 125 nmol/L or higher (50 mg/dL or higher): high risk

The European Atherosclerosis Society consensus uses similar rule-out (under 30 mg/dL or 75 nmol/L) and rule-in (over 50 mg/dL or 125 nmol/L) cut-offs, with the range between treated as a grey zone where other risk factors decide. Both groups stress that Lp(a) should be read together with a person's overall cardiovascular risk, not on its own, and that risk increases continuously with level.
