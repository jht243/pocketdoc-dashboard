The 2026 ACC/AHA multisociety dyslipidemia guideline brought back numeric treatment goals for LDL-C (LDL cholesterol, the so-called bad cholesterol) and non-HDL-C (total cholesterol minus HDL), which the 2018 guideline had dropped. For primary prevention, meaning adults without known heart or vascular disease, the goals follow the 10-year PREVENT-ASCVD risk tier:

- Low risk, under 3%: lifestyle optimization, no numeric goal set.
- Borderline, 3% to under 5%, or intermediate, 5% to under 10%, when therapy is started: LDL-C under 100 mg/dL and non-HDL-C under 130 mg/dL.
- High risk, 10% or higher: LDL-C under 70 mg/dL and non-HDL-C under 100 mg/dL.

Alongside the absolute goals, the guideline describes percentage reductions: about 30% or more for intermediate risk and 50% or more for high risk. For adults aged 40 to 75 with diabetes, the same goals apply, with the tighter under 70 / under 100 mg/dL goal when PREVENT risk is above 10%.

These are guideline goals for people being treated, not a diagnosis. A lab report's printed reference range for LDL is a population range and is separate from the guideline goal that applies to a given person's risk tier.
