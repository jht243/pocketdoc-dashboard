A standard lipid panel (cholesterol test, lipid profile) does not usually require fasting. The National Lipid Association 2021 scientific statement on lipid measurements says it is acceptable to screen with a nonfasting sample. Total cholesterol, HDL and non-HDL cholesterol change very little after a meal, and non-HDL-C is reliable either way. Triglycerides rise the most after eating.

Key points from the NLA statement:

- If triglycerides are above 175 mg/dL on a fasting or nonfasting test, a follow-up fasting lipid panel is recommended.
- Abnormal LDL-C or triglyceride screening results are followed up with fasting levels.
- Blood should be drawn when a person is in a stable metabolic state; lipids measured during an acute illness may read lower and should not simply be discounted.
- After a change in cholesterol-lowering treatment, lipids are rechecked in about 4 to 12 weeks.
- Lab reports should state whether the sample was fasting.

Clinical summaries of the 2026 ACC/AHA multisociety dyslipidemia guideline likewise describe nonfasting panels as acceptable, with fasting preferred when triglycerides are very high.
