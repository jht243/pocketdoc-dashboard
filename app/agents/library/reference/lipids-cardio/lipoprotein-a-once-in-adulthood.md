Lipoprotein(a), written Lp(a) or Lpa and sometimes called lipoprotein little a, is an LDL-like particle whose level is set mostly by genetics. The 2026 ACC/AHA multisociety dyslipidemia guideline recommends measuring Lp(a) at least once in all adults. Because the level is largely inherited and changes little with diet or exercise, repeat testing is generally not needed.

The guideline treats an Lp(a) of 125 nmol/L or higher (about 50 mg/dL or higher) as a risk-enhancing factor, associated with roughly 1.4-fold higher ASCVD risk. Levels of 250 nmol/L or higher (about 100 mg/dL) are associated with at least twofold higher risk. Risk rises continuously with level rather than switching on at one number.

Labs report Lp(a) either in nmol/L (particle number) or mg/dL (mass). The two units are not interchangeable by a single exact factor, so a result should be read against the threshold in the same unit. A high Lp(a) is a reason guidelines give for controlling all other risk factors more tightly, including LDL cholesterol. Keywords: Lp(a), Lpa, lipoprotein a, inherited cholesterol, genetic heart risk.
