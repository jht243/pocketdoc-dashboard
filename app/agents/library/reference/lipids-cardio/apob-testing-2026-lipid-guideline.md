Apolipoprotein B (ApoB, apo B) is a protein carried one per particle on every particle that can build plaque, including LDL, VLDL remnants and Lp(a). An ApoB result is therefore a count of atherogenic particles, while LDL-C measures the cholesterol those particles carry. The two usually move together but can disagree, most often when triglycerides are high or in diabetes.

The 2026 ACC/AHA multisociety dyslipidemia guideline uses ApoB selectively rather than for everyone. It describes ApoB testing as reasonable to refine risk and guide whether to intensify therapy once LDL-C and non-HDL-C goals have been met, especially in people with elevated triglycerides, type 2 diabetes, cardiovascular-kidney-metabolic syndrome, very low achieved LDL-C, or known cardiovascular disease. LDL-C and non-HDL-C remain the primary treatment targets in the guideline.

Some summaries of the guideline list numeric ApoB goals by risk tier, but published summaries disagree on the numbers, so specific ApoB goal values should come from the full guideline text or the treating clinician. Keywords: ApoB, apolipoprotein B, particle number, LDL particles, advanced lipid test.
