The AHA and AMA 2020 joint statement on self-measured blood pressure (SMBP, home blood pressure monitoring) calls for a validated device and a standard routine. A 2021 American Family Physician review of that statement and the ACC/AHA guideline lists the steps:

- Use a validated, automatic upper-arm cuff in the right size, on a bare arm. Wrist cuffs and pharmacy kiosks are not recommended for routine use. Validated devices are listed at validatebp.org.
- For 30 minutes before, avoid exercise, caffeine and smoking; empty the bladder.
- Sit quietly for 5 minutes with back supported, feet flat and legs uncrossed, arm resting on a table with the cuff at heart level. No talking or texting.
- Take 2 readings at least 1 minute apart, morning and evening, for at least 3 and ideally 7 days, then average them. Some guidelines drop the first day, which tends to read higher.

Home readings usually run a little lower than office readings. The ACC/AHA guideline table treats a home average of 130/80 mm Hg as matching an office 130/80, while an office 140/90 corresponds to about 135/85 at home. Evidence is Moderate because these technique steps are expert consensus.
