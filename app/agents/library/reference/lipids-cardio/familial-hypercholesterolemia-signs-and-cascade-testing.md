Familial hypercholesterolemia (FH, inherited high cholesterol) is a genetic condition that keeps LDL cholesterol high from birth. The CDC estimates it affects about 1 in 311 people, and an identifiable gene change is found in roughly 60 to 80% of those affected.

Warning signs the CDC lists:

- LDL cholesterol over 190 mg/dL in adults or over 160 mg/dL in children
- A family history of early heart disease or heart attacks
- Cholesterol deposits: bumps on the knuckles, elbows or knees, a thickened or painful Achilles tendon, yellowish patches around the eyes, or a grayish-white ring at the edge of the cornea

The UK NICE guideline CG71 advises clinicians to suspect FH when adult total cholesterol is above 7.5 mmol/L (about 290 mg/dL) or there is premature coronary heart disease, meaning an event before 60 in the person or a first-degree relative. A clinical diagnosis uses the Simon Broome or Dutch Lipid Clinic Network criteria, followed by DNA testing in a specialist service. Once a gene change is found, NICE recommends cascade testing: offering DNA tests to first- and second-degree, and when possible third-degree, relatives. Children of an affected parent are offered DNA testing at the earliest opportunity, with a further offer by age 10 if it has not happened. The 2026 ACC/AHA guideline treats an LDL-C of 190 mg/dL or higher or known FH as a reason for treatment without a risk calculator.
