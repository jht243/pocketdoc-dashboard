The 2012 AACE/ATA hypothyroidism guideline draws a line at a TSH (thyroid stimulating hormone) of 10 mIU/L:

- TSH above 10 mIU/L: the guideline states these patients are at increased risk of heart failure and cardiovascular death and should be considered for treatment with levothyroxine (L-thyroxine) (Recommendation 15, Grade B).
- TSH between the lab's upper limit and 10 mIU/L: treatment should be decided on individual factors, and considered particularly when there are symptoms suggesting hypothyroidism, positive TPO antibodies, or atherosclerotic heart disease, heart failure, or risk factors for these (Recommendation 16, Grade B). The guideline summary says the decision to treat below 10 mIU/L should be tailored to the person.

The guideline graded both recommendations B because the supporting evidence is largely observational, which is why this passage is labelled Moderate.

These are thresholds at which a clinician considers treatment, not automatic triggers. A mildly raised TSH is commonly rechecked, often with free T4 and TPO antibodies, before any decision. Whether to start, and at what amount, is decided by the treating clinician.
