---
title: When TSH is rechecked after starting or changing thyroid medication
source: AACE/ATA 2012 Hypothyroidism Guidelines; ATA 2014 Guidelines for the Treatment of Hypothyroidism
evidence: Strong
approved_by: pending
as_of: 2014

TSH (thyroid stimulating hormone) changes slowly after a change in thyroid hormone replacement, so a test done too early can mislead.

The 2012 AACE/ATA hypothyroidism guideline states that adjustments are guided by TSH measured 4 to 8 weeks after:

- starting levothyroxine (L-thyroxine),
- any change in the prescribed amount, or
- a switch to a different levothyroxine product or manufacturer.

The guideline adds that after smaller changes, TSH can take 8 weeks or longer to plateau. The 2014 ATA guideline notes that levothyroxine reaches steady state in about 6 weeks, and recommends rechecking TSH at steady state after a change in product. Many clinicians therefore use a window of roughly 6 to 8 weeks.

The same logic applies after changes that affect absorption, such as starting calcium or iron, changing when the tablet is taken, pregnancy, or significant weight change. Once TSH is stable on an unchanged regimen, testing is typically less frequent. The 2012 guideline also suggests considering free T4 alongside TSH when monitoring treatment (Grade B). The treating clinician decides the testing schedule and any adjustment.
