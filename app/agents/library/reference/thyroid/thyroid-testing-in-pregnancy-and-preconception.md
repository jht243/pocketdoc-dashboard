Pregnancy changes thyroid tests. In the first trimester, TSH (thyroid stimulating hormone) normally falls and T4 rises, so non-pregnant reference ranges do not apply. The 2017 American Thyroid Association (ATA) pregnancy guideline recommends population-based, trimester-specific TSH reference ranges.

Other points from the guideline, as summarized by the ATA:

- Screening: the task force found insufficient evidence for or against testing every pregnant woman. It recommends TSH testing for newly pregnant women at higher risk, for example with type 1 diabetes or another autoimmune disease, thyroid symptoms or goiter, a family history of thyroid disease, or a history of pregnancy loss, preterm delivery or infertility.
- Women already on levothyroxine (thyroid hormone replacement): TSH testing before conception, aiming for a TSH no higher than 2.5 mU/L. Needs for thyroid hormone usually rise early in pregnancy, so the guideline advises contacting the prescriber as soon as pregnancy is confirmed or suspected; the prescriber adjusts treatment and rechecks.
- TPO antibody positive women with normal thyroid function: TSH checked when pregnancy is confirmed and about every 4 weeks until mid-pregnancy.
- Iodine: a prenatal supplement containing iodine is recommended before and during pregnancy and while breastfeeding; excess iodine, such as seaweed snacks or high-iodine supplements, should be avoided.
- Postpartum thyroiditis occurs in about 5% of pregnancies.

Labelled Moderate because several pregnancy recommendations rest on limited evidence.
