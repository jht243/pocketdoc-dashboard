---
title: Biotin supplements can distort thyroid blood test results
source: American Thyroid Association Clinical Thyroidology for the Public 2018; FDA biotin safety communication 2017, updated 2019
evidence: Strong
approved_by: pending
as_of: 2019
url: https://www.thyroid.org/patient-thyroid-information/ct-for-patients/december-2018/vol-11-issue-12-p-3-4/
---

Biotin (vitamin B7) is common in hair, skin and nail supplements and some multivitamins. Many lab tests use a biotin-streptavidin reaction, and biotin in the blood sample can throw these tests off.

The American Thyroid Association explains that biotin can make TSH (thyroid stimulating hormone) look falsely low and T4 and T3 look falsely high. That pattern looks like an overactive thyroid (hyperthyroidism, sometimes mimicking Graves' disease) or like thyroid medication being too strong, when it is actually a lab artifact. The ATA has recommended that patients stop taking biotin for at least 2 days before thyroid blood testing.

The FDA issued a safety communication in 2017, updated in 2019, warning that biotin can cause falsely high or falsely low results on many lab tests. The FDA's main concern was a falsely low troponin, the heart attack blood test, which can lead to a missed diagnosis. The FDA advises clinicians to ask about biotin, including in multivitamins and hair, skin and nail products, and to consider interference when a result doesn't fit the clinical picture.

Practical point: thyroid results that don't match symptoms should prompt a question about biotin use before any conclusions are drawn.
