Iodine is needed to make thyroid hormone, but too much iodine can also upset thyroid function. Iodine, potassium iodide, kelp and seaweed supplements, and "thyroid support" products are commonly sold for energy or thyroid health.

The American Thyroid Association (ATA) statement on excess iodine (issued 2013, updated 2024):

- Advises against taking iodine or kelp supplements above a set daily amount. It notes that many such products contain far more iodine than the Tolerable Upper Intake Level, and that intake above the upper level may cause thyroid dysfunction.
- Lists infants, older adults, pregnant and breastfeeding women, and people with existing thyroid disease as most vulnerable to excess iodine.
- Recommends that women planning pregnancy, pregnant or breastfeeding take a prenatal vitamin containing iodine as potassium iodide. Higher iodine amounts are for specific medical uses under supervision.

The 2014 ATA hypothyroidism treatment guideline also recommends against dietary supplements, nutraceuticals and other over-the-counter products, either in people with normal thyroid function or as a way to treat hypothyroidism (strong recommendation, low-quality evidence). It particularly cautions against large amounts of iodine, which can cause both an overactive and an underactive thyroid in people with underlying thyroid disease.

Kelp and "glandular" thyroid supplements are worth mentioning to the clinician, especially when thyroid results change unexpectedly.
