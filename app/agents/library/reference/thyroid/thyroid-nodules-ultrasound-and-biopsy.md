Thyroid nodules (lumps in the thyroid gland) are very common and are often found by chance on a scan. The 2015 American Thyroid Association (ATA) guideline notes that high-resolution ultrasound finds nodules in 19% to 68% of randomly selected people, more often in women and older adults. Thyroid cancer is found in about 7% to 15% of nodules, depending on age, sex, radiation exposure and family history.

Key steps in the ATA guideline:
- Measure TSH (thyroid stimulating hormone) at the first evaluation. If TSH is low, a radionuclide thyroid scan is done to look for an overactive "hot" nodule, which is rarely cancer (strong recommendation).
- Thyroid ultrasound, including the neck lymph nodes, for everyone with a known or suspected nodule (strong recommendation).
- Routine thyroglobulin blood testing is not recommended for evaluating nodules.

Fine-needle aspiration (FNA, a needle biopsy) depends on the ultrasound pattern and size:
- High or intermediate suspicion pattern: FNA recommended at 1 cm or larger.
- Low suspicion pattern: FNA recommended at 1.5 cm or larger.
- Very low suspicion, for example spongiform: FNA may be considered at 2 cm or larger; observation is also reasonable.
- Purely cystic nodules and nodules below these sizes: FNA not required.

Results are reported using the Bethesda system. A radiology report describing a nodule is a reason to follow up with a clinician.
