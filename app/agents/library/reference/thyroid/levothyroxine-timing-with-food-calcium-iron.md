---
title: Levothyroxine absorption - timing with food, calcium and iron
source: AACE/ATA 2012 Hypothyroidism Guidelines; ATA 2014 Guidelines for the Treatment of Hypothyroidism
evidence: Moderate
approved_by: pending
as_of: 2014
url: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4267409/

Levothyroxine (L-thyroxine, thyroid hormone replacement; brand names include Synthroid) is absorbed inconsistently when taken with food or certain other products. Both major guidelines address timing; neither of these points concerns the amount, which the prescriber sets.

- The 2012 AACE/ATA guideline recommends taking it consistently with water 30 to 60 minutes before breakfast, or at bedtime 4 hours after the last meal (Grade B).
- The 2014 ATA guideline recommends taking it 60 minutes before breakfast or at bedtime, at least 3 hours after the evening meal, for best absorption.
- The ATA 2014 guideline recommends separating levothyroxine from products that interfere with absorption, such as calcium carbonate and iron (ferrous sulfate). It notes that the traditional 4-hour separation is untested.

The 2012 guideline lists other things that can reduce absorption, including multivitamins containing iron or calcium, calcium salts, proton pump inhibitors (acid reducers), bile acid sequestrants, sucralfate, phosphate binders, and oral bisphosphonates.

Consistency matters: a change in timing, a new calcium or iron supplement, or a new acid reducer can move TSH. That is why TSH is often rechecked after such a change. Questions about how to time a specific medication belong with the prescriber or pharmacist.
