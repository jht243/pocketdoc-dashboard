Desiccated thyroid extract (DTE, natural thyroid, porcine thyroid, brand names include Armour Thyroid and NP Thyroid) is dried, powdered pig thyroid gland. It contains both T4 (thyroxine) and T3 (liothyronine). Levothyroxine (L-thyroxine, synthetic T4; brand names include Synthroid) contains T4 only, which the body converts to T3.

The 2014 American Thyroid Association (ATA) hypothyroidism treatment guideline recommends levothyroxine as routine care for primary hypothyroidism (underactive thyroid), in preference to thyroid extracts (strong recommendation, moderate-quality evidence). Its reasons:

- Some patients said they preferred thyroid extract in one short study, but there are no high-quality long-term trials showing it works better than levothyroxine.
- Thyroid extract has a T4 to T3 ratio of about 4.2 to 1, compared with about 14 to 1 in what the human thyroid releases. The extra T3 can push blood T3 above the normal range, with peaks soon after each tablet.
- Long-term safety data are limited.

The guideline's overall conclusion is that levothyroxine should remain the standard of care, and that no alternative preparation has shown consistent superiority for improving health outcomes. That is a guideline position, not a ban: some people do use thyroid extract with monitoring. Which thyroid medicine to use, and any switch between products, is the prescriber's decision, usually with a TSH recheck afterwards.
