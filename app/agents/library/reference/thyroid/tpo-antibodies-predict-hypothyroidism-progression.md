TPO antibodies (anti-thyroid peroxidase antibodies, TPOAb, anti-TPO) are the main blood marker of autoimmune thyroiditis, also called Hashimoto's thyroiditis, the most common cause of an underactive thyroid in iodine-sufficient countries.

The 2012 AACE/ATA hypothyroidism guideline recommends that clinicians consider measuring TPO antibodies when evaluating subclinical hypothyroidism (Recommendation 1, Grade B). The reason is prognostic: in people with subclinical hypothyroidism, progression to overt hypothyroidism occurred at about 4.3% per year when TPO antibodies were elevated versus about 2.6% per year without them. The guideline notes that a positive result may or may not change the decision to treat.

Other points from the guideline:

- Positive TPO antibodies are common in people without thyroid disease. In the NHANES survey of disease-free adults, TPOAb was positive in 11.3%, more often in women and with increasing age.
- Once present, these antibodies generally persist.
- Among thyroid antibodies, only TPOAb was significantly associated with hypothyroidism.

A positive TPO antibody with a normal TSH is a risk marker, not a diagnosis of hypothyroidism.
