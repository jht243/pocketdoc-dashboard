Some people treated for hypothyroidism (underactive thyroid) still feel tired, foggy or low despite a normal TSH (thyroid stimulating hormone). A common question is whether adding T3 (liothyronine, brand name Cytomel) to levothyroxine (T4) would help.

The 2014 American Thyroid Association (ATA) hypothyroidism treatment guideline addressed this:

- It recommends against the routine use of combined levothyroxine plus liothyronine for primary hypothyroidism. The reason is that randomized trials comparing it with levothyroxine alone gave conflicting results, and long-term outcome data are scarce (weak recommendation, moderate-quality evidence).
- It found insufficient evidence to recommend trying combination therapy outside a formal clinical trial.
- For people with a normal TSH who still feel unwell, it recommends acknowledging the symptoms and looking for other causes (weak recommendation). It also encouraged research into whether some subgroups might benefit from combination therapy.
- It recommends against compounded thyroid hormones because of concerns about safety and potency (strong recommendation, low-quality evidence).

Labelled Moderate because the core recommendations are graded weak. Ongoing symptoms on thyroid treatment are worth raising with the treating clinician, who can review other possible causes and decide whether any change in treatment makes sense.
