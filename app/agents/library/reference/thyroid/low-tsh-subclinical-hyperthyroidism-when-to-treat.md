Subclinical hyperthyroidism (mild overactive thyroid) means a low or undetectable TSH (thyroid stimulating hormone) with free T4 and T3 still in the normal range. The 2016 American Thyroid Association (ATA) hyperthyroidism guideline notes that in a US sample without known thyroid disease, 0.7% had a TSH below 0.1 mU/L and 1.8% had a TSH below 0.4 mU/L.

Thyroid hormone medication, recent illness, some drugs and pituitary disease are causes to rule out first. TSH often normalizes on its own, so it is repeated: within 2 to 6 weeks in higher-risk people, otherwise at 3 to 6 months.

Untreated subclinical hyperthyroidism is linked to atrial fibrillation (an irregular heart rhythm), heart failure, bone loss and fractures. The risk is higher when TSH is below 0.1 mU/L.

When the low TSH persists, the ATA guideline:
- TSH below 0.1 mU/L: recommends treatment for people 65 and older, people with heart disease, cardiac risk factors or osteoporosis, postmenopausal women not taking estrogen or a bisphosphonate, and people with symptoms (strong recommendation). For people under 65 with none of these, treatment should be considered (weak recommendation).
- TSH from 0.1 mU/L up to the lower limit of normal: treatment should be considered for people 65 and older or with heart disease, osteoporosis or symptoms. People under 65 without symptoms can be observed (weak recommendations).

The treating clinician decides whether to treat and how.
