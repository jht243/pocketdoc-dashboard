The 2012 AACE/ATA hypothyroidism guideline uses TSH (thyroid stimulating hormone) together with free T4 (free thyroxine) to classify an underactive thyroid:

- Subclinical hypothyroidism: TSH above the laboratory's upper reference limit with a free T4 that is still within the normal range. Often called mild or early underactive thyroid.
- Overt hypothyroidism: an elevated TSH, usually above 10 mIU/L, together with a free T4 below the normal range.

The guideline adds that the subclinical label applies only when thyroid function has been stable for weeks or more, the pituitary-thyroid axis is normal, and there is no recent or ongoing severe illness. TSH can rise temporarily while recovering from an illness, so one high TSH does not settle the category.

For the thyroid hormone measurement, the guideline recommends free T4 (by free T4 index, estimate, or direct immunoassay) rather than total T4 outside of pregnancy (Grade A). Total T4 is affected by binding-protein changes, for example from estrogen or pregnancy, which free T4 largely avoids. In pregnancy the guideline uses different, trimester-specific approaches, so non-pregnant adult definitions should not be applied to pregnant members.
