Some medicines and tests carry a large iodine load that can change how the thyroid works.

Amiodarone (brand names include Pacerone and Cordarone) is a heart rhythm medicine. The 2016 American Thyroid Association (ATA) hyperthyroidism guideline notes that amiodarone is 37% iodine by molecular weight. It can cause thyrotoxicosis (an overactive-thyroid state) in two ways: by supplying excess iodine, or by directly damaging thyroid cells and causing inflammation. Amiodarone-induced thyrotoxicosis occurs in up to 6% of people taking it in iodine-sufficient regions such as the US, and up to 10% in iodine-deficient regions. The guideline suggests checking thyroid function tests before starting amiodarone, within the first 3 months, and then every 3 to 6 months (weak recommendation, low-quality evidence). The drug's effects can last for months after it is stopped.

Iodinated contrast dye, used in CT scans and angiograms, can occasionally trigger iodine-induced hyperthyroidism. The guideline describes this as uncommon, usually mild and often temporary, but sometimes severe. Older adults with nodular goiters are most susceptible. It does not recommend routine preventive treatment before contrast for everyone.

A new change in TSH or free T4 after starting amiodarone or after a contrast scan is worth flagging to the prescribing clinician. Decisions about heart rhythm medicines rest with the prescriber.
