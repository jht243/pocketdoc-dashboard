The Dietary Guidelines for Americans, 2025-2030 set the daily sodium intake limit for the general population aged 14 and older at less than 2,300 milligrams a day. Lower limits apply to younger children. The guidelines add that highly active people may benefit from more sodium to replace losses in sweat, and that highly processed foods high in sodium should be avoided.

The ADA Standards of Care in Diabetes, 2026 use the same figure, advising people with diabetes to keep sodium below 2,300 milligrams a day as clinically appropriate. They note the most effective way to get there is to eat fewer processed foods.

The American Heart Association 2021 Dietary Guidance explains why this matters: salt intake directly raises blood pressure, and most sodium in the US diet comes from packaged and processed foods and food prepared outside the home, not the salt shaker.

Plain-language terms members use: salt, salty food, sodium on the nutrition label, high blood pressure, hypertension, bloating or water retention. Note that sodium on a nutrition label is listed in milligrams, and that table salt is not pure sodium.
