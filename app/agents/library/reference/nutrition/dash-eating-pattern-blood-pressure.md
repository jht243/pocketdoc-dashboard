DASH stands for Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension. The DASH eating pattern emphasizes fruits, vegetables and low-fat dairy, includes whole grains, and is lower in saturated fat and cholesterol.

The DASH-Sodium trial (Sacks and colleagues, New England Journal of Medicine, 2001) fed adults with blood pressure above 120/80 mm Hg, including people with stage 1 hypertension, either a typical American control diet or the DASH diet, each at high, intermediate and low sodium levels for 30 days at a time. Lowering sodium reduced blood pressure on both diets, and the largest effect came from combining the two. Compared with the control diet at high sodium, DASH at low sodium lowered systolic blood pressure by 7.1 mm Hg in participants without hypertension and by 11.5 mm Hg in those with stage 1 hypertension. Diastolic pressure also fell.

The National Lipid Association also lists DASH among the healthy eating patterns suitable for managing cholesterol.

Members may ask about this as the DASH diet, a diet for high blood pressure, hypertension diet, lowering BP with food, or cutting salt. Whether diet alone is enough for a given blood pressure reading is a question for the clinician.
