Time-restricted eating (TRE) means eating only within a set daily window, such as 8 hours, and fasting the rest of the day. It is one form of intermittent fasting, alongside alternate-day fasting and the 5:2 pattern.

The best-known trial is Liu and colleagues, New England Journal of Medicine, 2022. In 139 adults with obesity, both groups followed the same calorie-restricted diet for 12 months; one group also ate only between 8 a.m. and 4 p.m. Weight loss averaged 8.0 kg with TRE and 6.3 kg with calorie restriction alone, a difference that was not statistically significant, and metabolic risk factors such as blood pressure, glucose and lipids did not differ meaningfully between groups. The authors concluded TRE added no benefit beyond cutting calories.

The ADA Standards of Care in Diabetes, 2026 summarize the wider evidence similarly: intermittent fasting and TRE produce mild to moderate weight loss over short periods with no significant difference from continuous calorie restriction. People who take insulin or other glucose-lowering medicines face a risk of low blood sugar while fasting, which is a question for their prescriber.

Evidence for benefits beyond those of calorie reduction is still emerging. Members ask about this as intermittent fasting, IF, 16:8, eating window, skipping breakfast or OMAD.
