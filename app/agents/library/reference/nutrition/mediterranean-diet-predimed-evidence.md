PREDIMED was a Spanish randomized trial of about 7,447 people at high cardiovascular risk. It compared a Mediterranean diet supplemented with extra-virgin olive oil, a Mediterranean diet supplemented with mixed nuts, and a control diet that advised cutting dietary fat. Both Mediterranean groups had roughly 30% fewer major cardiovascular events (heart attack, stroke or cardiovascular death) than the control group.

The original 2013 paper was retracted by the New England Journal of Medicine in June 2018 because of randomization problems at some sites. The authors republished a corrected analysis the same month, adjusting for clustering and excluding participants with suspected randomization departures. The roughly 30% reduction held, although some critics consider the evidence weaker than originally presented.

Guidelines continue to support the pattern. The ADA Standards of Care in Diabetes, 2026 (recommendation 5.30) advise considering a Mediterranean-style eating plan, rich in fatty fish, nuts and seeds, to reduce cardiovascular risk (grade A) and improve glucose metabolism (grade B).

A Mediterranean pattern centers on vegetables, fruit, legumes, whole grains, olive oil, nuts, fish, and limited red and processed meat. Members ask about it as the Mediterranean diet, olive oil, heart-healthy eating, or the best diet for the heart.
