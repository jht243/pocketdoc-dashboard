The Dietary Guidelines for Americans, 2025-2030 keep the long-standing limit on saturated fat: in general, saturated fat should not exceed 10% of total daily calories. The guidelines say that sharply cutting back on highly processed foods helps meet this goal, and they recommend prioritizing oils that supply essential fatty acids, such as olive oil. They also note that more high-quality research is needed on which types of dietary fat best support long-term health.

Other major guidance points the same way. The American Heart Association 2021 Dietary Guidance advises using liquid plant oils in place of tropical oils (coconut, palm, palm kernel), animal fats such as butter and lard, and partially hydrogenated fats. The ADA Standards of Care in Diabetes, 2026 advise people with or at risk for diabetes to limit foods high in saturated fat to reduce cardiovascular risk.

Saturated fat is found mainly in fatty and processed meats, butter, cheese, full-fat dairy, coconut and palm oil, and many baked and fried foods. It is the dietary fat most closely tied to LDL cholesterol ("bad" cholesterol, LDL-C), which is why members asking about cholesterol, butter, red meat, keto or high-fat diets often land on this limit.
