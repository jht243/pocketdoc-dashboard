A 2018 American Heart Association science advisory (Rimm and colleagues, Circulation) concluded that eating 1 to 2 seafood meals a week is reasonable to lower the risk of heart failure, coronary heart disease, ischemic stroke and sudden cardiac death, especially when seafood replaces less healthy foods. The advisory favors non-fried seafood and species richer in long-chain omega-3 fats (EPA and DHA), such as salmon, sardines, anchovies and tuna. Much of the supporting evidence is observational, so this passage is graded Moderate.

This is about eating fish, not fish oil pills. The library's separate passage on fish oil supplements covers the VITAL trial and atrial fibrillation.

Mercury matters most during pregnancy, breastfeeding and childhood. The FDA and EPA advice (revised 2021) suggests 2 to 3 servings a week from their lower-mercury Best Choices list during pregnancy and breastfeeding, and advises avoiding the highest-mercury fish, including king mackerel, marlin, orange roughy, shark, swordfish, Gulf of Mexico tilefish and bigeye tuna. The Dietary Guidelines for Americans, 2025-2030 also name low-mercury omega-3-rich seafood such as salmon, sardines and trout for pregnancy.
