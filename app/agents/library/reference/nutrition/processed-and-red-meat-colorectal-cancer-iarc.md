In October 2015 the International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC), the cancer agency of the World Health Organization, evaluated meat after a working group of 22 experts reviewed more than 800 studies.

- Processed meat was classified as carcinogenic to humans (Group 1), based on sufficient evidence that eating it causes colorectal cancer (colon and rectal cancer). Processed meat means meat transformed by salting, curing, fermentation, smoking or similar methods, such as hot dogs, ham, sausages, corned beef, beef jerky and canned meat; cured meats such as bacon fit the same definition.
- Red meat, meaning mammalian muscle meat such as beef, veal, pork, lamb and goat, was classified as probably carcinogenic to humans (Group 2A), based on limited human evidence plus strong mechanistic evidence. The association was mainly with colorectal cancer, with some links to pancreatic and prostate cancer.
- The experts estimated that each 50-gram portion of processed meat eaten daily raises colorectal cancer risk by about 18%.

An IARC group describes how certain the evidence is that something can cause cancer, not how large the risk is. IARC stressed that an individual's risk from processed meat remains small but rises with the amount eaten.

The American Heart Association 2021 guidance advises limiting processed meats. The Dietary Guidelines for Americans, 2025-2030 include red meat among protein foods and advise choosing meat without added sugars, refined starches or chemical additives.

Members ask about bacon, deli meat, hot dogs, red meat and cancer, or colon cancer risk.
