The PROT-AGE Study Group, convened by the European Union Geriatric Medicine Society, published evidence-based protein recommendations for older people in the Journal of the American Medical Directors Association in 2013. The group argued that the general adult RDA of 0.8 grams of protein per kilogram of body weight per day is too low to preserve muscle and function with age.

Its recommendations:
- Healthy adults over 65: an average of at least 1.0 to 1.2 grams of protein per kilogram of body weight per day.
- Older adults who exercise or are otherwise active: 1.2 grams per kilogram per day or more.
- Most older adults with an acute or chronic illness: 1.2 to 1.5 grams per kilogram per day.
- The exception is severe kidney disease (eGFR below 30 mL/min/1.73m² and not on dialysis), where protein may need to be limited.

The Dietary Guidelines for Americans, 2025-2030 also note that some older adults need fewer calories but equal or greater amounts of protein. This is an expert consensus position above the RDA rather than a US guideline requirement, so it is graded Moderate.

Related terms members use: muscle loss with age, sarcopenia, staying strong, protein for seniors, frailty.
