The Dietary Guidelines for Americans, 2025-2030, released by USDA and HHS in January 2026, take a firmer line on added sugar than earlier editions. They state that no amount of added sugars or non-nutritive (low-calorie or artificial) sweeteners is recommended as part of a healthy diet, and that a single meal should contain no more than 10 grams of added sugars.

The previous 2020-2025 edition set the limit as less than 10% of daily calories from added sugars. The new edition replaces that with the per-meal ceiling and the broader advice to avoid added sugar.

Practical points from the guidelines:
- Avoid sugar-sweetened beverages such as soda, fruit drinks and energy drinks.
- Spot added sugar on ingredient labels by words that include "sugar" or "syrup" or end in "-ose"; honey, molasses and fruit juice concentrate also count.
- Sugar that occurs naturally in whole fruit and plain milk is not counted as added sugar.
- 100% fruit juice should be kept to limited portions or diluted with water.

Added sugar is a common driver of higher triglycerides, blood sugar (glucose, A1C) and weight, which is why it comes up when members ask about sweets, soda, desserts or sugar cravings.
