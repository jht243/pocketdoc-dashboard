Two different protein figures are in circulation, and members often see both.

- The National Academies Recommended Dietary Allowance (RDA) for protein is 0.8 grams per kilogram of body weight per day. The RDA is the amount that covers the basic needs of nearly all healthy adults; it is a floor, not an optimal target.
- The Dietary Guidelines for Americans, 2025-2030 set a protein goal of 1.2 to 1.6 grams per kilogram of body weight per day, adjusted to individual calorie needs, and advise prioritizing protein foods at every meal from both animal sources (eggs, poultry, seafood, red meat) and plant sources (beans, peas, lentils, nuts, seeds, soy).

Commentators, including the Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health, note that the higher range is 50 to 100% above the RDA and was not part of the 2025 Dietary Guidelines Advisory Committee scientific report, so the evidence behind the specific range is debated. That is why this passage is graded Moderate rather than Strong.

Protein needs can differ in chronic kidney disease, where clinicians may advise limiting protein, so kidney function questions belong with the care team. Members ask about this as protein grams, how much protein, protein per pound or kilo, high-protein diet, or muscle building.
