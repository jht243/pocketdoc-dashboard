The Dietary Guidelines for Americans, 2025-2030 advise avoiding highly processed packaged, prepared and ready-to-eat foods that are salty or sweet, such as chips, cookies and candy, and limiting foods with artificial flavors, petroleum-based dyes, artificial preservatives and low-calorie sweeteners. They recommend nutrient-dense foods and home-prepared meals instead. The American Heart Association 2021 Dietary Guidance likewise advises choosing minimally processed foods over ultra-processed ones.

Research usually sorts foods with the NOVA system, where ultra-processed foods are industrial formulations such as soft drinks, packaged snacks, sweetened cereals and reconstituted meat products. The largest summary is an umbrella review by Lane and colleagues (The BMJ, 2024) of 45 pooled analyses covering about 9.9 million people. Higher ultra-processed food intake was linked to 32 of the 45 outcomes studied. The strongest (convincing) evidence was for higher risk of death from cardiovascular disease (about 50% higher), type 2 diabetes, anxiety and common mental disorders. Highly suggestive evidence linked it to higher all-cause mortality (about 21% higher), heart disease death, depression, obesity and poor sleep.

These are observational associations, and most were rated low or very low quality under GRADE, so they do not prove cause. That is why this passage is graded Moderate.

Members ask about this as processed food, junk food, UPFs, packaged snacks, food additives, or eating clean or real food.
