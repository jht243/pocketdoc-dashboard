The American Heart Association reviewed dietary cholesterol in a 2019 science advisory led by Carson and colleagues in Circulation. Its main findings, paraphrased:

- Observational studies from several countries generally do not show a significant link between dietary cholesterol, or egg intake, and cardiovascular disease. The authors caution that egg findings may be confounded by what eggs are eaten with, such as bacon and sausage, and by other lifestyle factors.
- Feeding studies differ, but most find that cholesterol intakes above current average levels raise total and LDL cholesterol ("bad" cholesterol, LDL-C).
- Rather than a numeric dietary cholesterol target, guidance should focus on healthy eating patterns such as Mediterranean-style and DASH diets, which are naturally fairly low in cholesterol.

The AHA's summary of the advisory says healthy people can include up to one whole egg, or its equivalent, a day as part of a heart-healthy pattern, with caveats for people with high cholesterol (dyslipidemia), diabetes, or risk of heart failure.

The Dietary Guidelines for Americans, 2025-2030 list eggs among recommended protein foods and do not set a dietary cholesterol limit. Saturated fat, not dietary cholesterol, remains the fat most closely tied to LDL cholesterol in current guidance.

Members ask about eggs and cholesterol, egg yolks, how many eggs a day, or whether shrimp and other cholesterol-rich foods raise LDL. Whether diet changes are enough for a given lipid panel is a question for the clinician.
