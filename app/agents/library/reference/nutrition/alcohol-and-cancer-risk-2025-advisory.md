On January 3, 2025, the US Surgeon General issued an advisory identifying alcohol as a well-established cause of at least seven cancers: breast, colorectal, esophageal, liver, mouth, throat (pharynx) and voice box (larynx). The link holds for beer, wine and spirits alike. Risk rises with the amount consumed, and for some cancers, such as breast, mouth and throat, risk may increase at around one drink a day or less. The advisory names alcohol the third leading preventable cause of cancer in the US, after tobacco and obesity, and notes that fewer than half of US adults recognize the link. A National Academies review released around the same time rated the certainty about risk at light drinking levels lower, so the size of the risk at small amounts is still debated.

The Dietary Guidelines for Americans, 2025-2030 advise consuming less alcohol for better overall health and list groups who should avoid it entirely, including pregnant women, people recovering from alcohol use disorder, and people on medications or with conditions that interact with alcohol. The American Heart Association 2021 guidance advises against starting to drink for heart health, and the ADA Standards of Care in Diabetes, 2026 advise non-drinkers not to start, noting alcohol can cause delayed low blood sugar in people using insulin or insulin secretagogues.
