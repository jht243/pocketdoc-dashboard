The ADA Standards of Care in Diabetes, 2026 (recommendation 5.24) advise emphasizing minimally processed, nutrient-dense, high-fiber carbohydrate sources, with at least 14 grams of fiber per 1,000 kcal eaten. The same ratio appeared in the Dietary Guidelines for Americans, 2020-2025, and traces back to the National Academies (Institute of Medicine) adequate intake for fiber. At a 2,000-calorie intake, that works out to roughly 28 grams of fiber a day. Most Americans fall well short of it.

The 2025-2030 Dietary Guidelines do not restate a gram target; they tell people to prioritize fiber-rich whole grains, eat vegetables and fruits in their whole form, and note that high-fiber foods support a diverse gut microbiome.

Good food sources include beans, lentils and other legumes, oats and barley, whole grains, vegetables, whole fruit, nuts and seeds. Soluble or viscous fiber (in oats, barley, beans, and some fruits) binds cholesterol and bile acids in the gut, which is why the National Lipid Association counts it as a way to help lower LDL cholesterol.

Members may ask about this as fiber, roughage, gut health, constipation, staying full, or lowering cholesterol or blood sugar with food.
