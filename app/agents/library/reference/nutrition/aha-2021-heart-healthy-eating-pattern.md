The American Heart Association 2021 scientific statement focuses on overall eating patterns rather than single foods or nutrients. Its main features, paraphrased:

- Balance calorie intake with activity to keep a healthy weight, pairing a healthy diet with at least 150 minutes of moderate physical activity a week. Calorie needs fall with each adult decade.
- Eat plenty of fruits and vegetables in variety; fresh, frozen, canned and dried all count.
- Choose mostly whole grains rather than refined grains.
- Favor plant proteins such as legumes and nuts, eat fish regularly, choose lean cuts if eating meat, and limit processed meats such as bacon and hot dogs.
- Use liquid plant oils instead of tropical oils, animal fats and partially hydrogenated fats.
- Choose minimally processed foods over ultra-processed foods.
- Limit drinks and foods with added sugars.
- Choose and prepare foods with little or no salt.
- If not drinking alcohol, do not start; if drinking, limit intake.
- Apply this wherever food is prepared or eaten, including restaurants.

The statement says the pattern can be adapted to cultural traditions and personal food preferences. Members may ask about this as a heart-healthy diet, eating for cholesterol, eating for blood pressure, or what the AHA recommends.
