Serum creatinine (SCr, creatinine blood test) depends on how much creatinine the body makes, not only on how well the kidneys filter it. The KDIGO 2024 CKD guideline lists these non-kidney influences on creatinine-based eGFR:

- Muscle mass: bodybuilders and very muscular people make more creatinine, which lowers eGFR; amputation, spinal cord injury, eating disorders and muscle wasting do the opposite.
- Diet: high-protein diets and creatine supplements raise creatinine; vegetarian, low-protein and keto diets also alter it.
- A recent cooked meat or fish meal: KDIGO cites a study where creatinine rose about 0.23 mg/dL after such a meal, equal to an eGFR drop of about 20 mL/min/1.73m². KDIGO suggests waiting at least 12 hours after meat or fish before the blood draw where practical.
- Medications that block tubular creatinine secretion, anabolic steroids, and some broad-spectrum antibiotics.

KDIGO practice point 1.2.2.4 states that creatinine must be interpreted with dietary intake in mind. When eGFR is likely inaccurate for one of these reasons and the result affects decisions, KDIGO recommends measuring cystatin C.
