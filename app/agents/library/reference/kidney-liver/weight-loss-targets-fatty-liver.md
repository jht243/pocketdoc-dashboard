For fatty liver disease (NAFLD, now MASLD; MASH, formerly NASH), the AASLD 2023 Practice Guidance treats lifestyle change as the foundation of care and describes a dose-response effect of weight loss:

- Weight loss of 3% to 5% of body weight improves steatosis (liver fat).
- Greater weight loss, generally more than 10%, is usually needed to improve steatohepatitis (MASH, liver inflammation) and fibrosis (scarring).
- Even modest weight loss can be impactful, especially in milder disease.

AASLD guidance statements recommend that people with fatty liver and overweight or obesity follow a diet that creates a calorie deficit, preferably one limited in carbohydrates and saturated fat and rich in fiber and unsaturated fats, such as the Mediterranean diet, for its added cardiovascular benefit. People should be strongly encouraged to increase activity as much as possible, because exercise has liver and cardiometabolic benefits even without weight loss.

AASLD acknowledges that sustaining weight loss is hard: few people achieve effective weight loss with structured programs at 1 year, and fewer than half of those maintain it at 5 years. A multidisciplinary team, with dietitians and behavioral support, improves the odds. Obesity medications and bariatric surgery are additional options a clinician may discuss.
