When the FIB-4 score is 1.3 or higher, the AASLD 2023 Practice Guidance recommends a secondary test of liver fibrosis (scarring). In primary care, vibration-controlled transient elastography (VCTE, FibroScan, liver stiffness scan) or other ultrasound elastography is favored over MR elastography because of cost; the ELF blood test is an alternative where elastography is not available.

AASLD interprets VCTE liver stiffness measurement (LSM) in kilopascals:

- Below 8 kPa: can be used to rule out advanced fibrosis, especially after a FIB-4.
- 8 to 12 kPa: may be associated with fibrotic steatohepatitis.
- Above 12 kPa: high likelihood of advanced fibrosis, although the positive predictive value is modest.

Liver stiffness also rises with congestion, marked inflammation and infiltrative disease, so a high reading is not specific to scarring. AASLD notes a 20% increase in stiffness over time may signal progression. People whose secondary testing still shows intermediate or high risk, or whose tests disagree, should be referred to a liver specialist under AASLD guidance.
