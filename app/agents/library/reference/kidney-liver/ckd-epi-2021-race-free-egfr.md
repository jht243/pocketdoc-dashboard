Estimated GFR (eGFR, estimated kidney function) on a lab report is calculated from serum creatinine with an equation. The KDIGO 2024 CKD guideline recommends using a validated estimating equation rather than relying on creatinine alone, and states that race should not be used in computing eGFR.

The 2021 CKD-EPI creatinine equation, widely used by US labs, was refitted without a race term and uses creatinine, age and sex. KDIGO notes that dropping the race coefficient leads to a small overestimate of GFR in non-Black individuals and a small underestimate in Black individuals compared with the older 2009 equation, but the change is small relative to the normal variability of eGFR. The 2021 CKD-EPI creatinine-cystatin C equation, which combines both filtration markers without a race term, improves accuracy in all groups.

KDIGO advises using the same equation consistently within a region, which matters when comparing eGFR results from different labs or over time. eGFR is reported in mL/min/1.73m² and rounded to a whole number.
