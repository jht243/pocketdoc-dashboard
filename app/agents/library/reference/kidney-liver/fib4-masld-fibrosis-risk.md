The AASLD 2023 Practice Guidance recommends the FIB-4 index (fibrosis-4 score, liver scarring risk score) as the first-line test to estimate the chance of advanced liver fibrosis in fatty liver disease (MASLD, formerly NAFLD). FIB-4 is calculated from age, AST, ALT and platelet count.

AASLD risk categories:

- Below 1.3: low risk. The high negative predictive value means advanced fibrosis is unlikely, and care can stay in primary care.
- 1.3 to 2.67: intermediate risk.
- Above 2.67: high risk.

AASLD guidance states that everyone with hepatic steatosis or clinically suspected fatty liver because of obesity and metabolic risk factors should have a FIB-4, and that people with prediabetes, type 2 diabetes, or two or more metabolic risk factors should repeat it every 1 to 2 years. General population screening is not advised. If FIB-4 is 1.3 or higher, AASLD recommends a second test, such as vibration-controlled transient elastography (FibroScan), MR elastography or the ELF blood test. AASLD notes FIB-4 performs less well in type 2 diabetes and its cut-offs for that group may change as data accumulate.
