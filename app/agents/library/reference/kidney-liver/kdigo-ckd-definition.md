The KDIGO 2024 CKD guideline defines chronic kidney disease (CKD, kidney disease, reduced kidney function) as abnormalities of kidney structure or function present for at least 3 months, with implications for health. Either of the following, lasting more than 3 months, meets the definition:

- Decreased GFR: a glomerular filtration rate below 60 mL/min/1.73m² (GFR categories G3a to G5).
- One or more markers of kidney damage, most commonly albuminuria with a urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR, ACR, urine microalbumin) of 30 mg/g or higher. Other markers include urine sediment abnormalities, persistent hematuria, tubular disorders, abnormalities on biopsy or imaging, and a history of kidney transplant.

Two consequences follow. First, a single low eGFR or a single high ACR is not CKD; the abnormality has to persist beyond 3 months, which separates CKD from acute kidney injury. Second, an eGFR of 60 or above (categories G1 and G2) does not meet the definition unless a marker of kidney damage is also present. KDIGO classifies CKD by Cause, GFR category and Albuminuria category, abbreviated CGA.
