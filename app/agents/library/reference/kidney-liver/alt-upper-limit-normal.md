ALT (alanine aminotransferase, SGPT, liver enzyme) is the most liver-specific of the routine liver tests. Lab reference ranges vary widely, and one study cited by the ACG found lab upper limits from 31 to 72 U/L. The 2017 ACG Clinical Guideline on abnormal liver chemistries proposed that a true healthy upper limit is 29 to 33 U/L for men and 19 to 25 U/L for women. The AASLD 2023 Practice Guidance repeats these figures, noting that normal values printed by most labs are higher than what should be considered normal in fatty liver disease, and the AASLD MASLD decision tree treats ALT above 33 U/L in males or above 25 U/L in females as elevated.

The ACG guideline notes that an ALT considered normal by a lab's own range can still carry higher liver-related mortality risk, and that clinicians should evaluate values above the healthy limits.

A normal ALT does not rule out liver disease. AASLD states aminotransferases are frequently normal in people with advanced fatty liver disease, which is why fibrosis risk is assessed with the FIB-4 score rather than ALT alone.
