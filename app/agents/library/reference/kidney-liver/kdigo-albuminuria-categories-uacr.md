The KDIGO 2024 CKD guideline classifies albuminuria (protein in urine, microalbumin, urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, UACR or ACR) into three categories:

- A1: ACR below 30 mg/g, normal to mildly increased.
- A2: ACR 30 to 300 mg/g, moderately increased (formerly called microalbuminuria).
- A3: ACR above 300 mg/g, severely increased.

KDIGO prefers a urine ACR, ideally on a first morning midstream sample, as the initial test. A random ACR of 30 mg/g or higher should be confirmed with a subsequent first morning sample, and dipstick results should be confirmed with a quantitative lab ACR.

Several factors can falsely raise ACR: blood in the urine, menstruation, recent exercise and a symptomatic urinary tract infection. Because the ratio divides by urine creatinine, people with low muscle mass or lower creatinine excretion can show a higher ACR, and high-protein intake or high body weight can lower it. Persistent albuminuria for more than 3 months is a marker of kidney damage even when eGFR is normal.
