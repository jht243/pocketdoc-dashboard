AST (aspartate aminotransferase, SGOT) and ALT (alanine aminotransferase, SGPT) are both liver enzymes, but the 2017 ACG Clinical Guideline on abnormal liver chemistries points out they are not equally specific. ALT is found mainly in the liver. AST is also present in heart muscle, skeletal muscle, kidney and brain, so an AST rise without an ALT rise suggests a heart or muscle source; hard exercise is a common everyday example.

Patterns the ACG describes:

- Alcohol-related liver disease is commonly associated with an AST to ALT ratio of at least 2 to 1, with AST or ALT rarely above 300 U/L; a ratio above 3 to 1 makes alcohol even more likely.
- Fatty liver disease has no unique pattern, although ALT is generally higher than AST.
- The degree of elevation guides the workup: under 5 times the upper limit of normal prompts evaluation for viral hepatitis, alcohol, fatty liver, hemochromatosis, Wilson disease, autoimmune hepatitis and drug or supplement injury; 5 to 15 times adds acute viral hepatitis; above 15 times adds acetaminophen toxicity and shock liver.
