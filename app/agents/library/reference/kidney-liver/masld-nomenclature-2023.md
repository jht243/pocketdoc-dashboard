In June 2023 a multisociety Delphi consensus led by AASLD, EASL and the Latin American liver association replaced the term nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD, fatty liver) with metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD).

- Steatotic liver disease (SLD) is the umbrella term for fat in the liver from any cause.
- MASLD is defined as hepatic steatosis plus at least one of five cardiometabolic risk factors: raised BMI or waist circumference, raised fasting glucose or A1c or type 2 diabetes, raised blood pressure, raised triglycerides, or low HDL cholesterol, each with treatment counting as present.
- MASH (metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis) replaces NASH.
- MetALD describes MASLD with greater weekly alcohol intake than MASLD allows.
- Steatosis with no metabolic risk factor and no known cause is cryptogenic SLD.

AASLD states the new names do not change natural history, staging, biomarkers or trial evidence; studies done under the NAFLD label still inform MASLD. Guidance documents published before the change, including the AASLD 2023 Practice Guidance, use the older term.
