Coffee is one of the few everyday drinks that liver guidelines mention favorably. The AASLD 2023 Practice Guidance on fatty liver disease (NAFLD, now called MASLD, metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease) states that coffee consumption, independent of its caffeine content, may be beneficial.

- Based on epidemiological studies and meta-analyses showing lower risk of fatty liver disease and liver fibrosis (scarring), AASLD says drinking three or more cups of coffee a day could be recommended in the absence of contraindications.
- Its key point: coffee, caffeinated or decaffeinated, at three or more cups daily is associated with less advanced liver disease.

The evidence is observational, which is why this is graded Moderate: coffee drinkers may differ from nondrinkers in other ways, and trials have not shown that adding coffee reverses liver disease. Coffee is an add-on, not a substitute, for the AASLD foundations of fatty liver care: weight loss when indicated, a healthy eating pattern such as the Mediterranean diet, regular physical activity, limiting alcohol, and managing diabetes, blood pressure and cholesterol. AASLD's advice is conditioned on the absence of contraindications, so personal factors such as caffeine sensitivity or effects on sleep are worth discussing with a clinician.
