NSAIDs (nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs) include common over-the-counter pain relievers such as ibuprofen (Advil, Motrin) and naproxen (Aleve), and prescription drugs such as diclofenac. The KDIGO 2024 CKD guideline explains how they affect the kidneys:

- NSAIDs reduce the prostaglandin-dependent blood flow that helps the kidney filter, which can lower GFR. They can also cause allergic interstitial nephritis and nephrotic syndrome, and are linked to analgesic nephropathy and high blood pressure.
- Indiscriminate chronic over-the-counter NSAID use has been associated with a higher risk of kidney failure compared with nonuse, and KDIGO says it should be discouraged.
- NSAIDs are among the most common potentially inappropriate nephrotoxic medicines: KDIGO cites data that 18% to 20% of people with CKD stages G3 to G5 receive at least one such drug each year.
- KDIGO lists acetaminophen (paracetamol, Tylenol) as a potential non-nephrotoxic alternative for pain, and notes that careful, supervised NSAID use may sometimes be preferred over pain medicines with greater harms, such as opioids.

KDIGO practice point 4.1.3 asks clinicians to review and limit over-the-counter medicines and dietary or herbal remedies that may harm people with CKD. Risk is higher with dehydration or illness. People with reduced eGFR or albuminuria who use NSAIDs regularly should review this with their clinician.
