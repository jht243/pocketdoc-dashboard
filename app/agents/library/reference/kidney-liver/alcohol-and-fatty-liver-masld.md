Alcohol and metabolic risk factors add together in the liver. The AASLD 2023 Practice Guidance on fatty liver disease (NAFLD, now MASLD) makes these points:

- Alcohol can be a cofactor for liver disease progression in fatty liver, and intake should be quantified and reassessed regularly (guidance statement 6).
- People with clinically significant liver fibrosis, stage F2 or higher, should abstain from alcohol completely (guidance statement 7).
- Moderate drinking increases the chance of advanced fibrosis, particularly with obesity or type 2 diabetes, and obesity plus alcohol synergistically raises the risk of liver injury, cirrhosis, liver cancer and death from liver disease.
- Earlier studies suggesting light drinking protects against fatty liver were not borne out: in a later study, moderate drinking was linked to less improvement in liver fat and lower odds of MASH resolution than not drinking.
- Daily alcohol may raise the risk of liver cancer and cancers outside the liver. Individual susceptibility varies widely, and AASLD notes the amount that changes the disease course for a given person is unclear.

Under the 2023 nomenclature, fatty liver with metabolic risk plus higher alcohol intake is called MetALD. Fibrosis stage is estimated with FIB-4 and, if needed, liver stiffness testing such as FibroScan, so those results help determine which AASLD advice applies.
