Cystatin C is a second blood marker of kidney filtration that is much less affected by muscle mass and diet than creatinine. The KDIGO 2024 CKD guideline recommends using a combined creatinine and cystatin C eGFR (eGFRcr-cys) in clinical situations when creatinine-based eGFR is less accurate and GFR affects clinical decisions, such as diagnosing or staging CKD or adjusting medications (Recommendation 1.2.2.1, graded 1C).

KDIGO's approach is stepwise: start with creatinine-based eGFR; if it is thought to be inaccurate, measure cystatin C and calculate eGFRcr-cys; if even more accuracy is needed, measure GFR directly with an exogenous filtration marker. Situations listed include high or low muscle mass, bodybuilding, high-protein diets or creatine supplements, amputation, eating disorders and some medications.

Cystatin C has its own non-kidney influences, including smoking, high inflammation, very low muscle mass, catabolic illness and steroid use. KDIGO notes that when creatinine and cystatin C estimates disagree, the combined eGFRcr-cys is more accurate than either alone, and the size and direction of the difference can itself be informative.
