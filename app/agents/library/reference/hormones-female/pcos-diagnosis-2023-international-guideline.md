PCOS (polycystic ovary syndrome) is diagnosed in adults when at least two of these three features are present, after other conditions that mimic it are excluded. The 2023 International Evidence-based PCOS Guideline refines the earlier 2003 Rotterdam criteria:

- Irregular cycles or ovulatory dysfunction: from 3 years after the first period until perimenopause, cycles shorter than 21 days or longer than 35 days, or fewer than 8 cycles a year.
- Hyperandrogenism (high male-type hormones): clinical signs, especially hirsutism (excess facial or body hair), or biochemical, using total and free testosterone; free testosterone can be estimated by the free androgen index.
- Polycystic ovarian morphology on ultrasound, OR a high AMH (anti-Müllerian hormone) blood level. For ultrasound, 20 or more follicles per ovary in at least one ovary is the adult threshold.

Changes and cautions in the 2023 guideline:
- When both irregular cycles and hyperandrogenism are present, neither ultrasound nor AMH is needed.
- AMH may replace ultrasound in adults, but AMH should not be used as a single test for PCOS, and ultrasound and AMH should not both be done because that leads to over-diagnosis.
- In adolescents, neither ultrasound nor AMH should be used; diagnosis needs both irregular cycles and hyperandrogenism, and those with one feature are followed as at increased risk.

Diagnosis is made by a clinician after excluding other causes such as thyroid disease or high prolactin.
