The 2022 Hormone Therapy Position Statement of The North American Menopause Society (now The Menopause Society) states that hormone therapy (HT, menopausal hormone therapy, MHT, HRT, estrogen therapy) remains the most effective treatment for hot flashes and night sweats (vasomotor symptoms, VMS) and for genitourinary syndrome of menopause, and has been shown to prevent bone loss and fracture.

Its central benefit-risk conclusions:

- For women younger than 60 or within 10 years of menopause onset, with no contraindications, the benefit-risk ratio is favorable for treating bothersome hot flashes and for preventing bone loss.
- For women who start HT more than 10 years after menopause or after age 60, the benefit-risk ratio appears less favorable because of greater absolute risks of coronary heart disease, stroke, venous thromboembolism (blood clots), and dementia.

The statement stresses that risks differ by type, dose, duration, route (pill, patch, gel, vaginal), timing of initiation, and whether a progestogen is used. It notes observational data suggesting lower clot and stroke risk with transdermal estradiol and lower doses. Treatment should be individualized and periodically reevaluated through shared decision-making with a clinician, who decides whether HT is appropriate and which product to use.
