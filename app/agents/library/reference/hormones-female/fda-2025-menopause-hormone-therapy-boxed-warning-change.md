Since 2003, US labels for estrogen and progestogen menopause products (hormone therapy, HT, HRT) carried a boxed warning ("black box") based on the Women's Health Initiative.

On November 10, 2025, the FDA and HHS announced they would remove most of that boxed warning. The FDA asked makers of all estrogen- and progestogen-containing menopause products to:

- remove references to cardiovascular disease, breast cancer and probable dementia from the boxed warning,
- remove the advice to use the lowest effective dose for the shortest duration, and
- add that starting therapy can be considered for moderate to severe hot flashes in women younger than 60 or within 10 years of menopause, with added WHI data for women aged 50 to 59.

What stayed: the boxed warning about endometrial (uterine) cancer remains for systemic estrogen-alone products, and for systemic products, cardiovascular and breast cancer risk information stays in the Warnings and Precautions section. The probable dementia warning was removed from labeling. The boxed warning changes also apply to vaginal products. On February 12, 2026, the FDA approved updated labels for the first six products, spanning all four product categories; 29 companies had submitted proposed changes.

The label change aligns with the Menopause Society's 2022 position that benefits outweigh risks for most healthy symptomatic women under 60 or within 10 years of menopause. It does not make hormone therapy right for everyone; individual risks are still assessed by the prescribing clinician.
