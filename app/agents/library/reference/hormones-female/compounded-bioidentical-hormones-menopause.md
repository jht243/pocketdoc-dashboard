"Bioidentical" means a hormone chemically identical to what the body makes, such as estradiol and progesterone. The 2022 Menopause Society hormone therapy position statement separates two kinds:

- FDA-approved bioidentical hormones, such as estradiol patches, gels and pills and micronized progesterone, which are regulated for purity, potency and labeling.
- Compounded bioidentical hormone therapy (cBHT), mixed by a compounding pharmacy from a prescription, sometimes combining several hormones in custom creams, pellets or troches.

The statement finds insufficient evidence to support the overall clinical use of compounded bioidentical hormones for menopause symptoms. Concerns it raises include minimal government regulation and monitoring, risks of overdosing or underdosing, impurities or lack of sterility, limited efficacy and safety data, and no package label describing risks. It also says saliva and urine hormone tests used to guide custom compounding are unreliable and not recommended (Level II/III), and that patient preference alone should not justify compounded therapy when regulated bioidentical products are available.

The statement does allow compounded products in limited situations, for example an allergy to an ingredient in approved products or a formulation not available in approved products, with the reason documented by the prescriber. Choice of product rests with the prescribing clinician.
