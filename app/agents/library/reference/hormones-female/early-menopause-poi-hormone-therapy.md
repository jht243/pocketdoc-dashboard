The 2022 Menopause Society hormone therapy position statement defines premature menopause as menopause before age 40 and early menopause as menopause between ages 40 and 45. Primary ovarian insufficiency (POI, sometimes called premature ovarian failure) is loss of ovarian function before 40, where periods and hormone production can still return on and off.

The statement explains that women who lose ovarian function early face a longer time without estrogen. Meta-analyses link early loss of ovarian function to lower quality of life and higher risk of fracture, cardiovascular disease, heart failure, diabetes, and overall mortality, as well as bone loss, mood and cognitive changes, and genitourinary symptoms.

For women with POI or premature or early menopause, including after surgical removal of both ovaries, the statement recommends hormone therapy at least until the average age of menopause, about 52, unless there is a contraindication (Level II). Combined oral contraceptives are an option in healthy younger women. Women with a uterus need endometrial protection. At about 52, treatment decisions are reevaluated.

The statement also says the Women's Health Initiative results in older women do not apply to women with POI or premature or early menopause (Level II). Care is individualized by the treating clinician.
