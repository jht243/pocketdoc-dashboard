The 2023 Nonhormone Therapy Position Statement of The North American Menopause Society reviewed options for hot flashes and night sweats (vasomotor symptoms, VMS) for women who cannot or prefer not to use hormone therapy, for example because of breast cancer history or cardiovascular disease.

Recommended, with good and consistent evidence (Level I):

- Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT)
- Clinical hypnosis
- SSRIs and SNRIs (certain antidepressant medicines)
- Gabapentin
- Fezolinetant (Veozah), a neurokinin 3 receptor antagonist

Also recommended, with more limited evidence:

- Oxybutynin (Levels I-II)
- Weight loss (Levels II-III)
- Stellate ganglion block, a nerve block procedure (Levels II-III)

The statement concludes that hormone therapy remains the most effective treatment for hot flashes and should be considered for women within 10 years of their final period, and that clinicians should know the evidence-based nonhormone options for women who are not candidates. It also noted that some SSRIs did not show benefit and are not recommended for this purpose, and that paroxetine and fluoxetine can interfere with tamoxifen activation through the CYP2D6 enzyme. Whether a prescription option fits, and which one, is a clinician decision.
