Genitourinary syndrome of menopause (GSM, formerly vulvovaginal atrophy or atrophic vaginitis) covers vaginal dryness, burning, irritation, pain with sex (dyspareunia), and urinary urgency or repeated urinary tract infections after menopause.

The 2022 Menopause Society hormone therapy position statement recommends, for bothersome GSM symptoms not relieved by over-the-counter options (vaginal moisturizers and lubricants), and in women who do not need systemic hormone therapy, low-dose vaginal estrogen or other prescription options such as vaginal DHEA (prasterone) or oral ospemifene.

Other points from the statement:

- Low-dose vaginal estrogen products (creams, tablets, inserts, rings) result in minimal absorption into the bloodstream.
- A progestogen is not required with low-dose vaginal estrogen, although trial data beyond one year are limited (Level II).
- Low-dose vaginal estrogen improves sexual function in women with GSM (Level I) and may help urinary symptoms, including prevention of recurrent UTIs (Level II).
- Vaginal therapy may be used at any age and for an extended duration if needed.

For women with a history of breast cancer, the statement advises that low-dose vaginal estrogen be prescribed in consultation with their oncologist (Level III). The prescriber chooses the product and schedule.
