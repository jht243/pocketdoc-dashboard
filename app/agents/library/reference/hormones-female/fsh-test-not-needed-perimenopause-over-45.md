---
title: FSH blood tests are not needed to diagnose perimenopause after 45
source: NICE Guideline NG23 Menopause - identification and management
evidence: Strong
approved_by: pending
as_of: 2026-04
url: https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng23/chapter/recommendations
---

FSH (follicle stimulating hormone) is often ordered to "check for menopause", but in the years leading up to menopause it swings widely from cycle to cycle, so one result can look normal one month and high the next.

The UK NICE menopause guideline (NG23) advises that, in otherwise healthy women over 45 with menopausal symptoms, perimenopause can be diagnosed from hot flashes and irregular periods, and menopause from 12 months without a period (when not using hormonal contraception), without laboratory tests. It advises against using FSH, AMH (anti-Müllerian hormone), inhibin, estradiol, antral follicle count or ovarian volume to diagnose perimenopause or menopause in women over 45. NICE's reason is that FSH fluctuates considerably over short periods during the menopause transition.

NICE does support considering an FSH test in women aged 40 to 45 with menopausal symptoms, including changes in periods, and in women under 40 when menopause or primary ovarian insufficiency is suspected. Other tests, such as thyroid function or a blood count, may still be needed if symptoms could have another cause. Diagnosis is harder in women taking hormonal treatments that affect periods.
