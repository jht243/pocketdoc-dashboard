Testosterone for women (female testosterone therapy, low T in women, testosterone cream or pellets) is widely marketed for energy, mood, weight, muscle and libido. The 2019 Global Consensus Position Statement on testosterone therapy for women, endorsed by multiple international societies including The Menopause Society and the Endocrine Society, concluded:

- The only evidence-based use is treating postmenopausal women with hypoactive sexual desire disorder (HSDD, distressing low sexual desire), after a full biopsychosocial assessment.
- A blood testosterone level should not be used to diagnose HSDD.
- Data show no effect on general wellbeing, and evidence is insufficient for cognition, bone density, or heart outcomes. There are insufficient data to recommend testosterone for premenopausal women.
- Oral testosterone is not recommended because of adverse effects on cholesterol.
- Compounded testosterone is not recommended, nor any product, such as pellets or injections, that produces higher-than-physiologic levels. Treatment should keep blood testosterone within the normal premenopausal female range, with a level checked at baseline, after starting, and periodically to screen for overuse; treatment is stopped if there is no benefit by 6 months.
- At physiologic levels, mild increases in acne and body or facial hair occurred in some women. Long-term safety data beyond 24 months are lacking.

There is no FDA-approved testosterone product for women in the US. Whether any treatment fits is a clinician decision.
