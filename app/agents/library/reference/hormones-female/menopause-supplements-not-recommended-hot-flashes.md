The 2023 Menopause Society nonhormone therapy position statement lists several popular options as not recommended for treating hot flashes and night sweats (vasomotor symptoms), because evidence of benefit is lacking, mixed or limited.

Supplements and herbal remedies (Levels I-II), including:

- Black cohosh (Level I, insufficient evidence; the statement also notes reports of possible liver harm)
- Vitamin E (Level I)
- Soy foods, soy extracts and the soy metabolite equol (Level II)
- Evening primrose oil, dong quai, ginseng, maca, chasteberry (vitex), wild yam creams, omega-3s
- Cannabinoids (Level II)

Other approaches not recommended specifically for hot flashes: paced breathing (Level I); cooling techniques, avoiding triggers, exercise, yoga, mindfulness, relaxation, acupuncture, and suvorexant (Level II); chiropractic and clonidine (Levels I-III); and dietary modification and pregabalin (Level III).

"Not recommended for hot flashes" does not mean harmful in general. Exercise, yoga and a healthy diet have other well-established health benefits; they just did not reliably reduce hot flashes in trials. Supplements are not FDA-reviewed for effectiveness, and some interact with medications, so they are worth mentioning to a clinician or pharmacist.
