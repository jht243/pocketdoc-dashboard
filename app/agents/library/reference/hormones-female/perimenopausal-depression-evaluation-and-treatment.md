Low mood, irritability and depression in the years around menopause are common questions. The 2018 Guidelines for the Evaluation and Treatment of Perimenopausal Depression, developed by The North American Menopause Society (now The Menopause Society) and the National Network of Depression Centers, describe perimenopause, meaning the early and late menopause transition and early postmenopause, as a window of vulnerability for both depressive symptoms and major depressive episodes.

Key conclusions:

- Most midlife women who have a major depressive episode during perimenopause have had depression before.
- Midlife depression often appears together with hot flashes, night sweats and sleep problems, plus the life stresses common in midlife. These overlap and can mask each other.
- Evaluation includes identifying menopause stage, assessing both mood and menopause symptoms, considering other diagnoses, and using validated depression screening questionnaires.
- Proven depression treatments, antidepressants and psychotherapy, are the front-line treatments for perimenopausal depression.
- Estrogen therapy is not approved to treat depression, but there is evidence it has antidepressant effects in perimenopausal women, particularly those who also have hot flashes. Data on estrogen plus progestin are sparse and inconclusive.

Labelled Moderate because the evidence on hormone therapy for mood is limited. Thoughts of self-harm need urgent help; in the US, call or text 988. Treatment choices belong to the clinician.
