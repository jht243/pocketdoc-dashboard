PCOS (polycystic ovary syndrome) affects more than periods and fertility. The 2023 International Evidence-based PCOS Guideline sets out health checks for people with PCOS:

- Blood sugar: women with PCOS have higher risk of prediabetes and type 2 diabetes regardless of age and BMI. Glycemic status should be assessed at diagnosis and reassessed every 1 to 3 years depending on other risk factors. The guideline names the oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT) as the most accurate test in PCOS; fasting glucose or hemoglobin A1c can be used if an OGTT is not possible, with lower accuracy.
- Insulin tests: insulin resistance is part of PCOS, but the guideline says clinically available insulin assays are of limited relevance and are not recommended in routine care.
- Heart: PCOS carries increased cardiovascular risk factors. All women with PCOS should have a lipid panel (cholesterol, LDL, HDL, triglycerides) at diagnosis, and blood pressure checked every year and when planning pregnancy.
- Sleep apnea: obstructive sleep apnea is more common in PCOS independent of weight; symptoms such as snoring and daytime sleepiness should prompt screening.
- Mood: depression and anxiety are common, and the guideline recommends screening all adults with PCOS.
- Uterus: premenopausal women with PCOS have a higher risk of endometrial hyperplasia and cancer, though the overall chance is low and routine screening is not recommended.

The treating clinician decides which tests are due.
