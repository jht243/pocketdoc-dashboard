A DXA scan (DEXA, bone density scan) reports bone mineral density as a T-score, the number of standard deviations above or below the average for a healthy young adult. The Bone Health and Osteoporosis Foundation (BHOF) 2022 clinician's guide uses the World Health Organization categories:

- Normal: T-score of -1.0 or higher.
- Low bone mass (osteopenia): T-score between -1.0 and -2.5.
- Osteoporosis: T-score of -2.5 or lower.

A Z-score compares bone density with people of the same age and sex instead.

The BHOF guide says clinicians should consider medication to prevent fractures in postmenopausal women and men 50 and older who have:
- A T-score of -2.5 or lower at the hip, femoral neck or spine.
- Osteopenia at the hip with a 10-year FRAX risk of 3% or more for hip fracture, or 20% or more for a major osteoporotic fracture.
- A hip or spine fracture, regardless of bone density, or a fracture of the upper arm, pelvis or wrist with osteopenia.

The guide notes that about half of white women aged 50 and older in the US will have an osteoporotic fracture in their remaining lifetime, and that most fractures happen in people whose T-score is better than -2.5. Screening recommendations are covered separately by the USPSTF. Treatment choices belong to the clinician.
