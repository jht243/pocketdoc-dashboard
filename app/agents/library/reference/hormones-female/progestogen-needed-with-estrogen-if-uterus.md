title: Women with a uterus using systemic estrogen need a progestogen

Estrogen taken alone (unopposed estrogen) stimulates the lining of the uterus (endometrium). The 2022 Menopause Society hormone therapy position statement explains that unopposed systemic estrogen in a postmenopausal woman with an intact uterus increases the risk of endometrial hyperplasia and endometrial cancer, that the risk rises with higher estrogen exposure and longer use, and that it can persist after stopping.

For that reason, the statement recommends that women with a uterus who use systemic estrogen (pills, patches, gels, sprays) also receive adequate progestogen, which can be a progestin or progesterone (Level I). The one exception it names is conjugated estrogens combined with bazedoxifene, a tissue-selective combination that protects the endometrium without a progestogen. Women who have had a hysterectomy generally do not need a progestogen with estrogen.

This is different for low-dose vaginal estrogen: the statement says a progestogen is not required, though trial data beyond one year are limited.

The FDA's November 2025 labeling action kept a boxed warning about endometrial cancer for systemic estrogen-alone products for this same reason. Unexpected vaginal bleeding while on hormone therapy is a reason to contact the prescribing clinician. Which progestogen and schedule to use is a prescriber decision.
