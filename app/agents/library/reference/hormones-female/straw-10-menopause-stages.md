STRAW+10 (Stages of Reproductive Aging Workshop + 10, published 2012) is the standard framework clinicians and researchers use to describe where a woman is in the menopause transition (perimenopause). Its main markers are changes in menstrual bleeding, with hormone levels as supporting markers.

- Early menopausal transition (stage -2): a persistent difference of 7 days or more in the length of consecutive cycles. Persistent means it happens again within 10 cycles.
- Late menopausal transition (stage -1): a gap of 60 days or more without a period. STRAW+10 describes FSH above about 25 IU/L on a random blood draw as characteristic of this stage, while noting that cut-offs depend on the lab assay.
- Final menstrual period (stage 0, menopause): confirmed only in hindsight, after 12 months with no bleeding.
- Postmenopause follows.

STRAW+10 states the system applies regardless of age, ethnicity, body size or lifestyle. It notes the model does not fit well for women who have had a hysterectomy or endometrial ablation, women with PCOS, hypothalamic amenorrhea, chemotherapy or premature ovarian insufficiency, among others. Bleeding patterns are the anchor because hormone levels fluctuate. This passage is labelled Moderate because STRAW+10 is a consensus staging system rather than a treatment guideline.
