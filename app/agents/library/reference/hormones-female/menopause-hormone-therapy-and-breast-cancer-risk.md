Breast cancer is the most common worry about hormone therapy (HT, HRT, menopausal hormone therapy). The 2022 Menopause Society hormone therapy position statement summarizes the Women's Health Initiative (WHI) trials, whose results differ by regimen:

- Estrogen plus progestin (conjugated estrogens with medroxyprogesterone acetate, for women with a uterus): about 9 additional breast cancer cases per 10,000 person-years of use. The statement puts the attributable risk at less than one additional case per 1,000 users per year, describes it as rare, and compares it to the risk linked with one to two daily glasses of wine, obesity or low physical activity.
- Estrogen alone (for women without a uterus): about 7 fewer invasive breast cancers per 10,000 person-years, not statistically significant during the trial, with a significant reduction after about 20 years of follow-up.

Other points from the statement:
- Observational data suggest risk rises with longer duration of use.
- Vaginal estrogen does not appear to affect breast cancer risk; oral and transdermal estrogen appear similar.
- HT does not appear to further raise relative risk in women with a family history of breast cancer, but the absolute number of cases is higher in women with higher baseline risk.
- Systemic HT after breast cancer is generally not advised.

In 2025 the FDA removed breast cancer from the boxed warning on these products, but the risk information remains in the label. Individual risk is reviewed with the prescribing clinician.
