A common question is whether hormone therapy (HT, HRT) must be stopped at a certain age, such as 60 or 65. The 2022 Menopause Society hormone therapy position statement says no arbitrary age-based stopping rule is clinically appropriate.

The statement's reasoning:

- Frequent hot flashes last on average about 7.4 years, and for many women more than 10 years; some women still have them decades after menopause.
- Risk increases with advancing age and longer duration, so women are advised to use the appropriate dose for the time needed to control symptoms.
- Longer use should be for documented reasons, such as persistent hot flashes or bone protection, with shared decision-making and periodic reevaluation.
- The longest blinded trial, the Women's Health Initiative, lasted only 5 to 7 years, so data on very long use are limited.
- Lowering risk through choice of dose, formulation and route, such as transdermal estradiol, becomes more important with age.

This passage is labelled Moderate because the statement acknowledges that questions about long-duration use remain unanswered by trial data. The decision to continue, taper or stop belongs to the woman and her clinician, based on her symptoms and personal risks for heart disease, stroke, blood clots, breast cancer and osteoporosis.
