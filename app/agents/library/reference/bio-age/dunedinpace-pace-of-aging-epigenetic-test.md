DunedinPACE (Pace of Aging Calculated from the Epigenome) is a DNA methylation blood test that estimates how fast a person is aging right now, rather than giving a biological age in years. Belsky and colleagues (eLife, 2022) built it from the Dunedin Study, a New Zealand birth cohort of 1,037 people born in 1972 to 1973, whose decline across 19 measures of organ function was tracked at ages 26, 32, 38 and 45.

A value of 1.0 equals the cohort average of about one year of biological aging per calendar year. Values above 1 suggest faster aging and values below 1 slower aging. In validation datasets the measure had high test-retest reliability and was associated with later disease, disability and death, with effect sizes similar to the GrimAge clock.

The authors note limitations: it was developed in one modest-sized cohort from one country, validation samples were mostly of white European ancestry, and it has not yet been shown to work as a surrogate endpoint that responds to interventions in a way that predicts healthspan. It is presented as a research tool for studies and trials. Evidence is Emerging. DunedinPACE is a different measure from Thumbprint's blood-test PhenoAge, so the two numbers cannot be compared directly. Keywords: DunedinPACE, pace of aging test, epigenetic clock, DNA methylation test, how fast am I aging.
