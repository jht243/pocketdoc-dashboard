The American Heart Association PREVENT equations (Predicting Risk of cardiovascular disease EVENTs; Khan et al., Circulation, 2024) estimate the chance of a first cardiovascular event in adults aged 30 to 79 without known cardiovascular disease. They give 10-year risk for ages 30 to 79 and 30-year risk for ages 30 to 59, with separate estimates for total cardiovascular disease (CVD), atherosclerotic disease (ASCVD: heart attack and stroke) and heart failure.

Required inputs: sex, age, total cholesterol, HDL cholesterol, systolic blood pressure, BMI, eGFR (kidney function, calculated with the race-free 2021 CKD-EPI equation), diabetes, current smoking, and whether the person takes blood pressure or cholesterol-lowering medication. Optional inputs that can refine the estimate: hemoglobin A1c, urine albumin-creatinine ratio (UACR), and a zip-code-based social deprivation index.

PREVENT does not use race as a predictor. It was developed in more than 3 million adults and corrected the roughly twofold overestimation seen with the older Pooled Cohort Equations, so its percentages are usually lower. The 2025 blood pressure and 2026 cholesterol guidelines both use PREVENT. Keywords: heart risk calculator, 10-year risk, 30-year risk, cardiovascular risk score, PREVENT calculator.
