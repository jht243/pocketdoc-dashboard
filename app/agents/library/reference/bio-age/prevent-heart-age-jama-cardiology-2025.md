Heart age, also called cardiovascular risk age, restates a 10-year cardiovascular risk percentage as an age. A Northwestern University team led by Sadiya Khan published a method in JAMA Cardiology (July 2025) that converts the AHA PREVENT 10-year risk into a heart age, and released a free PREVENT risk age calculator.

Applied to more than 14,000 US adults aged 30 to 79 without prior heart disease (NHANES 2011 to 2020), average heart age was older than actual age: about 55.4 versus 51.3 years in women and 56.7 versus 49.7 years in men. Gaps were larger in groups with lower education and in Black and Hispanic adults.

Heart age uses the same inputs as PREVENT, so blood pressure, total and HDL cholesterol, smoking, diabetes, BMI and kidney function all affect it. The authors note the tool does not replace clinical care, and that it has not yet been shown whether presenting risk as an age improves motivation, treatment adherence or outcomes. Heart age is a communication tool layered on PREVENT, not a separate measurement. Keywords: heart age, how old is my heart, cardiovascular age, risk age.
