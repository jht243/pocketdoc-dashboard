Grip strength, measured by squeezing a handheld dynamometer, is a simple marker of overall muscle strength and is often discussed as a sign of biological aging.

The PURE study (Leong et al., The Lancet, 2015) measured grip strength in 139,691 adults aged 35 to 70 in 17 countries of differing income levels and followed them for a median of 4 years. After adjustment for other factors, each 5 kg lower grip strength was associated with:

- about 16% higher risk of death from any cause (hazard ratio 1.16)
- about 17% higher risk of cardiovascular death (hazard ratio 1.17)
- about 7% higher risk of heart attack and 9% higher risk of stroke

Grip strength predicted death from any cause and from cardiovascular disease more strongly than systolic blood pressure did. It was not significantly linked with new diabetes, falls injuries or fractures in this study.

The authors described grip strength as an inexpensive way to stratify risk and said further research is needed to test whether improving strength lowers mortality. Because the findings are observational, the evidence is labelled Moderate. Grip strength is not part of the PhenoAge biological age formula. Keywords: grip strength, hand grip test, muscle strength and longevity, dynamometer, strength and lifespan.
