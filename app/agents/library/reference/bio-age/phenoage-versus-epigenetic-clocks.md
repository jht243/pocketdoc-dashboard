The 2018 Levine paper in Aging describes two related measures, and they are often confused. The first, Phenotypic Age (PhenoAge), is calculated from routine clinical blood tests: nine markers from a metabolic panel, a CBC with differential and hs-CRP, plus chronological age. Thumbprint's biological age uses this blood-test version.

The second, DNAm PhenoAge, is an epigenetic clock. The researchers built it by finding a set of DNA methylation sites in blood cells, 513 CpG sites, whose pattern predicts the blood-test Phenotypic Age. It requires a specialized methylation test rather than standard labs.

The two measures are correlated but not identical, and a commercial epigenetic age test will not give the same number as the blood-test PhenoAge. Neither is a diagnosis. Both were built to capture risk of death and age-related disease in populations, and individual results carry measurement uncertainty. Keywords: epigenetic age test, DNA methylation age, biological age test kit, epigenetic clock versus blood test.
