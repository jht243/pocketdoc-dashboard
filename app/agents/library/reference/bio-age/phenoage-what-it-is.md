Phenotypic Age, or PhenoAge, is the published model behind Thumbprint's biological age score. Levine and colleagues (Aging, 2018) built it from US national survey data: the model was trained on NHANES III and validated on NHANES IV. Starting from 42 candidate blood measures, a penalized mortality model kept 9 routine blood markers plus chronological age. The formula turns those inputs into an estimated 10-year mortality risk, then expresses that risk as the age at which an average person carries the same risk.

What it is: a research-validated summary of how a set of routine labs compares with population risk patterns. In validation, a PhenoAge higher than chronological age was associated with higher risk of death and age-related disease, and PhenoAge correlated strongly with chronological age.

What it is not: a diagnosis, a measure of any one organ, or a direct measure of how fast a person is aging at the cellular level. It reflects associations in large populations, so an individual's number is an estimate. It is also not an epigenetic or DNA methylation test. Keywords: biological age, bio age, PhenoAge, phenotypic age, how old is my body, Levine.
