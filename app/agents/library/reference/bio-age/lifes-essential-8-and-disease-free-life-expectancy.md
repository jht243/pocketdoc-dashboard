The American Heart Association's Life's Essential 8 (LE8) score rates diet, physical activity, nicotine exposure, sleep, BMI, non-HDL cholesterol, blood glucose and blood pressure from 0 to 100. It is often used as a healthspan measure because higher scores track with more years lived without major disease.

Wang and colleagues (JAMA Internal Medicine, 2023) followed 135,199 UK Biobank adults who started free of major chronic disease. They grouped people as low (under 50), moderate (50 to 79) or high (80 or higher) cardiovascular health and estimated life expectancy free of cardiovascular disease, diabetes, cancer and dementia:

- Men at age 50: about 21.5, 25.5 and 28.4 disease-free years for low, moderate and high scores, so high scorers gained about 6.9 years over low scorers.
- Women at age 50: about 24.2, 30.5 and 33.6 years, a gain of about 9.4 years.
- Among people with high scores, disease-free life expectancy did not differ significantly by socioeconomic status.

This was an observational study in a mostly white UK population, so it shows association, not proof that raising a score adds a set number of years. Evidence is labelled Moderate for that reason. Keywords: healthspan, disease-free years, Life's Essential 8 longevity, heart health score and lifespan.
