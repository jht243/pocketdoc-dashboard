The PhenoAge biological age measure was developed in NHANES III and then tested in a separate national sample. Liu and colleagues, with Levine as senior author (PLOS Medicine, 2018), evaluated it in 11,432 adults aged 20 to 84 from NHANES IV (1999 to 2010), with about 12.6 years of mortality follow-up. PhenoAge was highly predictive of death even after accounting for chronological age. The study examined whether it separated risk across healthy and unhealthy people, different age groups, and groups with differing race and ethnicity, socioeconomic status and health behaviors.

Because the measure was validated in adults roughly 20 to 84, results outside that age range are extrapolations. The validation was observational: it shows that higher PhenoAge is associated with higher risk, not that lowering one marker will add a matching number of years of life. The same paper's findings come from US population data, so performance in other populations or in people with acute illness is less certain. Keywords: biological age accuracy, is biological age reliable, PhenoAge validation, NHANES.
