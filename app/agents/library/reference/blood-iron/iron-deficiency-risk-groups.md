The NIH Office of Dietary Supplements iron fact sheet identifies groups more likely to have low iron (iron deficiency, low ferritin, iron deficiency anemia):

- Pregnant women, because plasma volume and red cell mass expand to supply the fetus and placenta. Iron deficiency in pregnancy raises the risk of premature birth and low birthweight.
- Infants and young children, especially those born preterm or at low birthweight, or whose mothers were iron deficient.
- Women with heavy menstrual bleeding (menorrhagia). The ODS cites evidence that menorrhagia may account for roughly 33% to 41% of iron deficiency anemia in women of reproductive age.
- Frequent blood donors.
- People with cancer, gastrointestinal disorders that reduce absorption or cause blood loss, and heart failure.
- Vegetarians and vegans, because nonheme iron from plants is less bioavailable.

Hemoglobin and hematocrit are the most commonly used screening tests but are neither sensitive nor specific; ferritin reflects iron stores but can be raised by inflammation. Clinicians often combine several measures. Unexplained iron deficiency in men and postmenopausal women prompts evaluation for gastrointestinal blood loss under AGA guidance.
