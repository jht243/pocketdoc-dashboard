The NIH Office of Dietary Supplements iron fact sheet (updated September 2025) names the groups more likely than others to have inadequate iron (low iron, iron deficiency, low ferritin, iron deficiency anemia):

- Pregnant women
- Infants and young children
- Women with heavy menstrual bleeding (menorrhagia, heavy periods)
- Frequent blood donors
- People with certain conditions, such as cancer, gastrointestinal disorders, and heart failure

The same fact sheet notes that people who follow vegetarian diets need more iron than those who eat animal products, because nonheme iron from plant foods is less bioavailable.

Hemoglobin and hematocrit are the most commonly used screening tests but are neither sensitive nor specific; serum ferritin reflects iron stores but can be raised by inflammation, so clinicians often combine several measures. Under the 2020 American Gastroenterological Association guideline, unexplained iron deficiency anemia in men and postmenopausal women prompts evaluation for a gastrointestinal source of blood loss.
