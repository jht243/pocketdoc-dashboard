Hereditary hemochromatosis (iron overload, HFE gene disorder) is screened with transferrin saturation (TSAT, TS, iron saturation) and serum ferritin. The 2019 ACG Clinical Guideline on hereditary hemochromatosis describes this sequence:

- Transferrin saturation is the preferred initial screening test, and fasting is not required. A TSAT above 45% identifies nearly all C282Y homozygotes.
- If TSAT is below 45% and ferritin is normal, further evaluation is not needed. ACG defines normal ferritin here as below 200 ng/mL in premenopausal women and below 300 ng/mL in men and postmenopausal women; together with a TSAT below 45%, this has a 97% negative predictive value for excluding iron overload.
- If TSAT is 45% or higher and ferritin is elevated, HFE gene testing should be performed. The separate 2017 ACG liver chemistries guideline also recommends HFE testing when TSAT is 45% or higher and/or ferritin is elevated in people with abnormal liver tests.

Ferritin alone is not specific for iron overload, since alcohol-related liver disease, hepatitis C, fatty liver disease and cancer also raise it. Iron overload can also occur with high ferritin and normal TSAT in non-HFE conditions.
