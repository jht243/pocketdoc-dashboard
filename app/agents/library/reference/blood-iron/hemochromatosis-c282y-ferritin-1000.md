The 2019 ACG Clinical Guideline on hereditary hemochromatosis (HH, HFE iron overload) covers who to test and what the numbers mean:

- General population screening for hemochromatosis is not indicated, because C282Y frequency varies by ancestry and penetrance is incomplete; many C282Y homozygotes never develop iron overload disease.
- Family members, particularly first-degree relatives of someone diagnosed with HH, should be screened (strong recommendation).
- The genotypes most clearly linked to HH are C282Y homozygosity and, less often, C282Y/H63D compound heterozygosity. In people with iron overload who do not carry these, ACG advises looking for other causes, including liver and blood disorders.
- Ferritin of 1,000 ng/mL is a key threshold. In C282Y homozygotes, ferritin above 1,000 ng/mL together with elevated liver enzymes and a low platelet count predicts cirrhosis in more than 80% of cases, and ACG suggests liver biopsy or fibrosis staging at that level. Those with ferritin below 1,000 ng/mL at diagnosis are unlikely to have advanced fibrosis.

ACG recommends phlebotomy (therapeutic blood removal) as first-line treatment for diagnosed HH, managed by the treating clinician.
