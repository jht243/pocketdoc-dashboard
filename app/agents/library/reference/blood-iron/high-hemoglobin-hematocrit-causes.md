A high hematocrit (Hct, packed cell volume) or high hemoglobin (Hgb, Hb) on a CBC can mean either too many red blood cells (erythrocytosis, polycythemia) or too little plasma. MedlinePlus, from the US National Library of Medicine, lists:

- Low plasma volume: dehydration, which MedlinePlus calls the most common cause of a high hematocrit, and shock. A repeat test when well hydrated often settles this.
- More red cells made in response to low oxygen or other drivers: lung disease, congenital heart disease, heart failure, and living at high altitude. MedlinePlus notes that normal ranges also differ by altitude and by smoking status.
- Testosterone therapy stimulates red cell production. The Endocrine Society 2018 testosterone guideline calls for checking hematocrit on therapy and for evaluating low oxygen and sleep apnea when hematocrit is high; see the separate passage on hematocrit monitoring on testosterone for its 50% and 54% thresholds.
- Polycythemia vera, a bone marrow disorder usually linked to an acquired JAK2 V617F gene change, which MedlinePlus says must be distinguished from other causes of increased red blood cells.

A persistently high hemoglobin or hematocrit without an obvious reason such as dehydration, smoking, altitude or testosterone use is a reason for clinician review, which may include oxygen levels, a sleep apnea assessment, an erythropoietin (EPO) level or JAK2 testing.
