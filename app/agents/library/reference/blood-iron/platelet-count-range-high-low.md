Platelets (PLT, thrombocytes) are the blood cells that help clotting; the count is part of every CBC. The National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute (NHLBI) gives a normal adult platelet count of 150,000 to 450,000 per microliter, which lab reports usually show as 150 to 450 x10^3/µL.

Low platelets (thrombocytopenia) means a count below 150,000. NHLBI notes serious bleeding usually does not occur until the count is very low. Causes listed by NHLBI and MedlinePlus include viral infections, autoimmune disease, certain medicines (including heparin), heavy alcohol use, liver cirrhosis and an enlarged spleen, vitamin B12 deficiency, bone marrow problems and blood cancers, and pregnancy, where gestational thrombocytopenia is mild and usually resolves.

High platelets come in two kinds, per NHLBI:

- Thrombocytosis, also called secondary or reactive thrombocytosis, is caused by another condition and is the more common kind. Causes include iron deficiency anemia, infection or inflammation (such as inflammatory bowel disease or rheumatoid arthritis), recovery from blood loss, spleen removal, and some cancers, where a high count can be an early sign.
- Thrombocythemia, or essential thrombocythemia, is a bone marrow disorder with no other cause.

A mildly abnormal count is often rechecked and read alongside the rest of the CBC; a very low or very high count, or one with bleeding or clotting symptoms, warrants prompt clinician review.
