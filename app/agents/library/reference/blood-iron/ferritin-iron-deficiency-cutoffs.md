Serum ferritin (iron stores, stored iron test) reflects how much iron the body has in reserve, and a low ferritin is the most specific single blood sign of iron deficiency. The NIH Office of Dietary Supplements notes that ferritin can be affected by inflammation, so it is often read alongside other iron measures.

The 2020 American Gastroenterological Association guideline recommends a ferritin cut-off of 45 ng/mL rather than 15 ng/mL for diagnosing iron deficiency in adults with anemia. At 45 ng/mL sensitivity is about 85% and specificity about 92%; the older 15 ng/mL cut-off is highly specific but misses many cases. AGA notes the right threshold for people without anemia is uncertain.

Ferritin is less accurate when inflammation, chronic kidney disease or liver disease is present, because those conditions raise it. In that setting AGA suggests adding tests such as transferrin saturation and C-reactive protein. In men and postmenopausal women, AGA recommends GI evaluation for the cause of iron deficiency anemia. Lab report ranges for ferritin vary, and the lower limit printed on a report is often below the AGA cut-off.
