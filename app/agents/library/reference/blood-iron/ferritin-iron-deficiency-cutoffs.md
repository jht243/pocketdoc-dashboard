Serum ferritin (iron stores, stored iron test) is the single most useful blood test for iron deficiency. The NIH Office of Dietary Supplements calls it the most efficient and cost-effective test, because ferritin falls in the first stage of iron depletion, before anemia appears.

Cut-offs in common use:

- The 2020 American Gastroenterological Association guideline recommends a ferritin cut-off of 45 ng/mL rather than 15 ng/mL for diagnosing iron deficiency in adults with anemia. At 45 ng/mL sensitivity is about 85% and specificity about 92%; the older 15 ng/mL cut-off is highly specific but misses many cases. AGA notes the right threshold for people without anemia is uncertain.
- The NIH ODS fact sheet states a ferritin below 30 ng/mL suggests iron deficiency and below 10 ng/mL suggests iron deficiency anemia.

Ferritin is less accurate when inflammation, chronic kidney disease or liver disease is present, because those conditions raise it. In that setting AGA suggests adding tests such as transferrin saturation and C-reactive protein. In men and postmenopausal women, AGA recommends GI evaluation for the cause of iron deficiency anemia.
