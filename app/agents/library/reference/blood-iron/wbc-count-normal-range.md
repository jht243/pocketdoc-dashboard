The white blood cell count (WBC, white count, leukocytes) in adults is about 4.5 to 11.0 x10^3/µL, written as 4,500 to 11,000 per mm³, according to the American Family Physician review on leukocytosis. Ranges differ by lab, with age and during pregnancy.

Leukocytosis (high white count) is generally a WBC above 11.0 x10^3/µL in nonpregnant adults. Common, usually benign causes listed in the review:

- Infection, especially bacterial.
- Physical or emotional stress, including surgery, trauma, burns and intense exercise.
- Medications such as corticosteroids, lithium and beta agonists.
- Chronic inflammatory conditions, smoking and obesity.

The review lists findings that call for prompt evaluation: a WBC above 30.0 x10^3/µL, fever, night sweats, unintentional weight loss, easy bruising or bleeding, enlarged lymph nodes, liver or spleen, anemia or abnormal platelet counts alongside the high WBC, or immature cells on the blood smear. A WBC above 100.0 x10^3/µL is almost always due to leukemia or a myeloproliferative disorder. A count below the lab's reference range (leukopenia) also warrants clinician review.
