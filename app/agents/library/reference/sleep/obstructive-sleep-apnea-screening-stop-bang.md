Obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) is repeated narrowing or closing of the airway during sleep. Common signs are loud snoring, pauses in breathing or gasping that a bed partner notices, and daytime sleepiness.

STOP-Bang is a widely used eight-item yes/no screening questionnaire developed by Chung and colleagues: Snoring, Tiredness, Observed apnea, high blood Pressure, BMI over 35, Age over 50, Neck size large, and male Gender. A score of 3 or more is the usual cutoff for higher risk; lower scores point to a lower likelihood of OSA. It is a screening tool, not a diagnosis.

The American Academy of Sleep Medicine 2017 guideline makes this explicit. It recommends that clinical tools, questionnaires and prediction algorithms not be used to diagnose OSA in adults without a sleep study (strong recommendation). Diagnosis uses in-lab polysomnography or, for uncomplicated adults at increased risk of moderate to severe OSA, a technically adequate home sleep apnea test. If a home test is negative or inconclusive, the guideline recommends polysomnography.

Consumer wearables and blood-oxygen readings from rings or watches are not diagnostic tests for sleep apnea. Members ask about snoring, gasping at night, waking tired, morning headaches or low overnight oxygen; these signs belong with a clinician, who can decide on testing.
