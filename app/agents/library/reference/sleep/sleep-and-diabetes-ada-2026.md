The ADA Standards of Care in Diabetes, 2026 include a sleep health section. Recommendation 5.56 advises screening people with prediabetes or diabetes, and those at risk for diabetes, for sleep health, including sleep disorders and diabetes-related sleep disruption, with referral to sleep medicine or behavioral health as needed (grade B). Recommendation 5.57 advises counseling people with diabetes to practice sleep-promoting routines and habits (grade A).

The Standards explain why. Sleep disorders are a risk factor for developing type 2 diabetes. Obstructive sleep apnea, insomnia and restless legs syndrome are common in people with diabetes. In OSA, broken sleep and drops in oxygen activate the sympathetic nervous system, contributing to higher blood sugar, insulin resistance and related problems. A meta-analysis cited in the Standards found that CPAP therapy for sleep apnea lowered A1C by an average of 0.24%. Sleep disturbances are also linked with less engagement in diabetes self-care and with difficulty keeping glucose in range.

Members ask about this as poor sleep and blood sugar, high fasting glucose after bad nights, sleep apnea and diabetes, or insulin resistance and sleep. Whether a sleep problem needs testing or treatment is a question for the clinician.
