The American Geriatrics Society Beers Criteria list medicines that are usually best avoided in adults 65 and older. The 2023 update applies to outpatient, hospital and nursing-home care, except hospice and end-of-life care, and is meant to support shared decision-making rather than replace it.

Three groups of medicines used for sleep carry an avoid recommendation, each rated strong with moderate-quality evidence:
- Benzodiazepines, because they raise the risk of cognitive impairment, delirium, falls and fractures in older adults, and carry risks of misuse and dependence.
- The Z-drugs eszopiclone, zaleplon and zolpidem, because their adverse effects resemble benzodiazepines, including delirium, falls and fractures, while they offer only minimal improvement in time to fall asleep and sleep duration.
- First-generation antihistamines such as diphenhydramine and doxylamine, found in many over-the-counter PM and sleep products, because they are strongly anticholinergic, clear more slowly with age and lose their sleep effect as tolerance develops.

This fits the American College of Physicians 2016 guideline, which recommends CBT-I as the first treatment for chronic insomnia, with medication considered only when CBT-I alone has not worked.

Any change to a current sleep medicine is a decision for the prescriber; some of these medicines need a supervised taper. Members ask about sleeping pills, Ambien, Benadryl or Tylenol PM for sleep, melatonin alternatives, or sleep aids for parents and grandparents.
