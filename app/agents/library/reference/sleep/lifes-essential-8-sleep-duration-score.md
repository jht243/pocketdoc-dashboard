In 2022 the American Heart Association updated its cardiovascular health score from Life's Simple 7 to Life's Essential 8, adding sleep health as a new metric (Lloyd-Jones and colleagues, Circulation). The eight components are diet, physical activity, nicotine exposure, sleep, body mass index, blood lipids, blood glucose and blood pressure. Each is scored from 0 to 100, and the overall score is their simple average.

Sleep is scored by average hours of sleep per night in adults:
- 7 to under 9 hours: 100 points
- 9 to under 10 hours: 90 points
- 6 to under 7 hours: 70 points
- 5 to under 6 hours, or 10 hours or more: 40 points
- 4 to under 5 hours: 20 points
- under 4 hours: 0 points

The scoring follows an inverse-U pattern: both short and very long sleep score lower. The advisory notes that wearable data can be used where available. It cites links between poor sleep and higher mortality and adds that sleep predicts cardiovascular events beyond the original seven metrics, but it acknowledges that evidence that improving sleep lowers heart disease rates is still limited, so this passage is graded Moderate.

This fits the AASM and Sleep Research Society position that adults need 7 or more hours a night. Members ask about sleep and heart health, sleep and blood pressure, cardiovascular health score, or how much sleep is too much.
