The Physical Activity Guidelines for Americans, 2nd edition (HHS, 2018), summarizing the 2018 Advisory Committee's evidence review, state that adults who are more physically active sleep better. Greater amounts of moderate-to-vigorous physical activity are associated with:
- Falling asleep faster (shorter sleep latency).
- Higher sleep efficiency, meaning more of the time in bed is spent asleep.
- Better sleep quality and more deep sleep.
- Less daytime sleepiness and less frequent use of sleep-aid medicines.

The guidelines add that people with insomnia and with obstructive sleep apnea also report better sleep with regular activity, and that activity helps prevent excess weight gain, a major risk factor for sleep apnea. The guidelines also say a single session of moderate-to-vigorous activity can improve sleep that same day, with larger gains from regular activity.

On timing, the guidelines say the hour of exercise relative to bedtime does not matter for these benefits: results were similar for activity more than 8 hours before bed, 3 to 8 hours before, and less than 3 hours before bedtime.

Most of this evidence is observational or from short trials, so the passage is graded Moderate. CBT-I remains the first-line treatment for chronic insomnia. Members ask about exercise and sleep, working out at night, evening workouts, deep sleep, or sleeping better.
