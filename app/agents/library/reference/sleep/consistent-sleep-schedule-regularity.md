In 2023 the National Sleep Foundation published a consensus statement on sleep regularity, led by Sletten and colleagues in the journal Sleep Health. An expert panel of sleep and circadian scientists reviewed the research literature and voted through a modified Delphi process. The panel agreed that keeping regular, consistent sleep and wake times matters for physical and mental health and for cognitive and academic performance.

Irregular sleep schedules have been associated with obesity and diabetes, cardiovascular disease, immune dysfunction, cancer and poorer mental health. These are associations from observational research, not proof that irregular timing causes these conditions, which is why this passage is graded Moderate. For people who run short on sleep during the work week, the panel suggested that sleeping an extra 1 to 2 hours on days off can help most people recover sleep debt.

Keeping a steady wake time, including on weekends, is also a core part of stimulus control and sleep restriction in CBT-I for insomnia.

Members ask about this as a sleep schedule, bedtime routine, social jet lag, sleeping in on weekends, shift work, or a sleep consistency or regularity score on a wearable such as Oura, Whoop, Apple Watch or Fitbit.
