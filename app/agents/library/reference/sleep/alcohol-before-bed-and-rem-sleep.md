title: Alcohol before bed reduces and delays REM sleep
source: Gardiner et al. 2025 Sleep Medicine Reviews, The effect of alcohol on subsequent sleep in healthy adults, systematic review and meta-analysis
evidence: Moderate

Many people drink alcohol in the evening believing it helps them sleep. A systematic review and meta-analysis by Gardiner and colleagues (Sleep Medicine Reviews, 2025) pooled 27 laboratory studies of healthy adults to test how alcohol changes the night's sleep.

Key findings:
- Alcohol delayed the start of REM (rapid eye movement, dreaming) sleep and shortened the total amount of REM sleep.
- REM disruption appeared even at low amounts, roughly two standard drinks, and grew worse as the amount increased.
- Falling asleep faster, and reaching deep sleep (N3) sooner, appeared only after high amounts, roughly five standard drinks. The authors note this apparent benefit likely comes at the cost of even more REM disruption later in the night.
- Effects on total sleep time, sleep efficiency and time awake during the night could not be pinned down because results varied widely.

The studies were small laboratory experiments in healthy people, so the passage is graded Moderate. The AASM 2024 restless legs guideline also lists alcohol among the factors that worsen restless legs symptoms.

Alcohol is also a common reason for a short-lived rise in overnight resting heart rate or dip in HRV on a wearable. Wearables estimate sleep stages, so their REM minutes are approximate. Members ask about a nightcap, wine before bed, alcohol and sleep quality, or waking at 3 a.m. after drinking.
