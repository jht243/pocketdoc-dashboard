The American Academy of Sleep Medicine (AASM) and the Sleep Research Society published a joint consensus statement in 2015 recommending that adults sleep 7 or more hours per night on a regular basis. The CDC uses the same figure: at least 7 hours of sleep a day for adults.

The panel linked regularly sleeping less than 7 hours with weight gain and obesity, diabetes, high blood pressure, heart disease and stroke, depression, higher risk of death, weaker immune function, more pain, poorer performance, more errors and more accidents.

The statement set no firm upper limit. Sleeping more than 9 hours regularly may be appropriate for young adults, people recovering from sleep debt and people who are ill; for others, the panel said it is unclear whether long sleep carries risk.

The authors also stressed that duration is only one part of healthy sleep; timing, regularity, quality and the absence of sleep disorders matter too.

Members ask about this as how much sleep I need, sleep hours, short sleep, sleep debt, not sleeping enough, or sleep duration on a wearable. A wearable's total sleep estimate is approximate, so a trend over weeks says more than any single night.
