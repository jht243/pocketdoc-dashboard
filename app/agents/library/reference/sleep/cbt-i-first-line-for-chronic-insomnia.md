Cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia (CBT-I) is a structured, non-drug program that targets the thoughts and habits that keep insomnia going. It usually combines stimulus control (using the bed only for sleep and getting up when unable to sleep), sleep restriction (temporarily matching time in bed to actual sleep), relaxation methods, cognitive techniques, and sleep education. It can be delivered in person, in groups, or digitally.

The American College of Physicians 2016 guideline recommends that all adults with chronic insomnia disorder receive CBT-I as the initial treatment (strong recommendation, moderate-quality evidence). It recommends that clinicians consider adding medication, through shared decision-making, only when CBT-I alone has not worked (weak recommendation), noting that sleep medicines can carry serious adverse effects.

The American Academy of Sleep Medicine 2021 guideline gives multicomponent CBT-I its only strong recommendation. It conditionally supports brief behavioral therapy, stimulus control, sleep restriction therapy and relaxation therapy as single components, and it advises against sleep hygiene education used on its own as a treatment for chronic insomnia.

Members ask about this as insomnia, can't fall asleep, waking at 3 a.m., trouble staying asleep, sleep therapy, or alternatives to sleeping pills. Whether a sleep problem is chronic insomnia, and how to treat it, is for a clinician to assess.
