---
title: Caffeine taken 6 hours before bed can still disrupt sleep
source: Drake et al. 2013 Journal of Clinical Sleep Medicine, Caffeine effects on sleep taken 0, 3, or 6 hours before going to bed
evidence: Moderate
approved_by: Dr. Adam Locker, 2026-10-21
reviewed: 2026-10-21
as_of: 2013-11
url: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3805807/

A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover study by Drake and colleagues (Journal of Clinical Sleep Medicine, 2013) gave 12 healthy normal sleepers a large dose of caffeine at bedtime, 3 hours before bedtime, or 6 hours before bedtime, and compared each with a placebo night. Sleep was measured with a sleep diary and with an objective in-home sleep monitor.

Caffeine disrupted sleep at all three timings. Even when taken 6 hours before bed, it cut objectively measured total sleep time by more than an hour. At the 6-hour timing, participants' own sleep diaries did not pick up the loss, meaning people may not notice how much afternoon caffeine affects their sleep. The authors concluded the results support avoiding substantial caffeine for at least 6 hours before bedtime.

The study was small and used a single high dose, so it is graded Moderate. The authors also note that how quickly the body clears caffeine (its elimination half-life) varies widely between people, which is why published cutoff advice ranges from about 4 to 11 hours before bed.

Members ask about this as coffee and sleep, afternoon coffee, energy drinks, pre-workout, tea, or when to stop caffeine.
