A consumer DNA report usually lists each marker by gene, an rsID (a reference number such as rs429358 that names one position in the genome) and a genotype, the two letters read at that position, one inherited from each parent.

When the two letters match (for example CC) the person is homozygous; when they differ (CT) they are heterozygous. Reports often call one letter the "risk" or "effect" allele. One copy usually carries a smaller effect than two, and for many variants the effect is small either way.

Most trait and wellness markers come from genome-wide association studies, which compare large groups of people. They report how much more common a trait is among carriers, often an odds ratio of 1.1 to 1.3, meaning a few percent difference in average risk. That is a statement about populations, not a prediction for one person. Lifestyle, other genes and chance usually matter more.

A small number of variants have large, well-replicated effects, such as BRCA1/2, Lynch syndrome genes, familial hypercholesterolemia genes, HFE C282Y and Factor V Leiden. Those are the findings worth separating from the rest and bringing to a clinician.
