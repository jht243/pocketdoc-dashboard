The APOE gene comes in three common versions, e2, e3 and e4, read from two positions (rs429358 and rs7412). Everyone carries two copies. About a quarter of people carry one e4 copy and roughly 2 to 3 percent carry two.

One e4 copy is associated with a risk of late-onset Alzheimer disease roughly two to three times the average, and two copies with a larger increase. Many e4 carriers never develop Alzheimer disease, and many people who do develop it carry no e4. APOE e4 is also associated with higher LDL cholesterol, and APOE e2 with lower LDL.

The joint ACMG and National Society of Genetic Counselors guideline does not recommend APOE testing to predict Alzheimer disease in people without symptoms, because it neither rules the disease in nor out, and advises genetic counseling for anyone who learns their APOE status.

What carries over regardless of genotype: the cardiovascular risk factors linked to dementia risk, such as blood pressure, LDL and ApoB, blood sugar, physical activity, sleep and hearing, are measurable and worth tracking with a clinician. APOE e4 status is a reasonable thing to bring to that conversation.
