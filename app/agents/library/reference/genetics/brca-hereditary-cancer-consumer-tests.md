Harmful variants in BRCA1 and BRCA2 raise the lifetime risk of breast, ovarian, prostate and pancreatic cancer substantially. Lynch syndrome genes (MLH1, MSH2, MSH6, PMS2, EPCAM) do the same for colorectal and endometrial cancer.

In 2018 the FDA authorized 23andMe to report three BRCA variants that are most common in people of Ashkenazi Jewish ancestry. More than a thousand harmful BRCA variants are known, so a negative result on that report does not rule out a hereditary cancer risk, especially for people of other ancestries or with a strong family history.

The US Preventive Services Task Force (grade B) recommends that clinicians assess women with a personal or family history of breast, ovarian, tubal or peritoneal cancer, or ancestry linked to BRCA variants, with a brief familial risk tool, and refer those who screen positive to genetic counseling and, if indicated, testing.

Any reported hereditary cancer variant, and any family history that suggests one, belongs with a clinician and a genetic counselor, who can arrange clinical confirmation and discuss earlier or additional screening.
