The American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics keeps a list of genes where a harmful variant is considered medically actionable: knowing about it allows prevention, earlier screening or treatment that changes outcomes. Version 3.2 (2023) lists 81 genes.

The list covers hereditary cancer syndromes (for example BRCA1, BRCA2, the Lynch syndrome genes and APC), inherited heart conditions (cardiomyopathies, arrhythmia syndromes and aortic conditions), familial hypercholesterolemia (LDLR, APOB and PCSK9), malignant hyperthermia risk with anesthesia (RYR1 and CACNA1S), and a few metabolic conditions, including HFE hemochromatosis for people with two C282Y copies.

A variant in one of these genes on any report, consumer or clinical, belongs with a clinician and, usually, a genetic counselor, who can arrange clinical confirmation and decide what follows. Most other markers on consumer reports, such as trait, nutrition, fitness and longevity variants, sit outside this list and carry no recommended medical action on their own.
