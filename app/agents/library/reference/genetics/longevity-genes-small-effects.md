Some consumer reports include "longevity" or "healthy aging" markers. The variants most often reported are in FOXO3 and APOE. Certain FOXO3 variants have been found more often among people who live into their late 90s and beyond in several cohorts, first in Japanese-American men in Hawaii, and APOE e2 and e4 are the variants most consistently linked with lifespan, mostly through heart disease and dementia.

How much of lifespan is inherited is smaller than often claimed. A 2018 analysis of more than 400 million people in family trees estimated the heritability of lifespan at well under 10 percent once the tendency of partners to share similar lifestyles was accounted for.

Any single longevity variant shifts the odds only slightly, and none has a recommended action. What the evidence supports for healthy aging is the same with or without these variants: blood pressure, lipids, blood sugar, activity, sleep, not smoking and preventive screening. A longevity result is best read as interesting background rather than a plan.
