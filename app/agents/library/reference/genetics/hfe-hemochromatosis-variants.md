---
title: HFE variants - C282Y, H63D and iron overload risk
source: ACG Clinical Guideline, Hereditary Hemochromatosis, American Journal of Gastroenterology 2019
evidence: Strong
approved_by: pending
as_of: 2019
---

Hereditary hemochromatosis is the body absorbing more iron than it needs. Most cases in people of Northern European ancestry come from two copies of the HFE C282Y variant (rs1800562), found in roughly 1 in 200 to 300 people of that ancestry.

Carrying two C282Y copies raises the risk of iron overload, but penetrance is incomplete: many people with this genotype never build up harmful iron, and men are affected more often and earlier than women. One C282Y copy alone, one C282Y plus one H63D (rs1799945), or two H63D copies rarely lead to iron overload by themselves.

The American College of Gastroenterology guideline uses blood tests to show whether a variant is doing anything: transferrin saturation and serum ferritin. A raised transferrin saturation with raised ferritin in someone with two C282Y copies calls for clinical evaluation; normal iron studies are reassuring for now and are usually rechecked over time.

A C282Y result on a DNA report belongs with a clinician, alongside the member's latest ferritin and transferrin saturation, or a request for them if they are not on file.
