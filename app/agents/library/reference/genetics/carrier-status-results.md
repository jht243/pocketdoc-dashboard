A carrier status result means a person has one copy of a variant for a recessive condition, such as cystic fibrosis, sickle cell disease, Tay-Sachs disease or spinal muscular atrophy. Carriers usually have no symptoms themselves. The result matters mainly for family planning: if both biological parents carry a variant in the same gene, each pregnancy has a one in four chance of being affected.

Consumer tests read only selected variants in each gene, so a "not a carrier" result lowers but does not remove the chance of being a carrier. Clinical carrier screening panels test more of each gene.

The American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics (2021) recommends offering expanded carrier screening to everyone who is pregnant or planning a pregnancy, and the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists offers carrier screening as part of preconception and prenatal care.

A carrier result is relevant to anyone planning a pregnancy, for themselves and for a partner who could be tested, and belongs with a clinician or genetic counselor.
