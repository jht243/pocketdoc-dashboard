---
title: Factor V Leiden and prothrombin variants - inherited clotting risk

Factor V Leiden (F5, rs6025) and the prothrombin G20210A variant (F2, rs1799963) are the two most common inherited clotting-risk variants. Factor V Leiden is carried by about 3 to 8 percent of people of European ancestry; the prothrombin variant by about 2 percent.

One copy of Factor V Leiden raises the risk of a venous blood clot (deep vein thrombosis or pulmonary embolism) several-fold over the average; two copies raise it further. The absolute risk for a carrier with no other risk factors is still low in most years. Other factors add to it: estrogen-containing contraception or hormone therapy, pregnancy, surgery, long immobility, and a personal or family history of clots.

The American Society of Hematology 2023 guideline frames testing around decisions it would change, such as for people with a family history of clots who are considering estrogen-containing contraception or hormone therapy, or pregnancy.

A Factor V Leiden or prothrombin result belongs with a clinician, and especially with whoever prescribes any estrogen-containing contraception or hormone therapy. It is worth knowing before surgery or long-haul travel as well.
