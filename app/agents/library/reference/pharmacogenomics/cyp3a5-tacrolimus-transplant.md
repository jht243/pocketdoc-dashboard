Tacrolimus (Prograf, Envarsus XR, Astagraf XL) is the main immunosuppressant used after kidney, liver, heart and lung transplants and after stem cell transplants. Its blood level (the trough level) is monitored closely because too little risks rejection and too much risks kidney damage, tremor and other toxicity.

The liver and gut enzyme CYP3A5 breaks tacrolimus down. Unlike most drug-metabolizing genes, the usual result in people of European ancestry is a non-working enzyme: CPIC notes that most white people carry two non-functional CYP3A5*3 copies. People with one or two working copies, called CYP3A5 expressers (normal or intermediate metabolizers), clear tacrolimus faster and reach target levels more slowly on standard dosing. CPIC grades CYP3A5 with tacrolimus as a level A pair.

The 2015 CPIC guideline (Birdwell et al.) gives strong recommendations: expressers should start at a higher dose than the standard starting dose, within a set ceiling, while CYP3A5 poor metabolizers (non-expressers) start at the standard dose. For liver transplants the recommendation applies only when donor and recipient genotypes match, because the donor liver carries its own CYP3A5 genes. The guideline also notes that people of sub-Saharan African ancestry often need higher doses for reasons beyond CYP3A5.

Genotype guides only the starting dose; therapeutic drug monitoring of tacrolimus levels continues throughout treatment. Dosing belongs to the transplant team.
