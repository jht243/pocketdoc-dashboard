Tamoxifen (Nolvadex, Soltamox) is a hormonal therapy for estrogen receptor-positive breast cancer and for breast cancer risk reduction. The liver enzyme CYP2D6 converts it into endoxifen, a much more potent anti-estrogen. People who are CYP2D6 poor or intermediate metabolizers, or who take medicines that strongly block CYP2D6, make less endoxifen.

CPIC grades CYP2D6 with tamoxifen as a level A pair. The 2018 CPIC guideline (Goetz et al.) acknowledges that the evidence is conflicting: early studies linked poor metabolizer status to higher breast cancer recurrence, but secondary analyses of large adjuvant trials did not consistently confirm it, and an FDA panel's proposed label change was not adopted. This is why the passage is graded Moderate.

CPIC recommendations for early breast cancer:
- Poor metabolizers (strong recommendation) and intermediate metabolizers (moderate): consider an alternative hormonal therapy, such as an aromatase inhibitor for postmenopausal women or an aromatase inhibitor with ovarian function suppression for premenopausal women, noting these approaches are superior to tamoxifen regardless of genotype. Where those are not suitable, CPIC describes adjusted tamoxifen use as an option.
- Normal and ultrarapid metabolizers: standard tamoxifen therapy.
- For everyone on tamoxifen, CPIC advises avoiding prolonged use of moderate and strong CYP2D6-blocking medicines, and for intermediate metabolizers weak ones as well. Some antidepressants block CYP2D6, so a pharmacist review of the full medication list is useful.

A CYP2D6 result, and the full medication list, are worth sharing with the oncology team. Hormonal therapy decisions belong to the oncologist.
