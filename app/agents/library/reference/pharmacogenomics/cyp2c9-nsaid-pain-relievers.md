NSAIDs (nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs) are widely used pain relievers. Several are cleared mainly by the liver enzyme CYP2C9. People who are CYP2C9 intermediate or poor metabolizers clear these drugs more slowly, so blood levels run higher and last longer. Because NSAID side effects (stomach bleeding, high blood pressure, kidney injury, heart attack and heart failure) are tied to drug exposure, higher levels are assumed to raise risk.

The 2020 CPIC guideline (Theken et al.) grades CYP2C9 as level A for celecoxib (Celebrex), ibuprofen (Advil, Motrin), flurbiprofen, meloxicam (Mobic), piroxicam (Feldene) and lornoxicam:
- Normal metabolizers, and intermediate metabolizers with an activity score of 1.5: standard starting dose.
- Intermediate metabolizers with an activity score of 1: a lower starting dose and cautious increases for celecoxib, ibuprofen and flurbiprofen; a reduced dose or an alternative for meloxicam; an alternative for piroxicam, which stays in the body very long.
- Poor metabolizers: a substantially reduced starting dose for the shorter-acting drugs, and an alternative drug for meloxicam and piroxicam.
These recommendations are mostly graded moderate, which is why the passage is graded Moderate.

CPIC found CYP2C9 variation does not support genotype-based changes for naproxen (Aleve), diclofenac, aspirin or indomethacin. In every case labels advise the lowest effective dose for the shortest time.

A CYP2C9 result is worth sharing with anyone prescribing a long-term NSAID; dosing decisions belong to the prescriber.
