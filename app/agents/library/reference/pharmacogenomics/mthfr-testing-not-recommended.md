MTHFR (methylenetetrahydrofolate reductase) is a folate-processing enzyme. Common variants, usually written C677T and A1298C, are found in a large share of healthy people and show up on many consumer DNA reports, often with claims about methylation, methylfolate, fatigue, mood, miscarriage or blood clots.

The American College of Medical Genetics and Genomics (ACMG) practice guideline (Hickey et al., 2013) concluded that MTHFR polymorphism testing has minimal clinical utility. It recommends against ordering MTHFR genotyping as part of a thrombophilia (blood clot) workup or for recurrent pregnancy loss, and against testing at-risk relatives, noting that meta-analyses did not support a link between these variants and venous blood clots. ACMG published an addendum in 2020 and kept the document as a practice resource. The American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG) reached a similar position in 2018. Folic acid recommendations for people who could become pregnant are the same regardless of MTHFR status.

When there is a real question about folate metabolism, labs and guideline groups point to measuring homocysteine in the blood rather than genotyping MTHFR.

CPIC has not published a prescribing guideline for MTHFR, so it falls outside the CPIC level A and B pairs Thumbprint discusses. An MTHFR result on a consumer report is not a diagnosis and is not, by itself, a reason to take a particular supplement.
