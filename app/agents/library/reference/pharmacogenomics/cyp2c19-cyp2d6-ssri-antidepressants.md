The 2023 CPIC antidepressant guideline (Bousman et al.) gives gene-based recommendations for several SSRIs and SNRIs.

CYP2C19 breaks down citalopram (Celexa), escitalopram (Lexapro) and sertraline (Zoloft):
- Ultrarapid metabolizers clear citalopram and escitalopram quickly, so levels may be too low to work. CPIC suggests considering an antidepressant not mainly metabolized by CYP2C19.
- Poor metabolizers build up higher citalopram, escitalopram or sertraline levels, which can mean more side effects. CPIC suggests considering an antidepressant not mainly metabolized by CYP2C19, or, if the drug is still warranted, a lower starting dose, slower titration and lower maintenance dose. Higher citalopram levels also carry a QT-prolongation (heart rhythm) concern.

CYP2D6 affects paroxetine (Paxil), fluvoxamine, venlafaxine and vortioxetine. For CYP2D6 poor metabolizers, CPIC suggests lower doses of paroxetine, fluvoxamine and vortioxetine, and considering an alternative to venlafaxine. CYP2B6 has a sertraline recommendation.

CPIC found that SLC6A4 (serotonin transporter) and HTR2A results, which appear on some consumer and commercial panels, do not support clinical use for antidepressant prescribing.

Antidepressant choice and dosing belong to the prescriber; a gene result is one input, and changing or stopping an antidepressant abruptly can cause withdrawal symptoms.
