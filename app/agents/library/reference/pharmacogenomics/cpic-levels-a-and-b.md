The Clinical Pharmacogenetics Implementation Consortium (CPIC) is the main expert body that turns pharmacogenomic (PGx, pharmacogenetic, gene-drug) test results into prescribing guidance. CPIC guidelines assume a genetic result is already available; they do not decide whether a person should be tested. CPIC grades each gene-drug pair:

- Level A: genetic information should be used to change prescribing of the drug. The evidence is high or moderate, and at least one moderate or strong prescribing action (a different drug or a different dose) is recommended.
- Level B: genetic information could be used to change prescribing, because alternative drugs or doses are extremely likely to be as effective and as safe. The evidence is weaker, and at least one optional action is recommended.
- Level C and level D: published studies exist, but the evidence or actionability is not strong enough for a prescribing recommendation.

Levels start as provisional and become final only after a full CPIC guideline review.

Thumbprint's medication specialist discusses only CPIC level A and B pairs. Common examples include CYP2C19 with clopidogrel, PPIs and some SSRIs; CYP2D6 with codeine and tramadol; SLCO1B1 with statins; CYP2C9 and VKORC1 with warfarin; DPYD with fluorouracil and capecitabine; TPMT and NUDT15 with thiopurines; and HLA-B with abacavir and carbamazepine. Any change based on a result is the prescriber's decision.
