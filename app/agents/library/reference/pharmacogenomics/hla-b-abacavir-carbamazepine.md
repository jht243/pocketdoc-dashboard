Some HLA immune-system gene variants predict severe, sometimes life-threatening drug allergies. Results are reported as positive or negative for the risk allele.

Abacavir and HLA-B*57:01: abacavir (Ziagen, and in Epzicom, Triumeq and Trizivir) is an HIV medicine. Carriers of HLA-B*57:01 have a high risk of abacavir hypersensitivity, a reaction with fever, rash, stomach symptoms and breathing problems that can be fatal on re-exposure. The FDA boxed warning calls for HLA-B*57:01 screening before starting or restarting abacavir and contraindicates it in carriers. CPIC (2014 update) likewise recommends abacavir not be used in HLA-B*57:01-positive people. A negative result does not rule out every reaction.

Carbamazepine and HLA-B*15:02 or HLA-A*31:01: carbamazepine (Tegretol, Carbatrol, Equetro) treats epilepsy, trigeminal nerve pain and bipolar disorder. HLA-B*15:02 is strongly linked to Stevens-Johnson syndrome and toxic epidermal necrolysis (SJS/TEN), severe skin reactions. The 2017 CPIC update recommends not using carbamazepine in carbamazepine-naive HLA-B*15:02 carriers, and the same for oxcarbazepine (Trileptal). HLA-A*31:01 is linked to SJS/TEN, DRESS and milder rashes; CPIC recommends avoiding carbamazepine in naive carriers when alternatives exist. CPIC reports HLA-B*15:02 is most common in people of East Asian, Oceanian and South or Central Asian ancestry, less common in Japanese and Korean people, and rare in most other groups. People who have taken carbamazepine for more than three months without a skin reaction are at very low risk.

These results are worth recording permanently and sharing with every prescriber.
