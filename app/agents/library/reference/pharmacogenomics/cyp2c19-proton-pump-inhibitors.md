Most proton pump inhibitors (PPIs, acid reflux and heartburn medicines) are cleared mainly by the liver enzyme CYP2C19. The 2020 CPIC guideline covers omeprazole (Prilosec), lansoprazole (Prevacid), pantoprazole (Protonix) and dexlansoprazole (Dexilant). Esomeprazole and rabeprazole depend less on CYP2C19 and are not covered by the dosing recommendations.

- CYP2C19 ultrarapid and rapid metabolizers clear PPIs faster, which lowers blood levels and raises the chance the PPI does not work well. For ultrarapid metabolizers CPIC suggests a higher starting dose with monitoring for effect (optional recommendation). For rapid and normal metabolizers it recommends a standard starting dose, with a higher dose considered for H. pylori infection and erosive esophagitis.
- CYP2C19 intermediate and poor metabolizers have higher PPI levels, which raises the chance of benefit and possibly of side effects. CPIC recommends a standard starting dose, and for chronic therapy beyond 12 weeks with symptoms controlled, considering a lower daily dose while monitoring that it still works.

The strength of these recommendations ranges from moderate to optional, which is why this passage is graded Moderate; dexlansoprazole recommendations are all optional because data are limited.

A CYP2C19 result is worth sharing with the prescriber of a long-term PPI, who decides any change.
