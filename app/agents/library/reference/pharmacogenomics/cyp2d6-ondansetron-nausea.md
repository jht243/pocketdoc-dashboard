Ondansetron (Zofran) is one of the most widely used anti-nausea medicines, given for nausea and vomiting after surgery and during chemotherapy or radiation. It belongs to the 5-HT3 receptor antagonist class, along with granisetron, palonosetron, dolasetron and tropisetron.

The liver enzyme CYP2D6 helps clear ondansetron and tropisetron. CYP2D6 ultrarapid metabolizers, often people with extra copies (duplications) of a working CYP2D6 gene, clear these drugs faster, which lowers blood levels and has been linked to more vomiting, with the strongest evidence after surgery. CPIC grades CYP2D6 with ondansetron and tropisetron as level A.

The April 2026 CPIC update (Moore et al.) keeps the recommendations of the 2016 guideline unchanged:
- CYP2D6 ultrarapid metabolizers: consider an alternative 5-HT3 antagonist not metabolized by CYP2D6, namely granisetron (moderate recommendation). CPIC does not support simply raising the ondansetron dose, because that has not been studied.
- Normal metabolizers: standard use.
- Intermediate and poor metabolizers: no recommendation, because evidence is insufficient.
- Dolasetron, palonosetron and ramosetron: CPIC level C, no recommendation.

Because the recommendation strength is moderate, this passage is graded Moderate. A CYP2D6 ultrarapid result is worth sharing with the surgical, anesthesia or oncology team before planned surgery or chemotherapy; the choice of anti-nausea medicine is theirs.
