Statin-associated musculoskeletal symptoms (SAMS) range from muscle aches without muscle damage (myalgia, the most common), to myopathy with a markedly raised creatine kinase (CK), to rare rhabdomyolysis that can injure the kidneys. The 2022 CPIC statin guideline covers all statins and three genes:

- SLCO1B1 encodes the liver transporter that pulls statins into the liver. Decreased or poor function (often from the rs4149056 variant, also written c.521T>C) raises blood statin levels and myopathy risk, most clearly with simvastatin. For SLCO1B1 poor function, and generally for decreased function, CPIC strongly recommends choosing an alternative statin to simvastatin based on the potency needed; lovastatin is also generally replaced, and other statins get lower starting doses or dose limits.
- ABCG2 affects rosuvastatin (Crestor). For ABCG2 poor function CPIC recommends a reduced starting dose of rosuvastatin, with an alternative statin or added non-statin therapy if a higher intensity is needed.
- CYP2C9 affects fluvastatin. Intermediate and poor metabolizers get reduced starting doses or an alternative.

CPIC states that statin therapy should not be stopped or avoided because of these genotypes in people who need it; the genes guide which statin and how much. The guideline also does not decide whether testing should be done. A result on file is worth sharing with the prescriber of any statin.
