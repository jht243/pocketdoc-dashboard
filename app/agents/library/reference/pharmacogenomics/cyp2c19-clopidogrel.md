Clopidogrel (Plavix) is an antiplatelet blood thinner used after a heart attack, after a coronary stent (PCI), and after some strokes and TIAs. It is a prodrug: the liver enzyme CYP2C19 converts it to its active form.

People who are CYP2C19 intermediate or poor metabolizers (carrying one or two no-function alleles such as CYP2C19*2 or *3) make less active metabolite. The CPIC 2022 update states that they have reduced platelet inhibition and a higher risk of major adverse cardiovascular and cerebrovascular events while on clopidogrel. This is a CPIC level A gene-drug pair.

CPIC 2022 recommendations for intermediate and poor metabolizers:
- Cardiovascular indications, such as acute coronary syndrome or PCI: avoid standard-dose clopidogrel and use prasugrel or ticagrelor if there is no contraindication.
- Neurovascular indications, such as stroke or TIA: consider an alternative P2Y12 inhibitor if clinically indicated.

The 2022 update expanded the guidance to neurovascular uses and strengthened the recommendation for intermediate metabolizers compared with the 2013 version. The strongest evidence comes from people with acute coronary syndrome undergoing PCI. Normal, rapid and ultrarapid metabolizers can use clopidogrel at standard dosing.

A CYP2C19 result on file for someone taking clopidogrel is worth raising with the cardiologist or neurologist. Stopping an antiplatelet drug on one's own after a stent can be dangerous.
