Fluorouracil (5-FU) and capecitabine (Xeloda) are fluoropyrimidine chemotherapy drugs used for colorectal, breast, head and neck and other cancers. The enzyme dihydropyrimidine dehydrogenase (DPD), made by the DPYD gene, breaks down most of the drug. People with DPD deficiency can build up toxic levels, causing severe diarrhea, mouth sores, very low blood counts, and sometimes death.

In February 2026 the FDA announced revised labeling for both drugs. The boxed warning now highlights the risk of serious adverse reactions or death with complete DPD deficiency, and the labels advise testing for DPYD variants before starting treatment unless immediate treatment is necessary. People with certain homozygous or compound heterozygous DPYD variants that cause complete DPD deficiency should avoid these drugs, and people with partial deficiency may also be at higher risk, so their dosing should be individualized.

CPIC grades DPYD with fluorouracil and capecitabine as level A. Its guideline recommends an alternative drug for DPYD poor metabolizers and a reduced starting dose for intermediate metabolizers, adjusted with close monitoring. CPIC updated its DPYD allele definitions in 2024.

A DPYD result already on file is worth flagging to the oncology team before any fluoropyrimidine is started; dosing decisions belong to the oncologist.
