Coenzyme Q10 (CoQ10, ubiquinone or ubiquinol) is made by the body and used in cell energy production. Statins block an early step in cholesterol synthesis that the body also uses to make CoQ10, and meta-analyses of placebo-controlled trials show statins lower blood CoQ10 levels. That led to the idea that CoQ10 supplements could relieve statin muscle aches (myalgia).

Trial results conflict:
- A 2015 Mayo Clinic Proceedings meta-analysis of randomized trials found no significant effect on muscle pain or creatine kinase (CK).
- A 2018 Journal of the American Heart Association meta-analysis reported less muscle pain, weakness and cramps, with no change in CK.
- A 2020 Atherosclerosis meta-analysis found no benefit for muscle pain and no improvement in how many people stayed on their statin.
Most trials were small and short. The NIH National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health (NCCIH) concludes that, overall, the evidence does not support CoQ10 for statin-related muscle pain, and the National Lipid Association has not endorsed it for this use. Benefit is therefore unproven.

This fits the blinded-trial finding that most muscle symptoms reported on a statin are not caused by the drug (see the statin muscle pain passage in the medications library).

Safety: NCCIH reports no serious side effects; mild insomnia or digestive upset can occur. CoQ10 may interact with warfarin and insulin. Muscle symptoms on a statin are worth raising with the prescriber, who can check CK and review options.
