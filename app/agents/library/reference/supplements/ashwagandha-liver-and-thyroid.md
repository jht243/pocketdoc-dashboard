Ashwagandha (Withania somnifera) is an Ayurvedic herb sold for stress, anxiety and sleep. The NIH Office of Dietary Supplements (ODS, updated May 2025) says extracts may help reduce stress and anxiety and improve sleep, but long-term safety is not known; common side effects such as loose stools, nausea and drowsiness are usually mild.

Liver: the NIH LiverTox database (updated December 2024) rates ashwagandha a likely cause of clinically apparent liver injury (likelihood score B). Cases typically appear 2 to 12 weeks after starting, usually with a cholestatic or mixed pattern, jaundice (yellow skin or eyes) and itching. Most resolve after stopping, but rare cases of fatal liver injury or need for liver transplant have been reported, particularly in people with existing liver disease. ODS notes some reported products were combination products.

Thyroid: ODS describes small rises in thyroid hormone (T4) in one study, a trial in people with subclinical hypothyroidism in which TSH fell and T3 and T4 rose, and case reports of thyrotoxicosis (overactive thyroid) that resolved after stopping. ODS concludes ashwagandha might interact with thyroid hormone medicines such as levothyroxine, and possibly with diabetes and blood pressure medicines, immunosuppressants and sedatives. Some experts advise against it in pregnancy and in men with hormone-sensitive prostate cancer.

New fatigue, dark urine, jaundice or itching while taking ashwagandha warrants prompt contact with a clinician, and a liver panel (ALT, AST, bilirubin) and TSH are reasonable labs to raise.
