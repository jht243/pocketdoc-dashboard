N-acetylcysteine (NAC, N-acetyl-L-cysteine, acetylcysteine) is a form of the amino acid cysteine that the body uses to make glutathione, an antioxidant. It is sold in supplements marketed for antioxidant support, respiratory health and liver health.

NAC is also an established prescription drug. The NIH LiverTox database describes oral and intravenous acetylcysteine as the treatment for acetaminophen (Tylenol) overdose: it restores glutathione and is given as soon as overdose is diagnosed.

Regulatory status: NAC was approved as a new drug in 1963. In March 2022 the FDA denied two industry citizen petitions and confirmed its position that NAC is excluded from the legal definition of a dietary supplement, because it was approved as a drug before being marketed as a supplement. Citing no safety concerns found so far, the FDA then issued final guidance in August 2022 saying it intends to exercise enforcement discretion, meaning NAC products labeled as dietary supplements that otherwise comply with the law can remain on sale while the agency considers a request for rulemaking. Industry groups disagree with FDA's interpretation, and the status could change.

Being on shelves does not mean NAC has been shown to work for the uses supplements advertise; as a supplement it was not FDA-reviewed for effectiveness. Anyone considering NAC alongside prescription medicines should check with a clinician or pharmacist first.
