Red yeast rice is rice fermented with a mold (Monascus purpureus). The fermentation can produce monacolins, including monacolin K, which is structurally identical to lovastatin, a prescription statin cholesterol drug.

What the NIH National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health (NCCIH, updated November 2022) says:
- Strength is unpredictable. Some products contain little or no monacolin K, others contain a lot, and some have been found with commercial lovastatin illegally added. Labels usually do not list the amount. A 2017 analysis of 28 US brands found the amount varied more than 60-fold.
- Legal status: the FDA treats red yeast rice products with enhanced or added lovastatin as unapproved new drugs that cannot be sold as dietary supplements.
- Products with meaningful monacolin K can lower LDL cholesterol, but they can also cause the same side effects as statins (muscle, liver and kidney damage) and the same drug interactions, for example with grapefruit and with medicines that block CYP3A4.
- Contamination: some products contain citrinin, a toxin that can damage the kidneys. A 2021 analysis found only one of 37 products below the EU limit, and four contaminated products were labeled citrinin-free.
- The European Food Safety Authority could not identify a safe intake level of monacolins, and red yeast rice is not recommended in pregnancy or breastfeeding.

Taking red yeast rice together with a prescription statin doubles up on the same drug. Anyone using it should tell the prescriber, who can track LDL-C, liver enzymes and muscle symptoms.
