Turmeric is a common spice, and its main active compounds are curcuminoids such as curcumin. Turmeric and curcumin supplements are widely used for joint pain and inflammation.

For many years turmeric was considered safe for the liver, partly because curcumin is poorly absorbed. Newer products add piperine (black pepper extract) or use lipid and nanoparticle formulations to boost absorption, and liver injury reports rose after these became popular.

The NIH LiverTox database (updated June 2025) now rates turmeric likelihood score A, recently established as a well-documented cause of clinically apparent liver injury. Key points from LiverTox:
- The injury is usually hepatocellular (high ALT and AST) and often comes with jaundice. Some cases show autoantibodies and resemble autoimmune hepatitis.
- It appears to be idiosyncratic and immune-mediated, and is closely linked to the HLA-B*35:01 gene variant, which is also tied to liver injury from green tea extract, garcinia cambogia and fo-ti.
- It is probably very rare relative to how many people take turmeric.
- Most cases resolve within 1 to 3 months of stopping the product, though acute liver failure has been reported.

Symptoms of liver injury include fatigue, nausea, dark urine, pale stool, itching and yellowing of the skin or eyes. Anyone with these symptoms while taking turmeric or curcumin, or with an unexplained rise in liver enzymes, should tell their clinician about the supplement.
