St John's wort (Hypericum perforatum) is an herbal supplement used for low mood and depression. The NIH National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health (NCCIH, updated May 2025) says it appears better than placebo and about as effective as standard antidepressants for mild to moderate depression, with uncertainty for severe depression and use beyond 12 weeks.

Its main risk is drug interactions. NCCIH says it can weaken the effects of many medicines, including:
- birth control pills (risk of unplanned pregnancy)
- warfarin (blood thinner)
- simvastatin (statin)
- cyclosporine (transplant drug)
- digoxin and ivabradine (heart drugs)
- some HIV medicines and some cancer drugs (irinotecan, imatinib, docetaxel)
- certain seizure drugs (phenytoin, carbamazepine)
- some antidepressants (amitriptyline, bupropion)

Published reviews explain the mechanism: St John's wort, largely through its hyperforin content, switches on the liver and gut enzyme CYP3A4 and the drug pump P-glycoprotein, so many drugs are cleared faster and blood levels fall. One study found enzyme activity returned to baseline about a week after stopping.

Separately, combined with SSRIs, SNRIs or other serotonergic drugs it can cause serious serotonin-related side effects (serotonin syndrome).

Anyone taking any prescription medicine should check with the prescriber or pharmacist before starting or stopping St John's wort, because stopping it can also change drug levels.
