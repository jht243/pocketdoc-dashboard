Fish oil and other omega-3 supplements supply EPA and DHA, the long-chain omega-3 fatty acids found in fatty fish.

Heart prevention: VITAL (VITamin D and OmegA-3 TriaL, 2018) tested a standard fish oil supplement against placebo in 25,871 US adults without prior heart disease. According to the NIH Office of Dietary Supplements (ODS) fact sheet, the supplement did not significantly reduce the combined rate of heart attack, stroke and cardiovascular death over a median of 5.3 years. ASCEND, in adults with diabetes, also found no significant reduction in serious vascular events. The ODS notes that the amount used in most supplement trials has had no significant effect on primary outcomes.

Prescription omega-3 is different. Icosapent ethyl (Vascepa) is an FDA-approved purified EPA drug, not a supplement. In the REDUCE-IT trial it lowered cardiovascular events in people with heart disease or diabetes who had triglycerides of 150 mg/dL or higher on a statin, and the FDA expanded its label for this in December 2019. ODS notes the mineral-oil placebo may have exaggerated the difference.

Atrial fibrillation (AFib, irregular heartbeat): the ODS reports that two large trials of high-amount omega-3 products in people with or at high risk of heart disease found a slightly higher risk of AFib, and the Vascepa label carries AFib and bleeding warnings. Fish oil may also raise INR in some people on warfarin. Anyone with AFib, or on a blood thinner, should raise fish oil use with their clinician.
