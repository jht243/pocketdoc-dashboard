SSRI antidepressants such as sertraline (Zoloft), escitalopram (Lexapro), citalopram (Celexa), fluoxetine (Prozac) and paroxetine (Paxil), and SNRIs such as venlafaxine and duloxetine, raise serotonin signaling.

The Zoloft prescribing information warns that SSRIs and SNRIs can cause serotonin syndrome, a potentially life-threatening reaction, and that the risk rises with other serotonergic agents. It names St John's wort alongside triptans (migraine drugs), tricyclic antidepressants, tramadol, fentanyl, lithium, tryptophan, buspirone and amphetamines. Serotonin syndrome symptoms include agitation or confusion, fast heart rate, sweating, shivering, muscle twitching or stiffness, fever, and diarrhea; severe cases need emergency care. MAO inhibitors are contraindicated with SSRIs for the same reason.

The NIH National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health (NCCIH, page updated May 2025) adds that St John's wort combined with certain antidepressants can increase serotonin-related side effects, which may be serious, and that the herb weakens many other medicines (see the St John's wort interaction passage in the supplements library).

Anyone taking an antidepressant who is considering St John's wort, or other serotonin-boosting supplements such as 5-HTP or tryptophan, should raise it with the prescriber first. Sudden agitation, fever, sweating and muscle jerking after combining them is an urgent symptom.
