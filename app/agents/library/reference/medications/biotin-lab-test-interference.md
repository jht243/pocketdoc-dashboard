---
title: Biotin supplements can distort lab test results
source: FDA Safety Communication on biotin interference with lab tests, 2017, updated 2019
evidence: Strong
approved_by: pending
as_of: 2019-11
url: https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/in-vitro-diagnostics/biotin-interference-troponin-lab-tests-assays-subject-biotin-interference
---

Biotin (vitamin B7) is common in multivitamins, prenatal vitamins and supplements marketed for hair, skin and nail growth. Many lab tests (immunoassays) use biotin-streptavidin chemistry, and high levels of biotin in the blood can make results falsely high or falsely low, depending on the test.

The FDA warned about this in 2017 and updated the warning in November 2019. Its main concern is troponin, the blood test used to diagnose a heart attack: a falsely low troponin could cause a missed diagnosis, and the FDA received a report of a death in a patient taking high levels of biotin whose troponin was falsely low. Some test makers have fixed the problem; the FDA keeps a list of troponin assays still subject to interference. Hormone tests are also affected. Thyroid tests are a well-documented example: biotin can produce results that look like an overactive thyroid (falsely low TSH, falsely high free T4 or T3) when the thyroid is normal.

The FDA advises telling the clinician and the lab about any biotin use, including in multivitamins, and asks clinicians to consider biotin interference when a result does not match the clinical picture. The Synthroid prescribing information advises stopping biotin at least 2 days before thyroid testing. A surprising thyroid, hormone or troponin result in someone taking biotin is worth raising with the ordering clinician.
