Levothyroxine (Synthroid, Levoxyl, Unithroid, Tirosint, generic T4) is thyroid hormone replacement for an underactive thyroid (hypothyroidism). How much reaches the blood depends on what it is taken with.

The Synthroid prescribing information (revised February 2024) says:
- Take it once daily, preferably on an empty stomach before breakfast.
- Keep it at least 4 hours apart from calcium carbonate, iron (ferrous sulfate) and phosphate binders such as sevelamer and lanthanum, which bind levothyroxine in the gut.
- Take it at least 4 hours before bile acid sequestrants (cholestyramine, colestipol, colesevelam), or have TSH monitored.
- Proton pump inhibitors (omeprazole, pantoprazole and others), antacids and sucralfate lower stomach acidity and can reduce absorption, so monitoring is advised.
- Soybean flour, cottonseed meal, walnuts and dietary fiber can reduce absorption, and grapefruit juice can delay it.

The label also notes that biotin supplements can interfere with thyroid blood tests (TSH, free T4) and advises stopping biotin at least 2 days before thyroid testing.

A TSH that drifts up after starting calcium, iron or an acid reducer, or after changing breakfast habits, is a pattern worth raising with the prescriber, who decides any change.
