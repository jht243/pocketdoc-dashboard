Long-term metformin (Glucophage, Glumetza, Fortamet, Riomet, and combination pills such as Janumet or Synjardy) can lower vitamin B12 (cobalamin) absorption in some people.

The ADA Standards of Care in Diabetes 2025 (Section 9) state that metformin use is associated with a higher risk of vitamin B12 deficiency and worsening of neuropathy symptoms. Since 2017 the ADA Standards have advised periodic measurement of B12 in people taking metformin, particularly those with anemia or peripheral neuropathy (numbness, tingling or burning in the feet). The evidence includes the Diabetes Prevention Program Outcomes Study, which linked long-term metformin use with B12 deficiency. The UK medicines regulator (MHRA) likewise advises checking B12 in metformin users who have symptoms or other risk factors, such as older age, a vegan diet or also taking a PPI.

Low B12 can cause anemia with large red blood cells (a high MCV on a CBC), fatigue, memory problems, and nerve symptoms that can be mistaken for diabetic neuropathy. On a lab report this appears as serum vitamin B12; methylmalonic acid (MMA) and homocysteine are sometimes added when B12 is borderline.

A B12 test is a reasonable thing to raise with the prescriber for anyone on long-term metformin. A low result is a reason to check and treat B12, a decision the prescriber makes. On its own, a low B12 does not mean metformin is the wrong medicine.
