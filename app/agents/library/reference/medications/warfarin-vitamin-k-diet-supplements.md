Warfarin (Coumadin, Jantoven) is a blood thinner (anticoagulant) that works by blocking vitamin K-dependent clotting factors. Its effect is tracked with the INR (international normalized ratio) blood test.

The Coumadin prescribing information carries a boxed warning that drugs, dietary changes and other factors affect the INR, and it calls for regular INR monitoring in everyone taking it. It also notes that some botanical (herbal) products can change the response to warfarin and warrant closer INR checks.

Vitamin K counteracts warfarin. The NIH Office of Dietary Supplements vitamin K fact sheet says people taking warfarin need to keep their vitamin K intake from food and supplements consistent, because sudden changes can increase or decrease its effect. The goal is steady intake, not avoidance: a sudden increase (for example, starting daily kale, spinach, collards or other leafy greens, a multivitamin with vitamin K, or a nutrition shake) can lower the INR and raise clot risk, and a sudden decrease can raise the INR and bleeding risk.

Supplements matter too. St John's wort lowers warfarin levels (NCCIH lists warfarin among the drugs it weakens), and patient guides commonly ask people on warfarin to report supplements such as fish oil, garlic, ginger, alfalfa and coenzyme Q10 before starting them.

Any diet change or new supplement while on warfarin is worth telling the anticoagulation clinic or prescriber about so the INR can be checked. Newer anticoagulants (apixaban, rivaroxaban, dabigatran, edoxaban) do not work through vitamin K.
