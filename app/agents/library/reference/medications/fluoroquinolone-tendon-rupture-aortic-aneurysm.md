Fluoroquinolones are antibiotics taken by mouth or injection: ciprofloxacin (Cipro), levofloxacin (Levaquin), moxifloxacin (Avelox), ofloxacin, delafloxacin and gemifloxacin.

Their labels carry a boxed warning for tendinitis and tendon rupture (often the Achilles tendon), peripheral neuropathy (nerve damage with tingling or numbness), central nervous system effects, and worsening of myasthenia gravis. The ciprofloxacin label says tendon risk is higher over age 60, with corticosteroid (steroid) use, and after a kidney, heart or lung transplant.

In July 2016 the FDA said these side effects can be disabling, potentially permanent and can occur together, and that for acute sinusitis, acute bronchitis flare-ups and uncomplicated urinary tract infections (UTI), fluoroquinolones should be reserved for people with no other treatment options. In July 2018 FDA added warnings about significant drops in blood sugar and mental health side effects.

In December 2018 the FDA warned that fluoroquinolones can increase rare but serious tears or ruptures of the aorta (aortic dissection or aortic aneurysm rupture); studies showed about twice the risk. Higher-risk groups include people with a known aortic or other aneurysm or blocked arteries, high blood pressure, Marfan or Ehlers-Danlos syndrome, and older adults.

Sudden, severe, constant pain in the chest, stomach or back is an emergency. New tendon pain, numbness or confusion during treatment should be reported to the prescriber promptly; FDA advises not stopping an antibiotic without talking to the prescriber first.
