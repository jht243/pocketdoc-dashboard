GLP-1 medicines such as semaglutide (Ozempic, Wegovy) and tirzepatide (Mounjaro, Zepbound) slow stomach emptying. That raises a concern about pulmonary aspiration, stomach contents entering the lungs, during general anesthesia or deep sedation for surgery or an endoscopy such as a colonoscopy.

In October 2024 five societies, the American Society for Metabolic and Bariatric Surgery, the American Society of Anesthesiologists, the American Gastroenterological Association, the International Society of Perioperative Care of Patients with Obesity and the Society of American Gastrointestinal and Endoscopic Surgeons, published joint guidance:
- Most people may continue GLP-1 drugs as prescribed before elective surgery and endoscopy; the societies noted that stopping them to prepare for a procedure may carry its own risks.
- Each person's risk of complications such as aspiration should be assessed before the procedure.
- For people at high risk of significant stomach side effects, a liquid diet for 24 hours before the procedure is advised, with the anesthesia plan adjusted.
- In rare cases the procedure may need to be postponed.
- Decisions should be shared between the patient and the care team.

This is consensus guidance, so it is graded Moderate. The Wegovy label also lists pulmonary aspiration during anesthesia among its warnings (see the GLP-1 label warnings passage).

Anyone on a GLP-1 drug with a procedure scheduled should tell the surgeon, endoscopist and anesthesia team, especially if nausea, vomiting or bloating are present, and follow their specific instructions.
