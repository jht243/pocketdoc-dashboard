The AGS Beers Criteria, maintained by the American Geriatrics Society and updated in 2023, is a list of potentially inappropriate medications (PIMs) for adults 65 and older. It applies in outpatient, hospital and nursing-home care but not hospice or end-of-life care, and AGS stresses that it supports, rather than replaces, shared decisions between patient and clinician.

It has five parts: medicines usually best avoided, medicines to avoid with certain conditions, medicines to use with caution, risky drug-drug combinations, and medicines needing kidney-function dose adjustment.

Well-known examples in the criteria:
- Strongly anticholinergic medicines, such as first-generation antihistamines like diphenhydramine (in many over-the-counter sleep aids) and hydroxyzine, which can cause confusion, constipation and urinary retention. The 2023 update added anticholinergics to the medicines to avoid after falls or fractures.
- Benzodiazepines (alprazolam, lorazepam, diazepam, clonazepam) and the sleep drugs zolpidem, eszopiclone and zaleplon, linked to falls, fractures, delirium and memory problems (see the sleeping pills Beers Criteria passage in the sleep library).
- Long-term NSAID use, and long-term proton pump inhibitor use without a clear indication.
- 2023 changes: aspirin for primary prevention moved to avoid starting; all sulfonylurea diabetes drugs are now listed because of low blood sugar risk; for new anticoagulation, newer blood thinners are preferred over warfarin.

A medicine on the list is not automatically wrong for a given person. The criteria are a prompt for a medication review with the prescriber or pharmacist.
