title: Grapefruit juice with simvastatin, lovastatin and atorvastatin

evidence: Strong
approved_by: pending

Grapefruit and grapefruit juice block CYP3A4, the gut and liver enzyme that breaks down several statin cholesterol medicines.

The US prescribing information for Zocor (simvastatin), revised in 2023, tells patients to avoid grapefruit juice because it can raise simvastatin blood levels and increase the risk of myopathy and rhabdomyolysis (muscle breakdown). The label does not define a safe amount. Lovastatin (Mevacor, Altoprev) is cleared by the same CYP3A4 pathway and is affected the same way. Atorvastatin (Lipitor) is also a CYP3A4 statin, but its labeling describes a meaningful rise mainly with large quantities of grapefruit juice, and the effect is smaller than with simvastatin or lovastatin. Rosuvastatin, pravastatin and pitavastatin are not mainly cleared by CYP3A4, so grapefruit is much less of a concern with them.

The same CYP3A4 logic applies to medicines: the simvastatin label lists strong CYP3A4 inhibitors as contraindicated, including certain azole antifungals (itraconazole, ketoconazole, posaconazole, voriconazole), macrolide antibiotics (clarithromycin, erythromycin), some HIV and hepatitis C antivirals, cobicistat-containing products and nefazodone.

Grapefruit habits, and any new antibiotic or antifungal, are worth mentioning to the prescriber or pharmacist when taking a statin.
