Proton pump inhibitors (PPIs) are acid reflux and heartburn medicines: omeprazole (Prilosec), esomeprazole (Nexium), lansoprazole (Prevacid), pantoprazole (Protonix), rabeprazole (Aciphex) and dexlansoprazole (Dexilant).

Low magnesium: in a March 2011 Drug Safety Communication the FDA warned that prescription PPIs can cause low serum magnesium (hypomagnesemia) with prolonged use, in most reported cases after more than a year of treatment. In about a quarter of the cases FDA reviewed, magnesium supplements alone did not correct the level and the PPI had to be stopped. Low magnesium can cause muscle spasms or cramps, tremor, an irregular heartbeat and seizures. The FDA advised clinicians to consider checking magnesium before starting a PPI expected to be used long term and periodically afterward, especially in people also taking digoxin or other drugs that lower magnesium, such as diuretics. The warning was added to all prescription PPI labels.

Vitamin B12: PPI labels also warn that daily acid-suppressing treatment over a long period (the omeprazole label cites longer than three years) can reduce absorption of vitamin B12 (cyanocobalamin), and that deficiency should be considered if symptoms appear.

Serum magnesium and vitamin B12 are reasonable labs to raise with the prescriber for anyone on a PPI long term.
