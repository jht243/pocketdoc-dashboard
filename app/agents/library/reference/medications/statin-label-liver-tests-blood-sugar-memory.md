In February 2012 the FDA changed the labels of all statin cholesterol medicines (atorvastatin, rosuvastatin, simvastatin, pravastatin, lovastatin, fluvastatin, pitavastatin and combination pills such as Vytorin):

- Liver tests: routine periodic liver enzyme monitoring (ALT, AST) was removed. Labels now advise liver tests before starting a statin and afterward only as clinically indicated. FDA concluded that serious statin liver injury is rare and unpredictable, and that routine checks did not detect or prevent it. Symptoms worth reporting promptly include unusual fatigue, loss of appetite, upper belly pain, dark urine or yellowing of the skin or eyes.
- Blood sugar: rises in fasting glucose and hemoglobin A1c were added to the labels. The current Lipitor label (revised April 2024) keeps this warning. A 2024 Cholesterol Treatment Trialists' meta-analysis of blinded trials found about 10% more new diabetes diagnoses with low or moderate intensity statins and about 36% more with high intensity, mostly in people whose glucose was already near the diabetes threshold; average A1c rose by less than 0.1 percentage point.
- Memory: rare reports of memory loss or confusion were added. FDA describes them as generally not serious and reversible, with a median resolution of about 3 weeks.

FDA and the CTT authors both concluded that the cardiovascular benefits of statins outweigh these small risks. Questions about any of these effects belong with the prescriber.
