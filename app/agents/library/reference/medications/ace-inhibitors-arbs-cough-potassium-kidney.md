ACE inhibitors (lisinopril, enalapril, ramipril, benazepril and other "-pril" drugs) and ARBs, angiotensin receptor blockers (losartan, valsartan, irbesartan, olmesartan and other "-sartan" drugs), lower blood pressure and protect the heart and kidneys.

What their labels say:
- Cough: the Cozaar label notes that a persistent dry cough is associated with ACE inhibitors and is a common reason they are stopped; in trials of people who had ACE inhibitor cough, losartan caused cough no more often than placebo.
- Potassium: both classes can raise blood potassium (hyperkalemia). The Zestril label (revised November 2024) advises periodic potassium checks and names kidney disease, diabetes, potassium-sparing diuretics (spironolactone, amiloride, triamterene), potassium supplements and potassium-based salt substitutes as risk factors.
- Kidney function: both can change kidney function, so the label advises periodic monitoring; a small, reversible rise in creatinine can occur. NSAIDs such as ibuprofen taken with these drugs can worsen kidney function in older adults, dehydrated people or people with kidney disease, and blunt the blood pressure effect.
- Angioedema: swelling of the face, lips, tongue or throat can occur at any time and is an emergency.
- Pregnancy: both carry a boxed warning for fetal toxicity.
- Combining an ACE inhibitor with an ARB or aliskiren generally adds risk without added benefit.

A basic metabolic panel (potassium, creatinine, eGFR) is the usual lab follow-up; changes to the medicine are the prescriber's decision.
