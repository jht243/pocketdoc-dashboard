Muscle aches, muscle pain (myalgia) and weakness are the side effects people most often blame on a statin such as atorvastatin, rosuvastatin, simvastatin or pravastatin.

In 2022 the Cholesterol Treatment Trialists' (CTT) Collaboration published an individual-participant meta-analysis in The Lancet covering large double-blind trials: 19 trials of statin versus placebo with 123,940 participants, plus 4 trials of more versus less intensive statin therapy. Muscle symptoms were reported often in both the statin and the placebo groups. The authors estimated that only about 1 in 15 reports of muscle pain or weakness in people taking a statin was actually caused by the statin; the University of Oxford summary put it as more than 90% of muscle symptoms in statin users not being due to the drug. Most of the small excess occurred in the first year, and higher-intensity regimens showed a somewhat larger excess than low or moderate intensity.

A European Heart Journal commentary stressed that this does not mean real symptoms should be dismissed. True statin myopathy, with a markedly raised creatine kinase (CK), and rhabdomyolysis are rare but real.

Muscle symptoms on a statin are worth raising with the prescriber, who can check CK and review options. Any decision about the statin itself is the prescriber's call.
