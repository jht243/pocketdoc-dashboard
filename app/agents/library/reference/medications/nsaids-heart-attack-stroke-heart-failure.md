Non-aspirin NSAIDs (nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs) are common pain and fever relievers: ibuprofen (Advil, Motrin), naproxen (Aleve), diclofenac (Voltaren), celecoxib (Celebrex), meloxicam and others.

In July 2015 the FDA strengthened the existing label warning that these drugs raise the chance of a heart attack or stroke. Key points from the FDA safety communication:
- The risk can appear as early as the first weeks of use and may rise with longer use.
- The risk appears greater at higher doses.
- It applies to people with or without heart disease, although people with heart disease or its risk factors start from a higher baseline risk.
- People given NSAIDs after a first heart attack were more likely to die in the following year.
- NSAIDs also raise the risk of heart failure.
- FDA estimated the increase at 10% to 50% or more depending on the drug and amount, and could not rank any one NSAID as clearly safer.

FDA also noted that many cold, flu and sleep products contain NSAIDs, so taking several at once is a common hidden overlap, and that ibuprofen and naproxen can interfere with the protective effect of low-dose aspirin. Aspirin itself is an NSAID but is not covered by this warning.

Chest pain, shortness of breath, sudden weakness on one side or slurred speech while taking an NSAID needs emergency care. Regular NSAID use is worth reviewing with a clinician, especially with heart disease, high blood pressure or kidney disease.
