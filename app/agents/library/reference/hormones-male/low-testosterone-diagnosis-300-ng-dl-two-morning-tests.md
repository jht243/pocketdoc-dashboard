The American Urological Association (AUA) testosterone deficiency guideline (published 2018, validity confirmed 2024) sets out how low testosterone (low T, testosterone deficiency, male hypogonadism) is diagnosed:

- A total testosterone below 300 ng/dL is a reasonable cut-off supporting the diagnosis (Moderate recommendation).
- The diagnosis should be made only after two total testosterone measurements on separate occasions, both taken in the early morning (Strong recommendation, Grade A). Testosterone is highest in the morning and varies day to day.
- Testosterone deficiency is diagnosed only when a low level is combined with symptoms or signs, such as low sex drive, erectile dysfunction, fatigue, or loss of muscle or bone (Moderate recommendation). A low number without symptoms, or symptoms with a normal number, does not meet the definition.

The Endocrine Society 2018 guideline is similar: it recommends diagnosing hypogonadism only in men with symptoms and signs plus unequivocally and consistently low testosterone, using a fasting morning total testosterone repeated to confirm. It notes that the lower limit of normal for total testosterone, harmonized to the CDC standard in healthy non-obese young men, is 264 ng/dL for CDC-certified assays.

A single low result, or one drawn in the afternoon or during acute illness, is commonly repeated before conclusions are drawn.
