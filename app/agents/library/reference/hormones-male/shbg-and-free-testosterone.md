---
title: SHBG and free testosterone - when total testosterone misleads
source: Endocrine Society 2018 Testosterone Therapy in Men With Hypogonadism Guideline
evidence: Strong
approved_by: pending
as_of: 2018
url: https://www.endocrine.org/clinical-practice-guidelines/testosterone-therapy

Most testosterone in the blood is bound to proteins: tightly to SHBG (sex hormone-binding globulin) and loosely to albumin. Only a small fraction is free. Total testosterone measures all of it, so changes in SHBG can make total testosterone look low or normal when free testosterone is not.

The Endocrine Society 2018 guideline recommends measuring free testosterone in men whose conditions alter SHBG, and when total testosterone is in the borderline zone around the lower limit of normal (it gives about 200 to 400 ng/dL as an example).

- Conditions that lower SHBG, and can push total testosterone below normal even when free testosterone is normal: obesity, type 2 diabetes, and androgen use.
- Conditions that raise SHBG, and can make total testosterone look normal or high even when free testosterone is low: aging, HIV infection, cirrhosis and hepatitis, hyperthyroidism, some anticonvulsants, and estrogen use.

The guideline says free testosterone should be measured by equilibrium dialysis or calculated from total testosterone, SHBG and albumin with an accurate formula. It notes that no harmonized reference range for free testosterone exists, so the lab's own range is used. Direct analog free testosterone immunoassays are less reliable. Interpretation in context belongs to the treating clinician.
