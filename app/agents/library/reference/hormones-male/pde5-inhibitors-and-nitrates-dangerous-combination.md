PDE5 inhibitors are the common erectile dysfunction pills: sildenafil (Viagra), tadalafil (Cialis), vardenafil and avanafil. They widen blood vessels.

The FDA-approved Viagra prescribing information lists these as contraindications, meaning the drug must not be used with them:

- Nitrates and other nitric oxide donors, taken regularly or occasionally. This includes nitroglycerin (chest pain tablets, sprays and patches), isosorbide mononitrate and dinitrate, and amyl nitrite ("poppers"). Sildenafil strengthens the blood-pressure-lowering effect of nitrates, which can cause a dangerous drop in blood pressure.
- Guanylate cyclase stimulators such as riociguat, a lung blood pressure medicine.

Other label warnings:
- Sexual activity carries some cardiac risk in people with existing heart disease.
- An erection lasting more than 4 hours needs immediate medical care.
- Sudden vision loss in one or both eyes, or sudden hearing loss, needs prompt medical attention.
- Combining with alpha-blockers (used for blood pressure or prostate symptoms, such as doxazosin or tamsulosin) can lower blood pressure, so caution is advised.

The nitrate contraindication appears in the labels of the other PDE5 inhibitors as well. Anyone who uses nitrates, or has chest pain after taking an erectile dysfunction pill, should tell emergency staff, because nitrates are a standard chest pain treatment.
