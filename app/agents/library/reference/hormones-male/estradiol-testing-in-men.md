Men make estradiol (E2, the main estrogen) mostly by converting testosterone through the aromatase enzyme, largely in fat tissue. Estradiol contributes to bone health, libido and other functions in men.

The AUA testosterone deficiency guideline recommends that serum estradiol be measured in men with testosterone deficiency who have breast symptoms or gynecomastia (enlarged breast tissue) before testosterone therapy is started. This is graded Expert Opinion, which is why this passage is labelled Moderate.

The AUA guideline does not recommend routine estradiol testing for every man on or considering testosterone, and it does not set a target estradiol level. Popular advice to keep estradiol within a narrow "optimal" range on testosterone therapy is not a guideline position and should be treated as Emerging.

Separately, the AUA guideline lists aromatase inhibitors (which lower estradiol) among options clinicians may use in men with low testosterone who want to preserve fertility (Conditional recommendation). Using such medicines is a prescriber decision. Breast tenderness or enlargement in a man is a reason to see a clinician, since it has several possible causes besides hormones, including some medications and liver or thyroid conditions.
