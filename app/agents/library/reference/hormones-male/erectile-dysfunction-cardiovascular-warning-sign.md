Erectile dysfunction (ED, impotence, trouble getting or keeping an erection) is often a blood vessel problem, and the arteries of the penis are small enough to show trouble early.

The American Urological Association (AUA) 2018 erectile dysfunction guideline states that men should be counseled that ED is a risk marker for underlying cardiovascular disease (heart attack and stroke risk) and other health conditions (Clinical Principle). The guideline notes:

- ED symptoms may come before a cardiovascular event by up to five years.
- In younger men, ED predicts a markedly higher risk of future cardiac events, and in one large trial ED was as strong a predictor of future cardiac events as cigarette smoking.
- ED and heart disease share risk factors: high blood pressure, diabetes, smoking, abnormal cholesterol, obesity, inactivity and depression.

The guideline calls for a full medical, sexual and psychosocial history, a physical exam and selected lab tests in men with ED (Clinical Principle). It recommends measuring a morning total testosterone in men with ED (Moderate Recommendation, Grade C), and counseling men whose health conditions affect erections that diet changes and more physical activity may improve erectile function (Moderate Recommendation, Grade C).

New ED is a good reason for a check of blood pressure, cholesterol, blood sugar and overall heart risk with a clinician, not only a prescription.
