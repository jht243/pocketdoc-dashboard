---
title: Testosterone therapy suppresses sperm production and fertility
source: AUA 2018 Evaluation and Management of Testosterone Deficiency Guideline, validity confirmed 2024; Endocrine Society 2018 Testosterone Therapy Guideline
evidence: Strong
approved_by: pending
as_of: 2024
url: https://www.auanet.org/guidelines-and-quality/guidelines/testosterone-deficiency-guideline
---

Testosterone taken from outside the body (exogenous testosterone: injections, gels, pellets, patches) signals the pituitary to reduce LH and FSH. Without those signals, the testes make less of their own testosterone and less sperm, which can reduce fertility and sometimes cause a very low or zero sperm count.

The AUA testosterone deficiency guideline states:

- Exogenous testosterone should not be prescribed to men who are currently trying to conceive (Strong recommendation, Grade A).
- The long-term impact of exogenous testosterone on sperm production should be discussed with men interested in future fertility (Strong recommendation).
- Men with testosterone deficiency who are interested in fertility should have a reproductive health evaluation before treatment (Moderate recommendation).
- Clinicians may use other approaches in men with low testosterone who want to maintain fertility, such as aromatase inhibitors, hCG (human chorionic gonadotropin), or SERMs like clomiphene (Conditional recommendation).

The Endocrine Society 2018 guideline likewise recommends against testosterone therapy in men planning fertility in the near term.

Anyone on testosterone who is planning a pregnancy with a partner should raise it with the prescribing clinician; choosing an approach is the clinician's decision.
