TRAVERSE, published in the New England Journal of Medicine in 2023, was a large randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial run after the FDA asked testosterone makers to study cardiovascular safety. It enrolled more than 5,000 men aged 45 to 80 with symptoms of hypogonadism, two fasting morning testosterone levels below 300 ng/dL, and either existing cardiovascular disease or high cardiovascular risk. Mean follow-up was about 33 months.

Main result: testosterone gel was noninferior to placebo for major adverse cardiac events (cardiovascular death, nonfatal heart attack, nonfatal stroke), which occurred in 7.0% of the testosterone group versus 7.3% of the placebo group.

Safety signals: the testosterone group had a higher incidence of:

- Atrial fibrillation: 3.5% versus 2.4%
- Acute kidney injury: 2.3% versus 1.5%
- Pulmonary embolism (lung blood clot): about 0.9% versus 0.5%

TRAVERSE reassures about heart attack and stroke in this population, but it does not show that testosterone prevents heart disease. Many participants stopped treatment early, and it did not study younger men or men without hypogonadism. The 2018 AUA guideline, written before TRAVERSE, told clinicians to counsel that the cardiovascular effect could not be stated definitively. Individual risk discussions belong with the prescribing clinician.
