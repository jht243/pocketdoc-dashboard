Testosterone gels (brand names include AndroGel and Testim) can rub off onto other people through skin contact, which is called secondary exposure.

In May 2009, the FDA required a boxed warning on testosterone gels after reports of children who were exposed through contact with a parent or caregiver's treated skin. Reported effects in children included enlarged genitals, early pubic hair, advanced bone age, increased libido and aggressive behavior; most improved once exposure stopped, but not all.

The current AndroGel label boxed warning states that virilization has been reported in children secondarily exposed to testosterone gel and that children should avoid contact with unwashed or unclothed application sites. The label instructs users to:

- Wash hands with soap and water right after applying.
- Cover the application site with clothing once the gel has dried.
- Wash the site before any expected skin-to-skin contact.
- If someone touches an unwashed or uncovered site, wash their skin with soap and water as soon as possible.

Women, especially those who are or may become pregnant, should also avoid contact, because testosterone can harm a developing baby. Signs of unexpected masculinization in a woman, or early puberty signs in a child, in a household where testosterone gel is used should be raised with a clinician.

This applies to topical gels and creams, not injections. Product choice belongs to the prescriber.
