PSA (prostate-specific antigen) is a blood test for prostate problems, reported in ng/mL.

The AUA testosterone deficiency guideline states that PSA should be measured in men over 40 before starting testosterone therapy, to exclude an existing prostate cancer (Clinical Principle). It also recommends that men be informed there is no evidence linking testosterone therapy to developing prostate cancer (Strong recommendation), and that men with a history of prostate cancer be told the evidence is inadequate to quantify the risk-benefit balance.

The Endocrine Society 2018 guideline:

- Recommends against starting testosterone in men with a PSA above 4 ng/mL, or above 3 ng/mL in men at high risk of prostate cancer, without further urological evaluation.
- Recommends urology referral during the first 12 months of therapy if PSA rises by more than 1.4 ng/mL above baseline, a confirmed PSA exceeds 4.0 ng/mL, or a prostate abnormality is found on exam.
- After the first year, recommends following standard prostate cancer screening guidelines, with shared decision-making about monitoring.

A rising PSA on testosterone is a reason for the prescribing clinician or a urologist to review, not a diagnosis of cancer.
