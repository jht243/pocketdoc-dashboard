---
title: No routine testosterone screening and treatment in men 65 and older
source: Endocrine Society 2018 Testosterone Therapy in Men With Hypogonadism Guideline
evidence: Moderate
approved_by: pending
as_of: 2018
url: https://www.endocrine.org/clinical-practice-guidelines/testosterone-therapy
---

Testosterone levels fall gradually with age, and many symptoms attributed to low T, such as fatigue, low energy and reduced sex drive, are common and nonspecific.

The Endocrine Society 2018 testosterone guideline:

- Recommends against routine screening of the general male population for low testosterone.
- Suggests against routinely prescribing testosterone to all men 65 or older with low testosterone.
- For men 65 or older with symptoms or conditions suggesting testosterone deficiency and consistently low morning testosterone, suggests that clinicians offer therapy on an individualized basis after discussing potential benefits and risks.

The AUA testosterone deficiency guideline similarly requires both symptoms and two low early-morning results, and asks clinicians to tell men that evidence is inconclusive on whether testosterone improves energy, fatigue, cognition, diabetes measures, lipids or quality of life. It notes possible improvements in erectile function, sex drive, anemia, bone density, lean body mass and depressive symptoms. The AUA also recommends lifestyle counseling for all men with testosterone deficiency, and prefers commercially manufactured testosterone over compounded products when possible.

This passage is labelled Moderate because the age-65 statements are suggestions rather than strong recommendations.
