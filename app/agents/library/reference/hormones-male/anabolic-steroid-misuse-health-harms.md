Anabolic-androgenic steroids (AAS, "roids", "gear", testosterone and related drugs used at high levels for muscle or performance) are used mostly by non-athlete weightlifters, not elite athletes. The Endocrine Society 2014 scientific statement on performance-enhancing drugs estimated that 2.9 to 4.0 million Americans have used AAS at some point, and that about 30% of users develop AAS dependence.

Health effects linked to AAS use in the statement:

- Heart and blood vessels: cardiomyopathy (weakened heart muscle, with reduced pumping function in controlled imaging studies), lower HDL and higher LDL cholesterol, atherosclerosis, high blood pressure, thick blood (polycythemia), clotting and heart rhythm problems. Some heart changes may be only partly reversible.
- Hormones in men: shutdown of the body's own testosterone production, with low testosterone (hypogonadism) that can last weeks to months after stopping, plus infertility, testicular shrinkage and gynecomastia (breast tissue growth).
- Mental health: mood disorders including mania, hypomania and depression, aggression, and dependence.
- Women: virilizing effects such as voice deepening and body hair growth.

The statement notes that users often take very high amounts, combine several drugs, and may underestimate the risks. Lab clues it lists include high hemoglobin and hematocrit, suppressed LH, FSH and natural testosterone, low HDL cholesterol and low sperm count.

Anyone stopping AAS after long use benefits from medical supervision.
