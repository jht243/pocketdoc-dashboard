Before testosterone therapy (TRT, testosterone replacement) is started for low testosterone, guidelines check for conditions where it could cause harm.

The Endocrine Society 2018 testosterone guideline recommends against testosterone therapy in men (recommendation 2.2, strong recommendation, low-quality evidence) who have:

- Plans for fertility in the near term, because testosterone suppresses sperm production.
- Breast cancer or prostate cancer.
- A palpable prostate nodule or hardening on exam.
- A PSA above 4 ng/mL, or above 3 ng/mL with high prostate cancer risk, without further urological evaluation.
- An elevated hematocrit (thick blood).
- Untreated severe obstructive sleep apnea (OSA).
- Severe lower urinary tract symptoms, such as a very weak stream or frequent night urination from an enlarged prostate.
- Uncontrolled heart failure.
- A heart attack or stroke within the last 6 months.
- Thrombophilia, an inherited or acquired tendency to form blood clots.

Several of these are reasons to evaluate or treat first rather than permanent bars; for example, sleep apnea can be treated and a high PSA can be evaluated by a urologist. Details on PSA, hematocrit and fertility are in separate passages.

The guideline's recommendation is strong, though the evidence quality is graded low. Whether testosterone is appropriate is decided by the prescribing clinician after reviewing these conditions.
