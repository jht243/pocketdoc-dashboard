In January 2025 a Lancet Diabetes & Endocrinology Commission of 58 experts proposed a new way to diagnose obesity that does not rely on BMI alone.

- Excess adiposity (too much body fat) should be confirmed rather than assumed from BMI. The Commission accepts direct body fat measurement, or BMI plus at least one body measurement such as waist circumference, waist-to-hip ratio or waist-to-height ratio. At a very high BMI, above 40 kg/m², excess fat can be assumed.
- Preclinical obesity means excess adiposity while organs and daily function are still working normally. It generally carries a higher risk of later clinical obesity and of diseases such as type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular disease.
- Clinical obesity is a chronic illness in which excess fat is already impairing organs or daily activities. The Commission lists 18 organ- and function-based diagnostic criteria for adults and 13 for children and adolescents.

The Commission states that BMI is useful as a population-level screen but should not by itself diagnose obesity in an individual. In a similar direction, the American Diabetes Association's 2026 Standards of Care in Overweight and Obesity section on diagnosis recommends combining BMI with waist measurements and population-specific thresholds. This framework is newer than most guidelines, and not all societies have adopted it.
