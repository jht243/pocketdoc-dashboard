Fasting insulin (serum insulin, insulin level, hyperinsulinemia test) is not part of any ADA diagnostic criterion. The ADA Standards of Care in Diabetes 2026 diagnose prediabetes and diabetes only with hemoglobin A1c, fasting plasma glucose, the oral glucose tolerance test, or a random glucose with symptoms.

There is no guideline-endorsed reference range or optimal value for fasting insulin in people without diabetes. Insulin immunoassays are not standardized across manufacturers, so the same blood sample can give different numbers at different labs; an inter-laboratory study concluded that insulin assays need standardization before normal values for insulin-based indices can be defined. A fasting insulin result should therefore be read against the reference interval printed by the lab that ran it, and compared over time only when measured by the same lab.

Any specific fasting insulin target promoted for metabolic health or longevity is an Emerging, non-guideline idea. A high fasting insulin can accompany insulin resistance, but it does not by itself establish prediabetes or diabetes under ADA criteria.
