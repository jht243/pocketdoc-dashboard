People without diabetes who wear a continuous glucose monitor (CGM, glucose sensor, Dexcom, Libre, Stelo, Lingo) often worry about spikes. A multicenter study in the Journal of Clinical Endocrinology and Metabolism gave 153 healthy, nonobese people ages 7 to 80 a blinded CGM for up to 10 days. Findings:

- Average sensor glucose was 98 to 99 mg/dL in most age groups and about 104 mg/dL in people over 60.
- The median time spent between 70 and 140 mg/dL was 96%.
- Healthy people still spent a median of about 30 minutes a day above 140 mg/dL and about 15 minutes a day below 70 mg/dL.
- The average within-person coefficient of variation was 17%.

So brief readings above 140 mg/dL after meals, and short dips below 70 mg/dL, occur in healthy people and are not by themselves a sign of diabetes. A follow-up analysis of the same group found that the glucose management indicator (GMI), the A1c estimate shown in CGM apps, ran on average 0.59 percentage points higher than lab hemoglobin A1c in people without diabetes, so GMI should not be read as an A1c result. Time-in-range targets used in diabetes care were built for people with diabetes, and there are no guideline CGM targets for people without diabetes. Diabetes is diagnosed with lab A1c, fasting glucose or an oral glucose tolerance test under ADA criteria.
