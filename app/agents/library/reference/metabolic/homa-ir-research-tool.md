HOMA-IR (homeostatic model assessment of insulin resistance, insulin resistance score) is calculated from one fasting glucose and one fasting insulin value. It was developed to estimate insulin resistance and beta-cell function, and its developers' 2004 review in Diabetes Care, Use and Abuse of HOMA Modeling, describes appropriate uses as cohort and epidemiological studies and warns that results depend on robust input data and careful interpretation.

Key points for reading a HOMA-IR result:

- No major guideline, including the ADA Standards of Care, uses HOMA-IR to diagnose prediabetes, diabetes or insulin resistance.
- Because insulin assays are not standardized between labs, HOMA-IR values calculated from different labs are not directly comparable, and published cut-offs vary by assay and population.
- A single value carries day-to-day variability from both glucose and insulin.

Any HOMA-IR cut-off presented as normal or optimal should be treated as Emerging and non-guideline. Guideline-based assessment of blood sugar status relies on hemoglobin A1c, fasting plasma glucose and the oral glucose tolerance test.
