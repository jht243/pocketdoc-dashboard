The Diabetes Prevention Program (DPP) trial is the strongest US evidence that type 2 diabetes can be prevented or delayed in people with prediabetes (borderline blood sugar, high A1c, insulin resistance). The ADA Standards of Care in Diabetes 2026 summarize it this way:

- An intensive lifestyle program lowered the risk of developing type 2 diabetes by 58% over 3 years.
- The two goals were at least 7% weight loss and 150 minutes a week of moderate-intensity activity such as brisk walking. Weight loss was the most important factor: each kilogram lost lowered risk by 16%. Meeting the activity goal alone, without the weight goal, still lowered risk by 44%.
- The benefit faded but persisted over time: 34% lower risk at 10 years, 27% at 15 years and 24% at 21 years in the follow-up study (DPPOS). Similar programs in China and Finland showed lasting effects.

The ADA recommends referring adults with overweight or obesity at high risk of type 2 diabetes to a diabetes prevention program aiming for at least 5% to 7% weight loss with a healthy reduced-calorie eating pattern and at least 150 minutes a week of moderate activity (grade A). It also recommends an evidence-based eating pattern such as Mediterranean or lower-carbohydrate for people with prediabetes, and accepts certified app-based and telehealth programs.
