The ADA Standards of Care in Diabetes 2026 define prediabetes (borderline diabetes, insulin resistance range, high-normal blood sugar) by any one of three lab results:

- Hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c, A1C) of 5.7% to 6.4%.
- Fasting plasma glucose of 100 to 125 mg/dL, called impaired fasting glucose (IFG).
- 2-hour plasma glucose of 140 to 199 mg/dL during an oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT), called impaired glucose tolerance (IGT).

Prediabetes is not a disease category of its own in the ADA framework; it marks a higher risk of progressing to type 2 diabetes and of cardiovascular disease. The ADA screening table states that people with prediabetes should be retested yearly. Values below these ranges (A1c under 5.7%, fasting glucose under 100 mg/dL) are in the normal range by ADA criteria. Values at or above 6.5% A1c or 126 mg/dL fasting fall into the diabetes range and need confirmation as described in the ADA diagnostic criteria.
