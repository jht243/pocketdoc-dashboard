The ADA Standards of Care in Diabetes 2026 list four ways to meet the laboratory criterion for diabetes (type 2 diabetes, high blood sugar) in nonpregnant adults:

- Hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c, A1C) of 6.5% or higher, measured with an NGSP-certified lab method.
- Fasting plasma glucose (fasting blood sugar) of 126 mg/dL or higher. Fasting means no calories for at least 8 hours.
- 2-hour plasma glucose of 200 mg/dL or higher during a standard oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT, glucose challenge test).
- Random plasma glucose of 200 mg/dL or higher, but only together with classic high-blood-sugar symptoms (thirst, frequent urination, unexplained weight loss) or a hyperglycemic crisis.

Confirmation matters. Unless hyperglycemia is unequivocal, the ADA requires two abnormal results: either two different tests from the same blood sample, or the same test repeated on a second occasion. If two different tests disagree, the test that came back above the cut point is repeated. A single borderline A1c or one high fasting glucose on its own does not meet the ADA definition of diabetes.
