The ADA Standards of Care in Diabetes 2026 set out who should be screened for prediabetes and type 2 diabetes (blood sugar screening, A1c test, diabetes check):

- Adults of any age with overweight or obesity plus one or more additional risk factors should be considered for testing. Risk factors in the ADA list include a first-degree relative with diabetes, high blood pressure, physical inactivity and polycystic ovary syndrome, among others.
- For everyone else, screening should begin at age 35.
- Fasting plasma glucose, the 2-hour glucose on an oral glucose tolerance test, and hemoglobin A1c are each acceptable screening tests.

If results are normal, the ADA considers repeat screening at a minimum of 3-year intervals reasonable, sooner if symptoms appear or risk changes (for example, rising weight). People whose results fall in the prediabetes range should be tested yearly. People with a history of gestational diabetes should have lifelong screening every 1 to 3 years.
