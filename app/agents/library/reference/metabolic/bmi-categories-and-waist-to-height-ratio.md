BMI (body mass index, weight in kg divided by height in metres squared) is a quick screen for overweight and obesity, but it does not measure where fat sits. The UK NICE guideline NG246 on overweight and obesity uses these adult categories:

- Healthy weight: BMI 18.5 to 24.9 kg/m²; overweight: 25 to 29.9; obesity class 1: 30 to 34.9; class 2: 35 to 39.9; class 3: 40 or more.
- For people of South Asian, Chinese, other Asian, Middle Eastern, Black African or African-Caribbean background, whose heart and metabolic risk rises at lower BMI, NICE uses lower cut-offs: overweight from 23 and obesity from 27.5 kg/m².
- BMI should be read with caution in very muscular people and in adults 65 and older.

To capture belly fat (central adiposity, visceral fat), NICE recommends waist-to-height ratio for people with a BMI under 35: waist divided by height, both measured in inches or both in centimetres.

- 0.4 to 0.49: healthy, no increased risk.
- 0.5 to 0.59: increased central adiposity and increased health risk.
- 0.6 or more: high central adiposity and further increased risk.

The NICE message is to keep the waist to less than half of height. Central adiposity is linked to type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure and cardiovascular disease, and NICE notes that a person with a healthy BMI can still carry excess belly fat.
