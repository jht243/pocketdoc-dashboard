Hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c, A1C, three-month blood sugar average) reflects glucose attached to red blood cells, so anything that changes red cell lifespan or the hemoglobin molecule can make it misleading. The ADA Standards of Care in Diabetes 2026 flag these situations:

- Anemia and altered red cell turnover, including iron deficiency, hemolysis and treatment with erythropoietin. The ADA notes A1c may not be a suitable diagnostic test in people with anemia.
- Hemoglobin variants such as sickle cell trait, which interfere with some A1c assays; an assay without that interference should be used.
- Kidney failure and dialysis.
- G6PD deficiency; the ADA notes the G202A variant lowers A1c by roughly 0.7 to 0.8 percentage points.
- HIV, where A1c may underestimate glucose levels.
- Pregnancy, because red cell turnover changes.

In these settings the ADA recommends relying on plasma glucose criteria, meaning fasting glucose or the oral glucose tolerance test, rather than A1c alone. A large mismatch between A1c and measured glucose is itself a reason to look for one of these causes.
