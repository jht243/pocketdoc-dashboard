In March 2024 the FDA cleared the Dexcom Stelo as the first over-the-counter continuous glucose monitor (OTC CGM, glucose biosensor, wearable glucose sensor), available without a prescription. According to the FDA:

- It is intended for adults 18 and older who do not use insulin. That includes people with diabetes managed with oral medicines and people without diabetes who want to see how diet and exercise affect their blood sugar.
- It is not intended for people with problematic hypoglycemia (low blood sugar), because it is not designed to alert to dangerously low glucose.
- The app shows readings and trends, and each sensor is worn for up to 15 days.
- The FDA states users should not make medical decisions based on the device output without talking to a healthcare provider.

Benefit for people without diabetes is Emerging: no major guideline recommends routine CGM use for people with normal blood sugar, and there are no guideline glucose targets for this group. A CGM can show patterns, such as how particular meals, sleep or activity affect glucose, but it does not diagnose prediabetes or diabetes. Those are diagnosed with lab hemoglobin A1c, fasting plasma glucose or an oral glucose tolerance test under ADA criteria. Persistently high sensor readings are a reason to arrange lab testing with a clinician.
