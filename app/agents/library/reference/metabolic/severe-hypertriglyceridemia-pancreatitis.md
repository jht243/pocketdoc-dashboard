The 2021 ACC Expert Consensus Decision Pathway on hypertriglyceridemia (high triglycerides, TG, blood fats) uses these categories:

- Mild to moderate: fasting triglycerides of 150 mg/dL or higher, or nonfasting of 175 mg/dL or higher, and below 500 mg/dL. At these levels the main concern is cardiovascular risk.
- Severe: 500 mg/dL or higher, and especially 1,000 mg/dL or higher. Fasting triglycerides at or above 500 mg/dL identify people at risk of acute pancreatitis.
- Above 1,000 mg/dL, chylomicronemia is likely; above 1,500 mg/dL the chylomicronemia syndrome, with eruptive xanthomas and pancreatitis, is often present.

The pathway calls for fasting lipid measurements to identify pancreatitis risk and to monitor response, and for addressing secondary causes, which include diabetes with high blood sugar, chronic kidney disease, obesity and metabolic syndrome, pregnancy, alcohol, and medications such as estrogens, steroids, beta-blockers and some diuretics. For persistent severe hypertriglyceridemia, it recommends clinicians consider very low-fat diets and triglyceride-lowering therapy to reduce pancreatitis risk. A triglyceride result of 500 mg/dL or higher warrants prompt clinician review.
